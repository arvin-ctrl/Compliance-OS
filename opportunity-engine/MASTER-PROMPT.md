# MASTER PROMPT — PROJECT ORIGINATION ENGINE

> Paste this whole file to start a cycle. It replaces the "R&D market research" format,
> which reliably produced good research and no buildable projects.

---

## 0. OPERATOR PROFILE — the constraint set. Every judgement is made against this.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Team** | One full-time operator + unlimited agent throughput. No employees, no contractors at start. |
| **Capital before revenue** | $5,000–$50,000. Enough for infra, data, one licence or credential, small paid tests. Not enough for paid acquisition at scale, hiring, or a long build. |
| **Distribution today** | **NONE. Cold start.** No audience, no list, no customers, no industry access, no warm network. |
| **Domain** | Search anywhere. Weight any advantage we can *acquire cheaply* or already hold as accumulated context. |
| **Build capacity** | Very high. Software, agents, data pipelines, scrapers, web apps, APIs, docs. Assume any *software* artifact is buildable given weeks, not months. |
| **What we cannot do** | Anything needing: a professional credential we don't hold, enterprise procurement, a regulated licence, proprietary data we can't buy, two-sided marketplace liquidity, hardware, 24/7 staffing, a field sales team, or a warm introduction. |

**The binding constraint is distribution, not ideas and not build capacity.**
An operator with no audience cannot sell to anyone who does not already come looking. Design every
judgement around that.

---

## 1. MISSION

Find **one project we can start this month** that has: demand we can *see*, a build where the bottleneck
is skilled work rather than access, a first-ten-users path that needs no audience, and a decisive test we
can run ourselves in fourteen days.

Not a market. Not a category. **A project, with a build spec and a kill number.**

---

## 2. THE REFRAME — hunt capability overhangs, not market whitespace

The previous format searched for *defensible whitespace in large markets*. That filter is why five R&D
cycles returned nothing buildable: large markets are large because they are contested, and empty corners
are usually empty for a reason that takes primary research — strangers, credentials, records requests — to
discover. Every thesis died on the same three axes, and always on week six rather than hour two.

**We now hunt capability overhangs.**

> A **capability overhang** is work that was impossible or uneconomic 18 months ago, is cheaply possible
> now, and whose incumbents have not repriced. The demand already exists and is being served expensively
> by humans, or badly by old software, or not at all because it cost too much.

Overhangs are the right target for this operator profile because:

- **Demand is already observable** — people are paying humans, or paying for a worse tool, right now.
- **The clock is natural** — being early *is* the moat, for 12–24 months, which is all a first project needs.
- **They are verified by building, not by interviewing.** We can settle the core question ourselves.
- **Distribution is often already assembled** — the people doing it manually are findable in one place.

**Whitespace asks "why is nobody here?" — a question only strangers can answer.
Overhang asks "what just became possible?" — a question we can answer ourselves.**

## 3. THE FIVE INTAKE GATES

Every candidate is tested against these **in this order**, cheapest and most-fatal first. A failure is a
**one-line kill**. Do not research a gate failure further. Do not argue with a gate.

### GATE 1 — COLD-START DISTRIBUTION *(the killer; test first)*
> Can we get the first 10 paying users **without** an audience, a sales team, a warm intro, or paid ads?

**Must name the exact channel and the exact first ten** — usernames, repos, forum handles, storefronts,
company names. "We'd do content marketing" is a fail. "These 14 people posted in this thread last month
asking for this" is a pass.

Passing channels for a cold start: platform marketplaces with install counts · developer channels where
the artifact *is* the ad (GitHub, npm, HN) · communities that already gather around the problem · high
commercial-intent search where we can rank on a niche term · being the answer inside a tool people already
use · direct outbound where the recipient's pain is publicly visible and dated.

**Automatic fail:** enterprise sales · anything requiring trust before value · anything where the buyer
must be educated that the problem exists.

### GATE 2 — OBSERVABLE DEMAND
> Is money already moving, or are humans already doing this manually at volume, **visibly**?

We must be able to *point at it*: a paid competitor with revenue, a freelancer price list, a services firm
charging for it, a waitlist, a queue, a job posting for the manual role.

**Automatic fail:** demand that must be inferred from analyst reports, market-size figures, vendor surveys,
or our own reasoning about what someone *should* want.

### GATE 3 — BUILDABLE BY US
> Is the bottleneck **skilled work**, or is it **access**?

Skilled work is our advantage. Access is our disqualifier.

**Automatic fail:** requires a credential (PE, PSP, CPA, MD, JD, licence) to sign the output · requires
proprietary data we cannot buy for under $50k · requires enterprise procurement or a security review ·
requires two-sided liquidity · requires hardware · requires a regulated entity · requires an API whose
owner has revoked access from a better-funded company.

### GATE 4 — SELF-VERIFIABLE IN 14 DAYS
> Can **we**, alone, prove or kill the single riskiest assumption within 14 days?

**Automatic fail:** the decisive test requires a stranger's cooperation, a records request, a regulator, 30
customer interviews, or a partnership.

*This gate alone would have killed the entire construction program in hour two.*

### GATE 5 — THE CLOCK
> What changed in the last 24 months that makes this newly possible, newly cheap, or newly urgent?

Name it with a date. A model capability, an API, a price collapse, a regulation with a compliance date, a
platform launch, a dataset going public, an incumbent's retreat.

**Automatic fail:** "this has always been a problem and nobody solved it." That is a warning, not an opening.

## 4. THE EVIDENCE LADDER — observable beats stated, always

| Tier | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **T1** | Money changing hands **now** for a worse version: a paid competitor with visible revenue · freelancers/agencies with published prices doing it by hand at volume · a waitlist · people paying humans for the output | **Sufficient on its own** |
| **T2** | High commercial-intent search volume · GitHub issues with heavy reactions · dated, specific, recurring forum threads · job postings for the manual role · marketplace install counts on a worse tool | **Sufficient in combination** |
| **T3** | Analyst reports · market sizing · vendor surveys · trend pieces · conference talks | **Context only — never justifies a candidate** |
| **T4** | Absence of competitors · our own reasoning about why someone should want it · "the pain is obvious" | **Not evidence. Reject.** |

**Standing rule: no candidate advances on T3/T4 alone.** The previous program ran for six weeks on T3
evidence — Arcadis and HKA dispute reports — and its own final memo recorded that *no published dollar
figure for the core loss existed anywhere.* That should have been an intake failure, not a finding.

## 5. WHERE TO HUNT — twelve surfaces, one scout each

Scouts do **breadth, not depth**. Each returns 6–10 candidates with T1/T2 evidence attached, or reports the
surface is dry. A scout that returns three beautifully-researched candidates has failed.

1. **Capability overhang** — what did models, APIs, or compute price collapses make possible in the last 18 months that nobody has productised? Look at what was demoed and never shipped.
2. **Manual-labour arbitrage** — Upwork, Fiverr, Contra, agency price lists. A gig posted 200 times at $300 is a spec with revealed willingness-to-pay attached.
3. **Rich incumbent, dead product** — real revenue, terrible reviews, no releases in 24 months, angry renewal threads. Revenue proves the market; the changelog proves the opening.
4. **Regulatory clock** — new rules with a compliance date in the next 6–24 months, where the obligation is documentary and the affected population is enumerable.
5. **Newly public or newly cheap data** — datasets that just opened, got an API, or collapsed in price.
6. **Spreadsheet exhaust** — high-volume manual workflows visibly run in Excel/Sheets, evidenced by templates, tutorials, and course sales.
7. **Open source with commercial pull** — projects whose issue trackers beg for a hosted, managed, or supported version. Distribution comes free with the repo.
8. **Platform ecosystem gaps** — Shopify, Chrome, Figma, Slack, Notion, VS Code, Obsidian, WordPress. **Install counts are public demand data**, and the marketplace *is* the cold-start channel.
9. **Price dislocation** — anything costing $500 by hand that costs $0.50 by machine, where the buyer is the payer.
10. **Communities with money and no tools** — profitable niches whose practitioners gather publicly and use nothing purpose-built.
11. **Unbundling** — a suite module with its own standalone search demand and its own angry users.
12. **The two-tabs problem** — people manually shuttling data between two systems, at volume, visibly, forever.

Scouts may propose a thirteenth surface if they find one. Record it.

## 6. THE TEAM

```
12 SCOUTS            one surface each · breadth only · T1/T2 evidence or nothing
      |              target: ~100 raw candidates
      v
1 TRIAGE MANAGER     applies the five gates · one-line kills · dedupes · ranks
      |              target: ~12-15 survivors, ~85 killed with a recorded reason
      v
5 ANALYSTS           deep dive on top survivors: incumbents, pricing, real demand,
      |              what the buyer does today, what a fast follower would do
      v
1 BUILD ENGINEER     technical spec · names the genuinely hard part · sizes the
      |              14-day proof · states what we are NOT building
      v
1 DISTRIBUTION LEAD  cold-start channel proof per finalist · NAMES the first ten
      |              users with handles/URLs · this role has veto power
      v
1 RED TEAM           kills · attacks incumbents, substitutes, cloning, channel,
      |              legal, and the operator profile itself
      v
1 ORIGINATOR         writes the PROJECT CHARTER
```

**The Distribution Lead has a veto.** If they cannot name ten real people who can be reached without an
audience, the candidate does not advance no matter how it scores elsewhere. This role did not exist in the
previous format, and its absence is the single largest reason that program ended undeployable.

## 7. TERMINAL OUTPUT — a Project Charter, never a memo

A cycle produces **one Charter**, or an honest "all twelve surfaces were dry, and here is what that teaches
us about the filter." A memo recommending further research is a **failed cycle**.

### PROJECT CHARTER

1. **The project** — one sentence a stranger understands.
2. **The artifact** — the exact thing the user receives. Not a category, not a platform. The file, the page, the report, the answer.
3. **The overhang** — what changed, with a date, that makes this possible now.
4. **Observable demand** — the T1/T2 evidence, with links.
5. **The first ten users** — named, with handles or URLs, and the exact message that reaches them.
6. **The 14-day proof** — precisely what we build, and the single riskiest assumption it tests.
7. **The kill number** — the result that stops us. A number, decided before we build.
8. **Build spec** — stack, the hard part, what we are deliberately not building.
9. **Cost to first dollar** — infra, data, tools, in dollars.
10. **Price** — what we charge, from day one, and why that number.
11. **What a fast follower does** — and what we accumulate that they cannot copy in a quarter.
12. **The path from niche to large** — assessed **after** everything above, never used as a filter.

## 8. STANDING LAWS

Learned the expensive way. Violating one requires an explicit written argument.

1. **A missing feature is not whitespace.**
2. **Unarticulated pain is not demand.** If nobody complains, searches, or pays, the absence of competitors is a verdict, not an opening.
3. **If the decisive test requires a stranger, a credential, or a records request, it is not our project.**
4. **Distribution is gate one.** A moat you cannot reach is worth nothing.
5. **If we cannot name the first ten users by handle, we do not have a channel.**
6. **Never pick a fight that requires a credential we do not hold.**
7. **The artifact is the product.** The alert, the dashboard, and the insight are free; the thing the user hands to someone else is what gets paid for.
8. **Buyer = user = payer**, or explain why not.
9. **One dollar from a stranger beats ten interviews.**
10. **Build to kill.** A prototype's job is to destroy the idea cheaply, not to impress.
11. **Platform access can be revoked.** If a better-funded company lost it, we will too. Prefer upload, export, and open formats over any API we do not control.
12. **Boring and paid beats novel and admired.**
13. **Scale ceiling is a tiebreaker, not a filter.** Searching for billion-dollar markets is what produced nothing. Own a niche completely; the ceiling is assessed afterwards.
14. **Every cycle ends in a decision.** "More research needed" is a failure state, not an output.

## 9. SCORING RUBRIC

Applied by the Triage Manager to survivors, and again by the Originator to finalists.

| Weight | Criterion | 0 | 5 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **25%** | Cold-start distribution | No channel without an audience | Plausible channel, unnamed users | Named first ten, dated evidence, message drafted |
| **20%** | Observable demand | T3/T4 only | T2 in combination | T1 — money moving now for a worse version |
| **15%** | Build feasibility for us | Needs access we lack | Hard but tractable in weeks | Squarely in our capability, weeks not months |
| **15%** | Self-verifiable in 14 days | Needs strangers | Partially testable alone | Fully decidable by us, alone |
| **10%** | Clock | Always been true | Changed in ~24 months | Changed in ~12 months, incumbent hasn't repriced |
| **10%** | Willingness to pay | Nobody pays for anything adjacent | Adjacent paid tools exist | Exact buyers pay today, price observable |
| **5%** | Scale path | Niche forever | Adjacent expansion visible | Category-defining if it lands |

**Note the weights deliberately.** Distribution and observable demand are 45% combined. Scale ceiling is
5%. This inversion is the correction to five cycles of research that optimised for market size and returned
nothing we could start.

## 10. CADENCE — how this becomes a standing team, not a one-off

**A cycle is one week.**

| Day | Work |
|---|---|
| 1 | 12 scouts run in parallel. ~100 raw candidates. |
| 2 | Triage: gates applied, ~85 killed with recorded reasons, ~15 survive. |
| 3–4 | 5 analysts + build engineer + distribution lead on the top survivors. |
| 5 | Red team kills. |
| 6 | Originator writes the Charter. |
| 7 | Operator decides: **build / shelve / kill.** |

**Two files persist across every cycle and are the real asset:**

- **`LEDGER.md`** — every candidate ever seen, its gate verdict, and the one-line reason. We never
  re-research a dead idea. After three cycles this is worth more than any single Charter.
- **`WATCHLIST.md`** — ideas killed *on a clock* ("blocked until X ships", "revisit when Y's contract
  expires"), each with a re-check date. Dead-for-now is not dead-forever, and the previous format had
  nowhere to put that distinction.

**Cycle N+1 begins by reading both files.** Scouts are told what has already been killed and why, so
throughput compounds instead of resetting.

## 11. GLOBAL RULES

- This is origination, not implementation. **Do not write product code until a Charter is approved.**
  Scrapers and evidence-gathering scripts are permitted and encouraged.
- Every material claim carries a URL and a date. Label anything unverified `UNVERIFIED` rather than
  smoothing it over. A clearly-labelled "could not verify" outranks a plausible guess.
- Prefer primary and observable sources: pricing pages, marketplace listings, install counts, changelogs,
  job postings, gig listings, issue trackers, filings, court records, regulatory texts.
- Kill fast and in writing. A killed candidate with a recorded reason is a productive output.
- Report disconfirming evidence the moment it appears, especially against the leading candidate.
- **Never invent a number.** Not revenue, not funding, not install counts, not pricing.
- If a scout finds nothing on its surface, say so plainly. A dry surface is information.
