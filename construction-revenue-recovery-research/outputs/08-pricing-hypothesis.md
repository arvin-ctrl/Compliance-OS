# 8. Pricing Hypothesis

## Direct answer to the brief's question

> *Is the ROI strong enough for $500–$5,000+/month or performance pricing?*

**Partially, and not from the budget line you would expect.**

| Price point | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **$500/mo** | **YES everywhere** | 1–3% of the technology purse at any size |
| **$1,500/mo** | **YES at $100M+ revenue** | Defensible against a $85–120K Change Order Engineer |
| **$3,000/mo** | **NO at $50M · YES at $100M+** with a CFO case | Needs an explicit displacement argument |
| **$5,000/mo** | **NO below ~$150M revenue** | Would be **30% of a $50M sub's entire technology purse** |
| **Performance pricing** | **LEGAL, in a narrow envelope** | See below |

## The constraint that sets the ceiling

CFMA (n=1,558): **Technology Costs = 0.26% of revenue** — $368K on $139.4M average revenue, inside SG&A of
11.33%. Commercial GCs run **4.4%** net income before taxes; heavy civil **8.3%**; specialty trade **7.7%**.

A $200M GC therefore has roughly **$500K of total technology budget**. And per-seat pricing is independently
capped at **$50–150/user/month** by commoditisation: DocuSign IAM publishes $45/$50/$80 per user/month with
AI extraction at every tier, and M365 Copilot answers contract questions at $18–30.

**Conclusion: never price per seat, and never sell from the IT line.**

## The unlock — two budget lines that escape the 0.26% cage

**1. Job cost / project overhead.** On federal and federally-aided work, ***Tip Top Construction v. Donahoe*,
695 F.3d 1276 (Fed. Cir. 2012)** makes **REA-preparation costs allowable contract administration** under
FAR 31.205-33. Only *post*-CDA-claim costs fall under FAR 31.205-47(f)(1). **A pre-claim product is therefore
a billable project cost, not an IT purchase.**
*(Flagged UNVERIFIED for Caltrans state-funded work — this is decision gate G4.)*

**2. Professional Fees — 0.50% of revenue, a purse 1.9x larger than Technology**, where consultants and
counsel are already being paid.

**What it displaces: headcount, not software.** A Change Order Engineer costs $85–120K — **7–20x** the
subscription. That is the argument that closes.

## The proposed architecture

### SKU 1 — PCR Pack *(the funded wedge)*
**$3,500 per pack**, job-costed to the project.
Anchor: a consultant produces the same artefact inside a 600–1,650 hour engagement at $240k–660k. At $3,500
you are ~1–2% of that, below any procurement threshold, and chargeable to the job.

### SKU 2 — Readiness base
**$600/project/month, unlimited users**, 6-month minimum.
Keeps snapshots current so the 20-day clock is survivable. 0.036% of a $20M job. CEMAR's **£435/licence/month**
(~$550) across £75bn of works proves the band exists in a mandated-notice regime.

### SKU 3 — Entitlement layer for consultancies *(year 1–2)*
**$2,500–4,000 per matter**, sold as the layer *on top of* Relativity aiR / Everlaw — which already do
chronology free — supplying what they lack: notice register, EOT/compensable split, Division 00 awareness.
Basis: US analyst rates of $175–275/hr against a 13–22% hours reduction.

### SKU 4 — Recovery fee *(deferred to ~customer #10)*
**8% of recovered amounts, capped at $50K per event**, credited against SKU 2, earned **only on an executed
change order — never on suit proceeds.**

## Performance pricing: the legal envelope

**Permitted.** *Factortame No.8* held Grant Thornton's **quantum-preparation** contingency was **not**
champertous. **The bar attaches to the witness role, not to the fee.** FTI discloses success fees.

**Barred.** Testifying-expert contingency — ABA Model Rule 3.4(b) cmt.; Alabama Formal Op. 1997-02;
Philadelphia 94-27; California 1997-149. The industry already separates claim *preparation* from *testimony*,
**and that separation is the seam performance pricing legitimately occupies.**

**Live hazards.** Kentucky **KRS 372.060** voids contracts for services *"in the prosecution or defense…
whereby the thing sued for… is to be taken, paid or received for such services."* New York Judiciary Law §489
targets *assignment*, not fees (*Justinian Capital*, 28 N.Y.3d 160). **UPL is the real risk**
(*NC State Bar v. Lienguard* — preparing lien claims held to be UPL; **citation requires verification**).
Georgia SB 69 registration applies from 1 Jan 2026. Anti-Assignment Act §3727 constrains federal work.
Touching insurance recoveries triggers public-adjuster felony exposure.

**Design rules:** never testify; never take a percentage of litigation proceeds; earn only on an executed
change order; cap the fee; disclose the arrangement in writing; avoid Kentucky.

## The value-capture ceiling

Take scales with downside borne — and a software vendor bears none.

| Comparable | Take | Downside borne |
|---|---|---|
| Litigation funders | 20–40% | Full capital risk |
| Recovery auditors | 20–30% | Full delivery cost |
| Public adjusters | 8–20% capped | Regulated, licensed |
| **Construction consultants** | **1.2–3.6% of disputed sum** | Professional liability |
| **A software vendor** | **1.5–8%** | **None** |

**On a $412K recovery, the capturable range is $5,000–$30,000** — confirmed from two independent directions.
**20–40% is not achievable and should not appear in any model.**

## What is still unproven

Every ROI model rests on a modelled leak of **0.1–0.6% of revenue that no published source establishes.**
The industry publishes **incidence** (77% of subs have written off change-order work; 97% start before
approval) and almost never **value**. The single value-side figure found in the entire program:

> **98% of GCs have experienced fee erosion from change-order negotiations; nearly half say erosion exceeded
> 10% of their fee on at least some projects.** *(Dodge/Clearstory 2026)*

**The number to verify before committing**, asked of 30 controllers:

> *"In your last fiscal year, what dollar value of work did you perform and either never bill, or bill and
> not collect, because the change order was never executed or was negotiated down?"*
