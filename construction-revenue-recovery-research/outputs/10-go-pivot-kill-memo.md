## The crux: the discovery tension, adjudicated

The red team promoted Day 20 while simultaneously finding that discovery doctrine protects **per-matter,
counsel-engaged** work (*William A. Gross* / Hill Int'l — held protected) and **not** subscription or
contractually-required output (*Alta Refrigeration*, *G.M. Harston* — held not protected). Day 20 is a
per-project subscription producing a contractually-required artefact — apparently the least protected
structure available.

**The premise is right; the inference is wrong.** Discoverability only harms a document you meant to keep.
Sort Day 20's outputs:

- The **itemised cost estimate** (§5-1.43C.3), the **TIA** (§5-1.43C.4) and the **evidence index** are handed
  to Caltrans *by contract*. Nobody asserts work product over a filed pleading, and §5-1.43D **requires**
  consistency across Initial, Supplemental and Final — the regime is *built* to force disclosure. *Alta* and
  *Harston* are not engaged.
- **Only the missing-evidence schedule is exposed** — and it is a working aid, not the deliverable. Making it
  ephemeral costs nothing.

Applying the same test to Matter Zero: *William A. Gross* held protection on **five conjunctive facts**
(retained by owner's counsel, firewalled team, no contractor contact, short-term engagement, litigation
imminent) — a shape the *vendor does not control*, with the burden on the party claiming protection. And
**FRCP 26(a)(2)(B)(ii) pierces it regardless**: anything received, reviewed, read or authored by the expert is
disclosed whether privileged or not.

**Ruling: adverse to both, a discriminator between neither. The promotion of Day 20 survives its own finding.**

**The design consequence neither manager reached:** doctrine punishes *routine subscription* output, not
*event-triggered* output. **So sell Day 20 per event.** That fixes the discovery structure, makes every
purchase self-justifying regardless of event frequency, and ensures the base tier generates no catalogue of
the buyer's own weaknesses.

## What would have to be true for KILL to be right

**Gather's null result would have to generalise:** that assembled evidence monetises as *savings for payers*
and *hours for professionals*, but never as *recovery for claimants*. The evidence for this is real and
uncomfortable — eleven case studies with **zero recovered compensation events in pounds**, a headline
retreating to "Detect 40% More", and staff shrinking from 14 to 10, over eight years, under ideal conditions,
in the one contract regime that mandates the workflow.

**The final-decision manager puts this at ~50%.**

That is why the verdict is PIVOT and not GO, and why it is PIVOT and not KILL: the decisive test costs
**$0–50 and three weeks**, the full pack **$2,500 and ten weeks**, and the founder ends up owning a
publishable, industry-first dataset either way.

## Answers to the ten research questions

**1. How do contractors manage this today?** Excel COR logs, Outlook folders, SharePoint, and a person —
Change Order Engineer ($85–120K), Contract Administrator, or Claims Manager ($140–182K), with 1,000+ open US
CA roles. Notice is tracked by memory or a calendar reminder, and **deliberately suppressed to protect owner
relationships**. Nothing checks evidence completeness.

**2. What's solved by the incumbents?** Everything up to the number. Record capture (Procore, ACC, Aconex,
InEight), change-order workflow (all five, plus Clearstory at $2.1B/month), correspondence with response
deadlines (Aconex scores 3), clause extraction (Document Crunch/Trimble, Procore since May 2026), event
detection (Procore Change Analysis, GA 23 Jul 2026), schedule arithmetic ($2,750–$25,000). **Unsolved:
evidence sufficiency, causation, quantum, claim package — nobody exceeds 1.**

**3. How strong are AI-native construction contract products?** Strong at extraction, absent at valuation.
Document Crunch is real and now Trimble-owned; Trunk Tools is real and gives extraction away free; Gather is
real, closest to the thesis, and does not do quantum. **Magra, Lexilio, ClaimMaster, Aven-AI are demoware** —
Magra's headline moved $240K→$17,824 with all integrations "Upcoming" and zero named customers.

**4. Are claims consultants effectively the incumbent?** Yes — they score 3 across the whole pipeline. But
they cannot self-disrupt (an hours business at 57–73% utilisation; **Diales converts £43.0m revenue into
£1.4m operating profit**), and HKA's dataset only counts a project once **30+ hours** of claim work exists,
so they structurally cannot see the pre-dispute phase.

**5. Can a product sit above Procore?** **No.** Procore revoked Trunk Tools' API access (Sept 2025); the
Developer Policy forbids parsing and database-building; the marketplace does not distribute commercial-risk
products (Document Crunch: 187 installs / 17,850 customers). **Hypothesis E is dead.**

**6. Can uploaded documents prove value before integrations?** **Yes, and upload is strictly better.** XER
deliverables are already contractually mandated, Oracle publishes the field maps, prior art shipped with zero
Oracle relationship, and forensic analysis runs on dated snapshots anyway — **so upload carries no evidential
penalty** while avoiding revocable access entirely.

**7. Which missed-revenue problem happens most frequently?** Unapproved/unbilled change orders — 97% start
before approval, 77% have written it off, 91% of GCs short-pay. **But the dominant *cause* of short-payment is
disputed pricing (66%), above insufficient backup (53%)** — which is the finding most threatening to an
evidence-centric thesis. Missed contractual notice is **not measured anywhere** and has a real fuse only in
state DOT work.

**8. Is ROI strong enough for $500–$5,000/mo or performance pricing?** $500 yes everywhere; $1,500 yes at
$100M+; **$5,000 fails below ~$150M**. Not from IT (0.26% of revenue) — from **job cost** (*Tip Top*, FAR
31.205-33) or **Professional Fees** (0.50%, a 1.9x larger purse). Performance pricing is **legal for claim
preparation, barred for testifying experts**, capped at **1.5–8%** of recovery.

**9. Who buys?** Claims consultancy (4.75) → heavy-civil/DOT contractor (4.30) → sub CFO (4.25). The contract
administrator is the **champion, never the buyer**. General counsel is a **gatekeeper with a veto**. The
sub owner has the most pain and is unreachable — **eSUB is the warning**.

**10. Could incumbents add the wedge easily?** Detection: already done. Notice triggering: 6–12 months.
**Evidence sufficiency and quantum: structurally protected**, because entitlement requires *originating* a
judgement, and Datagrid states the refusal in writing — *"entitlement and approval stay with the responsible
project professionals."* The cheapest leading indicator: **not one of the five has a field for clause
reference, notice date, notice deadline or entitlement basis on its change object.**

the specification and started the clock.**
