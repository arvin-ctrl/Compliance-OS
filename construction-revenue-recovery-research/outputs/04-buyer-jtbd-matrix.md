## The adjudication: contractor-side is the market, owner-side is distribution

Evidence pointed both ways and this was the program's hardest call.

**For owner-side:** Gather's only quantified outcomes are defensive — Network Rail's £300,000+ is money
*withheld from a contractor*, Costain's headline is *"15% of claims rejected on the spot."* Owner budgets are
better evidenced than contractor budgets anywhere in the program: **Caltrans District 3 alone is procuring
$7.0M over 3 years for claims-and-scheduling support** (03CONTCLM27). And owners *mandate* software —
*"The Contractor shall use the Owner's Project Management software, e-Builder…"* is boilerplate.

**It still resolves contractor-side, on four grounds:**

1. **The Gather result is about neutrality, not about which side has money.** They chose *"We don't take
   sides"* first, and never ran the contractor-side experiment. Their outcome is evidence about their
   positioning, not about the market.
2. **Neutrality caps price.** Gather charges £500/month flat while publishing 39x ROI. Only a partisan
   product can price on recovery.
3. **Owner-side assurance is the most copyable thing available** — it is precisely what incumbents *can*
   ship without breaking neutrality.
4. **All the pain data is contractor-side**: 77% write-off incidence, 33.4% of budgets claimed, and the
   margin structure (4.4% / 7.7% / 8.3%).

**One engine serves both.** An evidence-completeness score is a *claim-strength* score to the claimant and a
*rejection basis* to the payer. But **the order is one-way: take owner money first and you can never sell
contractors.** The reverse works — Verisk Xactimate sells the same price book to carriers and to the
contractors fighting them.

## The gatekeeper nobody planned for

**GC general counsel / risk (#11) can veto any of this**, on discovery grounds — a "missing-evidence
schedule" is a document cataloguing the weaknesses of your own client's claim. The red team found this lands
*opposite* to intuition: per-matter, counsel-engaged work has been **held protected** (*William A. Gross*,
Hill Int'l), while subscription or contractually-required output has been **held not protected**
(*Alta Refrigeration*, *G.M. Harston*).

**Design consequence: sell per event, not per subscription, and keep the gap analysis ephemeral.**
