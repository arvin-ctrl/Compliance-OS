### 1.3 `SOURCED` — US work-product law is adverse to the persisted missing-evidence schedule
Construction-specific authority, all collected in Alston & Bird, *When Is Work Product Not?*
(https://www.alston.com/-/media/files/insights/publications/2012/12/construction-cite-by-alston--bird--trends-and-deve/files/view-construction-cite-as-pdf/fileattachment/decemberconstructioncite.pdf):

- **Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(3)(A)** protects only documents *"prepared in anticipation of litigation."*
  **The party claiming protection bears the burden.**
- **Dual-purpose test:** protection fails *"if it would have been prepared in substantially similar form or
  content irrespective of the expected or anticipated litigation"* (*Adlman*, 134 F.3d 1194 (2d Cir. 1998);
  *Allied Irish Banks v. Bank of Am.*, 240 F.R.D. 96, 106 (S.D.N.Y. 2007)).
- ***Alta Refrigeration, Inc. v. AmeriCold Logistics, LLC***, 301 Ga. App. 738, 688 S.E.2d 658 (2009) — a report
  prepared **in accordance with the company's own investigation policy** was **not** work product, precisely
  *because* the company had a business policy to create such reports.
- ***G.M. Harston Constr. Co. v. City of Chicago***, 2001 WL 817855 (N.D. Ill. 2001) — Deloitte's evaluation of
  termination claims was **not** protected, because the city was **contractually obligated** to evaluate the
  claims, so the documents *"would have been created in essentially similar form irrespective of the threat of
  litigation."*
- ***Safeco Ins. Co. of Am. v. M.E.S., Inc.***, 2011 WL 6102014 (E.D.N.Y. 2011) — consultant reports evaluating
  contractor performance, prepared before default: **not** protected.
- ***William A. Gross Constr. Assocs. v. Am. Mfrs. Mut. Ins. Co.***, 262 F.R.D. 354 (S.D.N.Y. 2009) — Hill
  International's claim-evaluation reports **were** protected, but only on a very specific fact pattern:
  retained **by the owner's counsel**, a **separate team** firewalled from Hill's CM work, **no contact with the
  contractors**, a **short-term** assignment, litigation **imminent** (commenced two months later), and the
  report **discussed litigation positions**.

**Second exposure, independent of work product.** Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(a)(2)(B)(ii) requires disclosure of the
*"facts or data considered by the witness."* Courts read **"considered"** expansively: *"anything received,
reviewed, read, or authored by the expert, before or in connection with the forming of his opinion."*
https://www.americanbar.org/groups/litigation/resources/newsletters/pretrial-practice-discovery/expert-disclosures-must-include-facts-or-data-considered-even-if-not-relied/
**If the testifying delay or quantum expert ever opens the missing-evidence schedule, it goes to the other side.**

### 1.4 `SOURCED` — The MCAA method is the disfavoured method, and the buyer's own consultants publish the attack
- The MCAA loss-of-productivity percentages **are not empirical**: they are *"based on contractor opinions not
  empirical studies"*, and boards and courts are correspondingly conservative.
- **Measured mile is preferred**; the MCAA model is used *(1)* when measured mile and better methods are not
  applicable, or *(2)* to **corroborate** an estimate produced by another method.
- **Ankura**, one of the four largest construction-claims consultancies, publishes *"MCAA Labor Productivity
  Factors: Is it a Modified Total Cost Approach in Disguise?"* (19 Feb 2021), stating that *"research suggests
  that both methods have relatively low litigation success rates when compared to 'superior' approaches."*
  https://ankura.com/insights/mcaa-labor-productivity-factors-is-it-a-modified-total-cost-approach-in-disguise
- Ibbs & Sun, *A Long Time Coming — A Comprehensive Study and Critique of the MCAA Labor Productivity Factors*
  (https://constructionclaims.com/Content_Free/MCAA-IbbsSunReport.aspx) and the ASCE JLADR paper
  (https://ascelibrary.org/doi/10.1061/(ASCE)LA.1943-4170.0000196) are the standard references — and they are the
  rebuttal.
- The factors survive challenge only where *"subjectivity is overcome by the MCAA's admonition to apply them
  based on the expert's reasoned judgment in light of the facts of the particular project,"* and they require
  the analyst to **exclude contractor-caused inefficiency**.

## WEDGE 1 — MATTER ZERO · **WOUNDED**

### The strongest kill argument
**Three of the four load-bearing claims are false, and the fourth is a model the corpus itself refuses to stand
behind.**

**(a) "Nobody builds a chronology" is false, and the thing that does build it is free.**
SYNTHESIS §W1.5/§W1.6 rests the entire wedge on the assertion that Nuix, Relativity, Everlaw, P6, Acumen and
Excel *"none of those builds a chronology,"* that eDiscovery *"culls a corpus; it does not construct a dated
causal narrative,"* and that closing this would take a vendor **24–36 months and is "unlikely to be attempted."**
**Relativity aiR for Case Strategy went GA on 12 January 2026** and does fact extraction with citations,
chronology construction, **evidence-gap analysis** and helpful/harmful classification — and RelativityOne
**"includes … aiR for Case Strategy – at no additional cost"** (§1.1). Four of the five documents in the Matter
Pack — chronology, per-event sufficiency read, missing-evidence identification, cited exhibit set — now have a
free, GA, vendor-supported analogue sitting inside the platform the instructing law firm already pays for.

**(b) The price is anchored on a rate the corpus's own raw report contradicts.**
SYNTHESIS prices the displaced block at **$396–$442/hr**, taken from **Exponent** (a general science and
engineering firm) and **FTI's FLC segment** (all forensic and litigation consulting). But raw/11 line 322 derives
the realised rate at **Diales — the only listed pure-play construction claims consultancy — at ~£165–£200/hour**
(£43.0m ÷ 180 fee earners × ~1,700–1,900 hrs × 71.6% utilisation). That is **~$210–$260/hr blended**, and
document review and chronology is *junior analyst* work, i.e. below blended.

`DERIVED` re-pricing on the corpus's own inputs:

| | Synthesis | Corrected |
|---|---|---|
| Hours in the block | 200–600 | 200–600 (unchanged, still a model) |
| Realised rate applied | $400/hr (Exponent/FTI) | **$120–$180/hr** (analyst-level, below Diales' £165–200 blended) |
| Value of the block | **$80,000–$240,000** | **$24,000–$108,000** |

**The "you are 3–9% of the thing you replace" argument — the entire justification for $7,500 and for the
one-signature, no-business-case sale — is an artefact of choosing the highest rate in the corpus over the only
directly relevant one.**

**(c) "Bid the sub-$5m matters you currently turn away" is arithmetically false on the corpus's own table.**
raw/11 §12 Q2 gives the $5–25m matter as **600–1,650 total hours**, of which document review and chronology is
**200–600**. Removing 40–60% of that block removes **80–360 hours — 13–22% of the engagement.** The reason a
$400/hr (or £180/hr) pyramid at 57–73% utilisation with a >30-hour floor cannot serve the sub-$5m band is not
chronology hours; it is the **delay analysis, quantum, report drafting and expert time** (400–1,050 of the
600–1,650 hours), none of which Matter Zero touches. **A matter uneconomic at $240K is still uneconomic at
$190K.** The tool does not open the abandoned 39%; it makes the *served* band 13–22% cheaper. This is the single
most important correction in this report, because the revenue framing is what the synthesis explicitly relies on
to defeat the cannibalisation objection (§W1.5, CM-D §5.1 #1).

**(d) "No procurement, no MSA, no security questionnaire, signer is the partner, same week" does not survive
2026 privilege practice.** The documents are counsel-supplied and privileged; the privilege belongs to the *end
client*, not the consultancy. 2026 outside-counsel guidelines increasingly *"require explicit approval before
client data is processed by any third-party tool, AI included"* (§1.5). The real approval chain is
partner → instructing firm → end client, plus a contractual no-training warranty and, for institutional clients,
a security posture a solo founder does not have. Consultancies also cannot pool or train — which the corpus
concedes — meaning the compounding "record-quality-vs-outcome dataset" (the whole defensibility case) requires
an affirmative retention permission from every client behind every consultancy.

**(e) Internal build is easier than the corpus assumes.** A 25-person consultancy in 2026 has M365 Copilot with
**Researcher** grounded on the matter's SharePoint and mailbox (§1.2), or Claude, and the entitlement rubric is
public (SCL 2nd ed. free; AACE free to members) — the corpus says so itself, as a *solo-founder-positive*
finding. It is equally a consultancy-positive finding.

**(f) Founder credibility is a live objection here specifically.** The buyer's product is defensibility under
cross-examination; AACE RP 29R-03 §1.1 exists to minimise *"black-box or voodoo analyses."* A chronology and
sufficiency tool sold by a founder with no PSP/CCP/RICS/PE and no chronology ever built is an unbadged component
in a tribunal-facing workflow. Not disqualifying — the tool is a non-testifying aid — but it means credential
cover must be bought *before* the first pitch, not after.

### The best available rebuttal
The entitlement semantics really are absent from aiR: no notice register, no EOT/compensability separation, no
Division 00 clause reasoning, no schedule linkage, no per-event tribunal-facing sufficiency test against a
construction rubric. aiR is a litigator's tool sold per-GB inside RelativityOne — many 10–50 person claims
consultancies do not hold that licence, and the sub-$5m matters at issue rarely justify an eDiscovery platform
at all. Alone among the eight, this wedge's buyer pays for **hours**, not for **recovery** — which is the only
willingness-to-pay that has ever been evidenced anywhere in the corpus. The ingestion constraint genuinely is
native. And the discovery analysis (§1.3) actually *favours* this wedge: a per-matter engagement, commissioned
after a dispute exists, delivered into a consultancy retained through counsel, is the ***William A. Gross***
fact pattern, which was **held protected**.

### Verdict: **WOUNDED**
Not killed: the entitlement-register and sufficiency delta is real, unserved, and the buyer's payment motive is
the only proven one in the set. Not surviving as written: the moat claim, the price, the revenue framing and the
sales-cycle claim are all falsified.

**Required reshaping to survive:**
1. **Stop selling chronology.** Sell the **construction entitlement layer** — notice/entitlement register,
   EOT-vs-compensable separation, per-event tribunal-facing sufficiency — explicitly positioned *on top of*
   whatever review platform they already run, including aiR.
2. **Reprice to $2,500–$4,000/matter** against a $120–180/hr analyst rate. $7,500 is not defensible against a
   buyer who knows their own cost basis, and this buyer does.
3. **Delete the "bid the sub-$5m matters" line.** It is false and a partner will do the arithmetic in the room.
4. **Solve privilege before the first pitch**: single-tenant or customer-tenant processing, contractual
   no-training/no-pooling, deletion on demand, and a one-page outside-counsel-guideline response pack.
5. **Buy credential cover** — a named PSP/CCP/RICS advisor who has reviewed the rubric.

## WEDGE 2 — DAY 20 · **WOUNDED — and the strongest survivor**

### The strongest kill argument
**The entire ROI rests on an event frequency that a dedicated search still cannot verify, and the "statutory
fuse" has no located judicial enforcement.**

- No Caltrans publication of PCR filing counts, supplementation rates or waiver determinations exists in the
  public record. I looked directly. The Construction Manual language is confirmed
  (https://dot.ca.gov/programs/construction/construction-manual/section-5-4-disputes) and ePCR is real
  (https://dot.ca.gov/programs/construction/epcr), but the volume is dark.
- The only public volume signal points the wrong way: Caltrans has described a **falling arbitration caseload**
  and **19 cases in arbitration, ~$39M exposure** (§1.7, `UNVERIFIED` secondary). A model of 2–4 waived events
  per $100M of *contractor* revenue implies, across a multi-billion-dollar annual program, statewide waived
  entitlement in the tens of millions per year. That is possible — waived claims never reach arbitration by
  definition — but it sits at the top of the plausible band, not the middle.
- **No reported California decision enforcing §5-1.43A waiver could be located.** The fuse is currently a
  spec-book assertion. California and federal doctrine disfavour forfeiture and routinely excuse notice where the
  owner had actual knowledge and suffered no prejudice (cf. *Hoel-Steffen*, 456 F.2d 760, 768, on the federal
  side). Until a case is found, the pitch is *"your spec book says so,"* not *"a tribunal has done it."*

**Second kill: the corpus's own restated structural rule predicts this is the copyable one.** CM-A §3.3 —
two-sided platforms ship adversarial machinery *when an external authority makes the judgement* (Levelset
$484.1M; Payapps $387M). Caltrans is an external authority with a bright-line rule, a published form and a
statutory consequence. One state, one spec book, published date arithmetic. HCSS already holds the daily-report
substrate with 128,000 users; Oracle owns XER; InEight is owned by a heavy-civil contractor.

**Third kill — and this is where the brief's discovery concern actually lands.** The Day 20 output is
generated **routinely, under a subscription, on every project, because the contract requires it.** That is
simultaneously the *Alta Refrigeration* pattern (created in accordance with company policy → not work product)
and the *G.M. Harston* pattern (created to discharge a contractual obligation → ordinary course → not work
product). The dual-purpose test asks whether the document *"would have been prepared in substantially similar
form … irrespective of the expected litigation"* — for a subscription product the honest answer is **yes**.
**So the persisted missing-evidence schedule on a Caltrans job is (i) not work product, (ii) discoverable in any
subsequent arbitration, and (iii) sitting on a public agency's contract file, potentially reachable under the
CPRA.** It is a monthly, dated, machine-generated catalogue of the contractor's own evidentiary weaknesses.

**Fourth kill: the TIA is the weakest component.** No tribunal is known to have accepted software-generated
delay analysis without an expert; AACE §1.2(d) concedes *"all methods are subject to manipulation as they all
involve judgment calls by the analyst."* A machine TIA invites the resident engineer to reject on method — which
is worse than late, because it looks like compliance and is not.

**Fifth: the buyer may already comply.** If heavy-civil contractors with a schedule engineer and a cost engineer
already file on time, the product saves hours and collapses to a $500/mo efficiency tool.

### The best available rebuttal
It is the only wedge where the **counterparty writes the product specification** and the **counterparty
supplies the deadline**. The regime is confirmed not displaced by statute — **PCC §9204 expressly exempts
Transportation** (§1.6), which I attempted to use as the kill and could not. The budget line escapes the 0.26%
technology cage (job cost; *Tip Top*, 695 F.3d 1276, on federally-aided work). The channel is 1,350 named firms
with published directories at ~$1,000 of association membership. The messaging survives both the "GC 101" test
and the eSUB test because the artefact is mandated, not habitual. **And critically: the load-bearing unknown is
resolvable for $0 in three weeks by a CPRA request** — which is not true of any other wedge.

The discovery objection is a **design constraint, not a kill**: ship the gap analysis as an **ephemeral,
non-retained "evidence you can still collect" checklist** with customer-controlled purge, and never as a
persisted schedule. The *deliverable* is the costed estimate + TIA + evidence index; the gap list is a
working aid that expires.

### Verdict: **WOUNDED — and I would promote it above Matter Zero**
Its two live attacks (frequency, copyability) are **testable for free in weeks**. Matter Zero's attacks (aiR
already ships it free; the rate is ~2× wrong; the sub-$5m argument is arithmetically false) are **already
resolved against it** and cost a repricing of 50–65%.

**Required reshaping:** (1) CPRA first, build second; (2) ephemeral gap analysis, persisted cost build + TIA;
(3) per-project, job-costed, allowability confirmed contract by contract (`UNVERIFIED` for Caltrans state funds);
(4) a named PSP/CCP or licensed PE reviewing every TIA before it leaves — the artefact goes to a resident
engineer and the founder has no credential; (5) treat California as the *only* state until 5 paying customers.

## WEDGE 3 — THE WRITE-OFF AUTOPSY · **KILLED as a paid engagement**

### The strongest kill argument
**The buyer's own lawyer kills it, and the synthesis has identified the wrong failure mode.**

The synthesis names the kill condition as *confidentiality* — will CFOs hand over files? The real kill is
**creation, not disclosure.** The autopsy produces a dated, company-wide, CFO-commissioned document that
catalogues, job by job, every dollar the company failed to bill **and the specific reason its own contemporaneous
records were inadequate**. Under §1.3:

- It is commissioned by the CFO for a **business** purpose (understanding write-offs), not by counsel in
  anticipation of litigation. Under the "because-of" test it fails at the first hurdle.
- It is precisely the *Alta Refrigeration* shape — a report generated because the organisation decided to
  generate reports of that kind — and the *Safeco* shape — a consultant evaluating performance before any
  dispute exists.
- It is **retrospective and portfolio-wide**, which is the worst possible scope. Once it exists it is
  discoverable in **every** future dispute the contractor has, against **every** counterparty, and it is a
  ready-made impeachment exhibit: *"your own 2026 audit concluded your records do not support entitlement."*
- Collateral exposure the synthesis does not consider: the external auditor may have to consider it in the
  WIP/receivable review; the surety may ask for it at renewal; and if it surfaces live, time-barred or
  released claims, the contractor now has **actual knowledge**, which starts clocks and creates disclosure
  questions it did not have before.

**Second kill: it is the wedge most exposed to the 66/53 problem, and it is priced on the assumption that the
answer comes back favourable.** The synthesis's own kill condition #3 concedes this. Charging $12,500 for a
diagnostic whose most likely finding is *"you were short-paid on price, not on paperwork"* is selling a
coin-flip as a conclusion.

**Third: it is founder-bound, non-recurring, unventurable** — conceded — and the delivery-compression assumption
(below ~40 founder-hours after 10 iterations) is untested.

settlement agreements with confidentiality clauses that bind the contractor, not just embarrass it.

shitty GPT wrapper."* It requires nobody to change behaviour. It lands in the Professional Fees purse (0.50% of

### Verdict: **KILLED as a $12,500 product. SURVIVES as a free, anonymised, counsel-mediated research programme.**
Reshaped: run it **free**, on **anonymised closed matters**, engaged **through the contractor's construction

## WEDGE 4 — THE DEFENSIBLE PRICE · **KILLED in its stated form**

### The strongest kill argument
**The credential pitch runs backwards. "Argue with MCAA" hands the payer a free, published, peer-reviewed
rebuttal written by the claimant's own industry.**

The wedge's central mechanism (§W4.5) is that citing an association-published factor *"changes the argument from
'your number is made up' to 'argue with MCAA.'"* Per §1.4:

- The MCAA percentages are **not empirical** — *"based on contractor opinions not empirical studies."*
- **Measured mile is preferred**; MCAA is used only where measured mile is unavailable, or to **corroborate**
  another method. It is the **fallback**, not the standard.
- **Ankura** — a top-four construction claims consultancy, i.e. exactly who will be on the other side —
  publishes that MCAA factors and modified total cost *"have relatively low litigation success rates when
  compared to 'superior' approaches"* and asks in its own title whether MCAA is *"a Modified Total Cost Approach
  in Disguise."* Modified total cost is the method tribunals most distrust.
- Ibbs & Sun's *comprehensive study and critique* is the standard reference and is the ready-made cross-
  examination.

So the GC's rebuttal to a $2,500 "defensible price" is: *"Your number is the MCAA factor table, which is
contractor opinion, which Ibbs and Sun and Ankura say has a low success rate, and which the boards accept only
where measured mile is unavailable — and you have not shown me measured mile is unavailable."* **That rebuttal is
free, published, and one search away. The wedge arms the counterparty better than the claimant.**

**Second kill: the one thing that makes MCAA factors survive is the thing software structurally cannot supply.**
They are accepted where *"subjectivity is overcome by the … expert's reasoned judgment in light of the facts of
the particular project,"* and they require excluding contractor-caused inefficiency. A machine applying a factor
table is the definition of the *"black-box or voodoo analysis"* AACE RP 29R-03 §1.1 was written to attack.
**Automating MCAA does not convert a black box into an audited method. It converts an expert's judgement into a
black box.**

**Third: the input prerequisite is already the documented blocker.** Long International, on 20+ years of
process-industrial projects: *"there has not been a single instance where job conditions or project records
would allow the proper 'textbook' use of the Measured Mile Method."* The wedge's own kill condition — fewer than
3 in 10 contractors can supply labour hours joined to activity and date — is very likely already met.

**Fourth: C4 — it cannot ship standalone**, conceded by the synthesis.

**Fifth: the lane is being occupied from two directions.** Clearstory's **COR Pricing Agent** (closed beta 28 May
2026, GA later in 2026) prices CORs off the project rate library for 14,000+ contractors, and **Magra** now
advertises the full quantum stack including lost productivity (§1.10). Neither does defensible productivity loss —
but both own the moment, the buyer and the word "pricing."

**Sixth: the association will probably not license it, and a refusal is not neutral.** The corpus flags MCAA/NECA
permission as a free phone call and near-fatal if refused. Add: a *conditional* permission ("cite us, but the
output must state that the factors require expert judgement on project-specific facts") is the most likely
outcome, and it is functionally a refusal for an automated product.

### The best available rebuttal
66% disputed pricing is the largest `SOURCED` cause of short-payment; nobody in any language scores above 1 on
recoverable_dollar_estimation; and CM-B's actual insight is correct — *"the product that wins on quantum is the
one that assembles the evidence for the prerequisites, not the one that does the arithmetic."*

### Verdict: **KILLED as "association-method quantum."**
The *problem* is the largest sourced one in the program. The *proposed solution* is the industry's disfavoured
method, automated, without the judgement that makes it admissible, cited from a source the opposing consultant
publicly critiques. **What survives is not a wedge but a component:** the **measured-mile-prerequisite assembly
engine** — the baseline-period identification, the labour-hours-to-activity-to-date join, and an explicit
statement of what the record cannot support. That belongs inside Wedges 1 and 2, which is where the synthesis
already put it as a step-3 expansion. Do not resurrect it as an entry.

## 3. THE SIX SPECIFIED KILL SHOTS — ADJUDICATED

### 3.1 The evidence hole — *fatal to the ROI models and to the pitch, not to the mechanism*
Van Oord (~£10m claim lost entirely; repay £1,895,349.89 + £588,882.98, on out-of-time notice and Daily
Progress Reports that *"make so few references to standing time or disruption"*) and its mirror (Walter Lilly,
won *because* of COINS records) establish that **record quality determines outcomes in both directions**. That
is a mechanism, and it is real. It establishes **no frequency and no value**, and CM-C exhausted HKA, Arcadis,
Dodge, CFMA, Rabbet, Levelset, Billd, Siteline, FMI and JBKnowledge trying.

**The operative consequence, applying the brief's own rule C11:** *no wedge may be sold on a modelled leak.*
Only three artefact types survive that rule — (i) an invoice the buyer already pays (consultant hours),
(ii) an artefact an external authority already demands (the Caltrans PCR), (iii) the buyer's own ledger (the
autopsy). **Those are precisely the synthesis's top three.** So the *ranking method* is sound even though the
*numbers inside it* are not. That is the most useful thing I can say in the synthesis's favour.

### 3.2 66% disputed pricing vs 53% insufficient backup — *real, but the synthesis over-reads it in both directions*
Three corrections:
1. **They are not mutually exclusive.** The survey permits multiple reasons; 53% is enormous in absolute terms.
   "The smaller half" is still a majority of respondents.
2. **They are causally linked, not competing.** A price is disputed *because* it is unsupported. Clearstory's own
   GC report says two-thirds of GCs name unsolicited changes and T&M as the single biggest driver of change-order
   risk — i.e. the pricing dispute is largely *about* T&M substantiation. Wedges 1/3/5/6 are not optimising a
   disjoint smaller problem.
3. **But the ranking is not upside down either — because Wedge 4 is the one I killed.** The correct conclusion is
   sharper and worse than a re-ranking: **the price problem is the largest sourced cause of non-payment, and
   nobody in the wedge set attacks it defensibly.** The only wedge that tried does so with the industry's
   disfavoured, non-empirical, publicly-critiqued method. **That is a genuine hole in the wedge set.**

### 3.3 Gather's eleven case studies with zero recovered CEs — *the most important fact in the corpus, and it does not falsify the category — it falsifies the recovery pitch*
Eight years, £25bn of works, 4,500 daily users, 10M+ records, an NEC4 drafter as founder, a mandated notice
regime, two-way Procore sync, a public API and an MCP server. Every quantified published outcome is **money
withheld from a contractor** (Network Rail £300k+), **claims rejected** (Costain A12, 15% on the spot), or
**admin labour saved** (Circet £140k). Not one recovered compensation event in GBP. And in 2026 the headline has
moved *further* from recovery: **"Detect 40% More Comp Events"** (§1.12). The company is shrinking.

**Read correctly:** detection is achievable and does not monetise; assembled evidence converts into savings for
**payers** and hours for **professionals**, and into nothing measurable for **claimants**. Every wedge that
depends on contractor willingness-to-pay-for-recovery (2, 3, 4, 5) inherits this null result. **Wedge 1 does
not** — its buyer pays for hours. That is the single strongest surviving argument for the funded pick, and it is
not the argument the synthesis leads with.

### 3.4 The eSUB precedent — *weaker than claimed, in a way that helps nobody*
eSUB still trades in 2026 (§1.11). So the natural experiment does not prove death; it proves **a small, durable,
unventurable ceiling** for documentation-hygiene positioning. That is the honest read, and it is the realistic
ceiling for Wedge 5 and for any wedge that drifts into "capture everything."

### 3.5 Discovery exposure on the missing-evidence schedule — *doctrinally strong, empirically unproven, and it lands in the opposite place to where the brief expected*

**It is NOT fatal to Wedge 1.** Per-matter, commissioned after a dispute exists, delivered into a consultancy
retained through counsel, short-term, discussing the claim's positions — that is the ***William A. Gross***
(Hill International) fact pattern, which was **held protected**, 262 F.R.D. 354 (S.D.N.Y. 2009).

**It IS the controlling design constraint on Wedges 2, 3 and 5**, which are routine, subscription-generated, and
in Wedge 2's case contractually required. *Alta Refrigeration* (policy-generated report → not protected) and
*G.M. Harston* (contractually-required evaluation → ordinary course → not protected) are directly on point, and
the dual-purpose test — *"would it have been prepared in substantially similar form irrespective of the expected
litigation"* — answers **yes** for anything a subscription produces automatically.

**Second, independent exposure that applies to all of them:** Rule 26(a)(2)(B)(ii)'s **"facts or data
considered"** is read expansively — *"anything received, reviewed, read, or authored by the expert."* If the
testifying expert opens the missing-evidence schedule, it is disclosed.

**Empirical status: unproven.** I searched for a construction case in which a party's own internal
document-completeness assessment was used against it and found none — consistent with CM-D's `UNVERIFIED`.

**Verdict: not fatal, but it costs the product three things the corpus was counting on** — (a) persistence (the
artefact must be ephemeral and purgeable), (b) the retrospective portfolio artefact (which is what kills Wedge
3), and (c) the "system of record the testifying expert opens on day one" end-state (which must be contractually
firewalled instead). And it must be answered convincingly, in the first meeting, by a founder with no legal
credential, to a buyer whose entire product is defensibility. **Treat it as a first-order design input, not a
legal footnote.**

### 3.6 Consultant channel conflict — *the rebuttal is arithmetically false*
The synthesis defeats cannibalisation with *"you can bid the sub-$5m matters you currently turn away."* On the
corpus's own hours table that is wrong: the tool removes **13–22% of total matter hours** (§W1 attack (c)), and
the reason the sub-$5m band is abandoned is the delay/quantum/report/expert block the tool does not touch.
**The channel is real only for fixed-fee and capped-T&M margin protection — which raw/11 itself calls "a narrow
slice"** — and it comes with the customer-becomes-competitor risk and a 3.3%-operating-margin end market.
CM-C §3(d) already wrote the correct verdict: *"real near-term revenue, genuine channel, terrible long-term
strategic position."*

## 5. THE SINGLE MOST LIKELY REASON THIS WHOLE THESIS FAILS

Nobody will pay for evidence sufficiency, because the party who values assembled evidence most is the party who
benefits from the *absence* of a claim. Gather ran exactly this experiment for eight years under the best
conditions available anywhere on earth — a mandated NEC4 notice regime with an integer deadline and a named
administrator, an NEC4 drafter as founder, £25bn of works, 4,500 daily users, ten million records, two-way
Procore sync — and produced eleven case studies in which every quantified outcome is money **withheld** from a
contractor, claims **rejected**, or administrative labour **saved**, and not one is a compensation event
recovered; in 2026 its own headline has retreated further, to "detect 40% more comp events," while the company
shrank from fourteen employees to ten. That is not a marketing failure, it is the market's answer: assembled
evidence monetises as savings for payers and as hours for professionals, and does not monetise as recovery for
claimants. Everything else follows from it. The contractor-side ROI models all rest on a leak of 0.1–0.6% of
revenue that no published source establishes and that CM-C failed to find after exhausting every industry
dataset that exists. The consultancy channel pays for hours rather than outcomes, and the hours the product
actually removes are 13–22% of a matter — not enough to change which matters they can bid, which is the only
argument that defeats cannibalisation. The chronology half of the work has just been given away free inside
RelativityOne. And the only buyer in the entire program with a documented, appropriated, published budget is the
owner, whose money forecloses every other wedge the moment you take it. The most likely end state is therefore
not failure to build and not failure to sell — it is a $300–700K-revenue tooling business selling into a
3.3%-operating-margin industry whose customers can become competitors, which is precisely what CM-C predicted in
its own words and precisely what Gather already is.
