# 1. THE CRUX — ADJUDICATING THE DISCOVERY TENSION

> *"A per-project subscription that produces a contractually-required artefact appears to be the least
> protected structure — yet Day 20 is built on exactly that."*

**The premise is correct as doctrine. The inference is wrong, and it is wrong because it treats
discoverability as a uniform harm. It is not. Discoverability only harms a document you intended to keep.**

### 1.1 Sort Day 20's outputs by intended destination

| Output | Destination | Discovery exposure |
|---|---|---|
| Itemised cost estimate with derivation stated (§5-1.43C.3) | **Handed to Caltrans by contract** | **None. It is the claim.** |
| Time Impact Analysis (§5-1.43C.4) | **Handed to Caltrans by contract** | **None.** |
| Evidence index mapping each line to a dated source | Supports the two above | **None — it is the substantiation.** |
| **Missing-evidence schedule** | **Internal working aid** | **Real. This is the only exposed item.** |

Nobody asserts work product over a filed pleading. Three of Day 20's four outputs are **disclosure-by-design**;
§5-1.43D goes further and *requires* consistency of nature, circumstances and basis across the Initial,
Supplemental and Full-and-Final records — Caltrans has drafted a regime whose entire purpose is to force early,
consistent, written disclosure. *Alta Refrigeration* and *G.M. Harston* are simply not engaged by a document
you are contractually obliged to serve on the counterparty.

**So the discovery finding costs Day 20 exactly one feature, and that feature is not the one anyone pays for.**
The red team already prescribed the fix (ephemeral, purgeable gap checklist). The fix is free.

### 1.2 Now run the same test on Matter Zero — the protection is weaker than the red team allowed

The red team says *William A. Gross* (262 F.R.D. 354) **held protected** and therefore favours Matter Zero.
Read the fact pattern the red team itself reproduced: Hill International was retained **by the owner's
counsel**, on a **separate firewalled team**, with **no contact with the contractors**, on a **short-term**
assignment, with litigation **imminent**, and the report **discussed litigation positions**. Five conjunctive
conditions, and **the party claiming protection bears the burden** (FRCP 26(b)(3)(A)).

Matter Zero's buyer is **the consultancy**, not counsel. Consultancies are frequently retained **directly by
the contractor**, pre-suit, on open-ended assignments. On that engagement shape the *Gross* protection is not
available — and the vendor does not control which shape any given matter takes.

**Worse, there is a second exposure that pierces work-product entirely.** FRCP 26(a)(2)(B)(ii) compels
disclosure of *"facts or data considered by the witness"*, read expansively as *"anything received, reviewed,
read, or authored by the expert."* Matter Zero's missing-evidence schedule is designed to be read by the
analyst who works **under the testifying expert**. Privilege does not save it. Rule 26 disclosure is not a
privilege question.

### 1.3 Ruling

> **The discovery finding is a design constraint on both wedges and a discriminator between neither.**
> It confines Day 20's exposure to one deletable working aid whose deletion costs nothing.
> It leaves Matter Zero's exposure **conditional on a fact pattern the vendor cannot control** and
> **pierced independently by Rule 26(a)(2)(B)(ii)** regardless of privilege.
>
> **The red team's promotion of Day 20 survives the red team's own discovery finding. Ranking stands.**

### 1.4 The design consequence that neither manager reached

The discovery doctrine punishes **routine, automatic, subscription-generated** output. It does not punish
**event-triggered** output produced because a specific dispute has arisen. **Therefore: stop selling Day 20 as
a per-project subscription and sell it as a per-event artefact.**

This single change does three things at once:

1. **It fixes the discovery structure.** A pack commissioned *after* the Engineer's adverse RFI response, in a
   regime whose express consequence is *"Bar to arbitration (Pub Cont Code §10240.2)"*, is prepared **because
   of** anticipated adversarial proceedings. That is the correct side of the dual-purpose test.
2. **It removes frequency risk from the customer's purchase decision.** Under a subscription, a customer with
   one event a year is overpaying and will churn. Under per-event pricing, every purchase is self-justifying.
   Frequency stops being the customer's problem and becomes purely **my TAM problem**.
3. **It stops the product being surveillance.** A base tier that only ingests and watches the 5-day clock
   produces **no dated catalogue of the contractor's own weaknesses**. Nothing to discover.

**This is the most valuable structural change available in the entire program, and it costs nothing.**

# 2. THE OTHER TWO DISPUTES, ADJUDICATED

## 2.1 Is Matter Zero dead given Relativity aiR? — **aiR does not kill it. The fee model does.**

**On aiR itself: the red team over-claims and the synthesis over-claimed worse.**

*The synthesis was wrong.* §W1.5's *"none of those builds a chronology"* and §W1.6's *"24–36 months and
unlikely to be attempted"* are falsified: **aiR for Case Strategy went GA 12 January 2026** with chronology
construction, citation-linked fact extraction, **evidence-gap analysis** and helpful/harmful classification,
and RelativityOne *"includes … aiR for Case Strategy – at no additional cost."*

*But the red team over-reads it.* aiR is **litigation-stage**, sold **per-GB inside RelativityOne**, to a
**litigator**. It has no notice register, no EOT-vs-compensable separation, no Division 00 general-conditions
reasoning, no schedule linkage, and no per-event sufficiency test against a construction rubric. The
pre-litigation construction-entitlement layer **is** open, exactly as the brief suspects. A 23-person QS/delay
consultancy does not hold a RelativityOne licence; the instructing law firm might, on the matters large enough
to have one.

**So the honest verdict on aiR: it commoditises the chronology — which was the visible, demonstrable,
"look what it saved you" half of the pitch — and leaves the entitlement semantics open. That is a wound, not
a kill.**

**The kill comes from somewhere else, and both managers walked past it.**

raw/11 §13, Org incentive, rated **HARD**, verbatim:

> *"A product that removes 400 chronology hours from a $600k engagement destroys ~$160k of revenue and
> returns, at best, a licence fee an order of magnitude smaller. **Nobody's compensation plan rewards that.**"*

Combine three sourced facts:

- **T&M is dominant.** CRA 10-K: *"In most instances, we charge clients on a time-and-materials basis."*
  FTI's fixed-fee language is permissive and minority: *"Certain practices … **may** enter into … fixed-fee."*
  raw/11 calls the fixed-fee/capped-T&M slice **"a narrow slice."**
- **On T&M, hours removed are revenue removed.** The value of Matter Zero to a T&M consultancy is
  **negative**, before you charge them anything.
- **On the narrow fixed-fee slice, the value is real but small** — the red team's arithmetic is correct and
  decisive: the tool removes **13–22% of total matter hours**, not enough to change which matters get bid.

**Ruling: Matter Zero's willingness-to-pay is confined to a minority fee model, within which the saving is
13–22% of matter hours. That is not a wedge. It is a feature with a narrow addressable base.** The corpus's
proudest claim for it — *"the buyer pays for hours, which is the only willingness-to-pay ever evidenced"* — is
exactly backwards on the dominant contract type: **the buyer is paid for hours, which is why it will not buy
their removal.**

## 2.2 Is the corrected rate fatal to per-matter pricing? — **No. And the red team over-corrected.**

**The red team's correction is directionally right and quantitatively wrong.**

It substitutes **Diales** (£165–200/hr) for **Exponent/FTI** ($396–$442/hr). But Diales is the **UK / Middle
East / APAC** pure-play, and its rate is `DERIVED` from an **assumed denominator** (180 fee earners ×
*~1,700–1,900 assumed available hours* × 71.6%). The buyer named in CM-D §1.10 is **US** — Trauner, Imperium,
Long International, VERTEX, CCI. raw/11's own reading is explicit: *"A US construction-claims engagement is
staffed on a pyramid that produces a blended realised rate of roughly **$350–$500/hour**"*; *"A UK/international
engagement blends much lower."* Note also that FTI's **$442** is a genuine *average billable rate* that
*"excludes success fees, pass-throughs and outside consultants"* — it is not a revenue-over-hours artefact.

**Where the red team is right is the layer, not the geography.** Document review and chronology is **analyst**
work, which sits below the blend in any pyramid. The defensible US analyst figure is **~$175–$275/hr billed**,
not $400 and not $120–180.

| | Synthesis | Red team | **Adjudicated** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rate applied to the displaced block | $400/hr (US blend) | $120–180/hr (Diales-derived) | **$175–$275/hr (US analyst)** |
| Value of a 200–600 hr block | $80k–$240k | $24k–$108k | **$35k–$165k** |
| Saving at 40–60% | $32k–$160k | $10k–$65k | **$14k–$99k** |
| Defensible price at ~15% of saving | $5,000–$25,000 | $1,500–$10,000 | **$2,000–$15,000** |

**Ruling: the rate correction resets the price to roughly $3,000–$5,000/matter. It is not fatal.** At
$4,000/matter against a firm running 20–60 matters a year, ACV is $80k–$240k — a perfectly good business.
**The price was never the problem. The fee model is.** $4,000 of value to a firm whose economics *punish* the
saving is still unsellable; $7,500 to a firm whose economics reward it would have sold.

# 4. THE TEN RESEARCH QUESTIONS, ANSWERED

**1. How do contractors manage this today?**
Manually, late, and with a consultant hired **after** the loss. The stack is Excel COR logs, Outlook, a daily-
report tool (HCSS HeavyJob, 128,000 users), P6/XER, and a change-order workflow that is well covered. The DIY
substitute stack scores **3 on daily reports and change-order workflow** but **0 on evidence_completeness** —
the corpus's phrasing is exact: *"nothing on earth checks whether a claim's proof is complete before a human
submits it."* Dedicated headcount already exists and is priced: **Tutor Perini Change Order Engineer
$85–120k**, AECOM Claims Manager $140–182k, 1,000+ open US Contract Administrator roles.

**2. What is solved by incumbents?**
The **front half and the containers**. Contract ingestion, clause extraction, notice *drafting*, deadline
*alerting*, change-order workflow and evidence *storage* are all solved and largely free: Trunk Tools gives
14-category clause extraction away with no account; Procore shipped a Contract Review agent 21 May 2026 and a
Change Analysis agent GA 23 Jul 2026; Document Crunch (Trimble, $246.4M, closed 4 Apr 2026) has generated
notices agentically since 9 Jun 2026; **Aconex scores 3 on deadline tracking** with working-day arithmetic and
auto-flipping Overdue status; Caltrans ePCR sends reminders free. **What is unsolved: dims 14, 15 and 16 —
evidence completeness, recoverable-dollar estimation, claim-package generation — where nobody exceeds 1 across
23 scored entities including the DIY stack.**

**3. How strong are AI-native contract products?**
Strong at reading, absent at asserting. Document Crunch is the benchmark and its **Procore app requests zero
data permissions** (`connector_required:false`) — it reads contracts and nothing else, never crossing from
static documents into project records; its Notice Builder is 100% human-triggered. The claims-AI cohort
(Magra, Lexilio, ClaimMaster.ai, Aven-AI) is **demoware**: every Magra integration still marked "Upcoming",
zero named customers, and a headline that moved $240k → $17,824 per event. **Every AI claims product in every
language scores 0 on recoverable_dollar_estimation.** The one product doing quantum properly — Easyclaim,
€599/case, 21-page derivation over 26 cost categories — has **no AI, no ingest, and runs offline as a single
HTML file.**

**4. Are consultants the incumbent?**
**Yes, and they are structurally unable to self-disrupt.** HKA runs ~2,000 engagements a year across 6,500
clients; capability scores are 3s across the entire recovery pipeline. This is *"not an under-served problem —
it is an expensively-served, late-served problem."* But an hours business at 57–73% utilisation cannot fund
the destruction of its own hours; **Diales turns £43.0m into £1.4m underlying operating profit (3.3%)**; and
**in ~9 years of aggressive M&A none of the twelve major firms has bought or built a claims-detection
product** — they buy *people*, not software. Their blind spot is structural: HKA's CRUX only counts a project
once **>30 hours** of claim work exists, so they literally cannot see the pre-dispute phase.

**5. Can a product sit above Procore?**
**No. This is closed, and it is the cleanest negative finding in the program.** The Developer Policy
(eff. 30 Sep 2025) forbids developers to *"Scrape, parse, harvest, build databases, bulk export"* API Data or
use it to train AI. **Procore revoked Trunk Tools' API access in September 2025** and refunded its Groundbreak
booth — a $70M-funded, Insight-backed company with 200+ Gilbane projects; marketplace enumeration on
19 Aug 2026 returned **zero "trunk"** across 455 apps. And the marketplace does not distribute: **Document
Crunch has 187 installs against 17,850 Procore customers (~1.0%) after four years of partnership.**
Hypothesis E is dead twice over — by policy and by distribution.

**6. Can uploads prove value pre-integration?**
**Yes, and upload is strictly better than integration here.** Oracle publicly documents the XER field
mappings; **contracts already mandate monthly XER deliverables**, so the file is already being produced and
sent; prior art (XER Schedule Toolkit, Schedule Auditor, ScheduleLens) proves it works with zero Oracle
relationship; and **forensic delay analysis runs on dated snapshots anyway, so upload loses nothing
evidentially.** Upload beats integrate on cost, speed, legal risk and procurement simultaneously. There is a
counterintuitive advantage: because Procore's policy bars the best-funded competitors from parsing its data,
**a file-upload/email-forward V1 has better commercial-record coverage than they do** — and Trunk Tools has no
email connector and no daily-log agent, the two richest contemporaneous sources.

**7. Which missed-revenue problem is most frequent?**
**Unapproved, unbilled change orders — by a wide margin.** 97% of specialty contractors start change-order
work before authorization; **77% have written it off**; 91% of GCs short-pay. That is ~1.7× more frequent than
the statutory-lien-deadline leak (56% of subs in two years, Siteline 2026, n=492). **But note the ranking
below it:** disputed **pricing** is cited by **66%** of GCs as the reason they short-pay, above the **53%**
citing insufficient backup. **Missed contractual notice is not measured anywhere by anyone** — its only
real fuse in the US is state DOT.

**8. Is ROI strong enough for $500–$5,000/mo or performance pricing?**
**Partially, and only outside the technology budget.** CFMA (n=1,558): **technology costs are 0.26% of
revenue** — $368k on $139.4M — so **$500/mo is a YES everywhere** (1–3% of the purse), **$1,500 YES at $100M+**,
**$3,000 NO at $50M**, **$5,000 NO below ~$150M** (30% of a $50M sub's entire tech purse). **The unlock is the
budget line, not the price:** on federal and federally-aided work ***Tip Top*, 695 F.3d 1276** makes REA-
preparation costs **allowable contract administration under FAR 31.205-33**, so a pre-claim product is a
**billable job cost**, and Professional Fees is a purse **1.9× larger** (0.50% of revenue) where consultants
are already paid. On performance pricing: **contingency for claim *preparation* is legal** (*Factortame No.8*
held Grant Thornton's quantum-preparation contingency not champertous) while **testifying-expert contingency is
barred** (ABA Model Rule 3.4(b) cmt.) — **the bar is the witness role, not the fee.** But the **value-capture
ceiling is hard: $5,000–$30,000 on a $412k recovery (1.5–8%)**, because take scales with downside borne and a
software vendor bears none. **Do not lead with contingency; cap any success component at 3–8% earned on an
executed change order, never on suit proceeds.**

**9. Who buys?**
**Rank order: (1) claims consultancy 10–50 people — first revenue but a bad long-term position; (2) heavy-
civil / state-DOT contractor — the beachhead; (3) sub CFO/controller $25–150M — cheapest channel.** The sub
owner under $50M has maximum pain and is **unreachable** — eSUB is the warning. The contract administrator is
the **champion, never the buyer**. The rule the corpus earned: ***do not sell to the highest-pain buyer; sell
to whoever can sign and can be found.*** Owner-side has the only appropriated budget in the program
(**Caltrans D3 alone: $7.0M over 3 years, 03CONTCLM27**) but taking it is a **one-way door**.

**10. Could incumbents add the wedge easily?**
**They could; the question is whether they will, and the answer differs by layer.** Detection: **already
shipped** (Procore Change Analysis GA 23 Jul 2026). Notice triggering: **6–12 months.** The schema change
alone — adding `clause_reference` / `notice_date` / `notice_deadline` / `entitlement_basis` to a change object
— is **one quarter of engineering with no liability tail**, and **not one of five incumbents has done it**.
**Evidence sufficiency and quantum are structurally protected**, because Procore, Trimble, Autodesk, Oracle,
Clearstory and Gather all sell to **both sides on the same records** and none can compute *"your client owes
you $412k and here is why they are wrong."* **But the protection has a documented exception:** CM-A's restated
durable rule — *two-sided platforms ship adversarial machinery when an EXTERNAL AUTHORITY makes the judgement,
but will not ORIGINATE the judgement* (Levelset $484.1M; Payapps $387M). **Caltrans is an external authority.
That is why Day 20 is both the best beachhead and the most copyable one, and why the window is 12–24 months.**
