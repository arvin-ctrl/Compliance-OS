# CM-D — BUYERS, JTBD, PRICING & DISTRIBUTION
### Who buys, why, and how a solo founder reaches them

Researched 2026-08-19. Builds on BRIEF.md, NOTES-running.md, raw/11, raw/15, raw/16, CM-A, CM-B.
`UNVERIFIED` = not confirmed from a primary source. `DERIVED` = my arithmetic on sourced inputs, method shown.
`INFERENCE` = my judgement, not a finding.

---

# 0. THE FIVE CLAIMS THIS DOCUMENT MAKES

1. **The fastest, largest, most reachable first buyer is not a contractor. It is the small-to-mid claims
   consultancy** (and the DOT on-call claims prime), because the pain is 200–600 chargeable hours per matter,
   the buyer is a partner with instant signing authority, the cost is a rebillable disbursement, and the data
   arrives as a file dump — which is *exactly* the upload-first V1 the founder constraint demands.
2. **The budget holder for the contractor-side product is the CFO/controller, not the PM** — and the entire
   documented cultural hostility to this product came from PMs and supers, with **not one CFO or project
   executive present in any thread**. The hostility is real and it is from the wrong persona.
3. **Contractor-side is the market; owner-side is a distribution unlock the founder cannot use yet.** Owners
   mandate software (proven, with clause language) — but a mandate is won by a procurement, and procurement is
   the one thing a solo founder structurally cannot run. Worse, **the sequence is one-way**: take owner money
   first and you can never credibly sell the contractor product.
4. **The messaging that survives never asks anyone to document better and never asserts a verdict.** It either
   (a) prices hours already being burned, (b) produces an artefact an external authority already demands, or
   (c) restates the buyer's own accounting numbers back to them.
5. **Price the artefact, not the seat.** Per-seat is capped at $50–150/user/mo by DocuSign/Copilot and is
   charged to a 0.26%-of-revenue G&A line. Per-matter and per-project pricing escapes both cages, and
   per-project pricing is **job-costable**, which matters because 58.8% of contractors recover no IT cost from
   owners today.

## 1.1 Subcontractor owner / president (<$50M revenue)

| | |
|---|---|
| **JTBD, in their words** | *"Get paid for the extra work we already did without blowing up the relationship with the GC I need for the next three jobs."* Corroborated verbatim: *"We have some customers that we handle 5 and 6 figure changes verbally. Others we won't spend an extra $100 without them signing first."* ([Mike Holt Forum](https://forums.mikeholt.com/threads/general-contractor-not-signing-change-orders.47657/), `FLAG: 2008`) |
| **Measured on** | Cash in the bank this Friday; gross margin per job; backlog; the personal relationship with 3–5 key GCs. There is no scorecard — he *is* the scorecard. |
| **Budget authority** | **Total and instant.** No procurement, no committee, no security review. Signature threshold is whatever he says at 6pm. |
| **Buys today** | An Excel COR log; accounting ERP (Foundation, Sage 100 Contractor); sometimes Clearstory Basic (free tier); a lawyer at year-end; **not** Procore (*"ProCore… is out of the budget"*). Specialty trade technology spend = **0.40% of revenue** ([CFMA FY2024, n=510](https://cfma.org/files/o-files/download-file/5aff6a42-7a29-491c-bb4a-fde57e74487b)) → on $30M that is $120K *total*, mostly committed. |
| **Why yes** | **77% of specialty contractors have written off change-order work as bad debt**; 83% say the CO process hurts cash flow ([Clearstory/Dodge 2025](https://www.clearstory.build/construction-blog/2025-specialty-contractor-change-order-report)). He *personally* absorbed those write-offs. At 7.7% net margin ([CFMA FY2024](https://cfma.org/files/o-files/view-file/ce6fe0cc-2dfe-420a-999b-a2ad030acd9a)) a recovered $200K is a visible year. $500/mo is a credit-card purchase below any threshold. |
| **Why no** | Three hard ones. (a) **The relationship strategy is deliberate, not lazy** — lawyers confirm contractors *choose* to suppress formal notice ([Smith Currie, 19 Jun 2025](https://www.smithcurrie.com/publications/common-sense-contract-law/dont-be-litigious-give-notice/)). (b) **Nobody to operate it** — he is the ops person; below ~$100M there is no owner of this problem (raw/15 §2.4). (c) **eSUB is the natural experiment**: it marketed this thesis verbatim to exactly this buyer for a decade and is the smallest, slowest-growing company in the category. |
| **Sales cycle** | **2–6 weeks** once you are in the room. Getting in the room is the entire problem — no list, no channel, not on LinkedIn, and the community channel is hostile and astroturfed. |

## 1.6 GC general counsel / risk manager

| | |
|---|---|
| **JTBD, in their words** | *"Reduce the number and severity of disputes and make sure our file survives discovery."* `INFERENCE`. Roles are real and priced: Jacobs *Construction Risk Manager* **$120,000–$200,000** ([posting](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/view/4338590085)); Turner & Townsend *Risk Manager* **$140–160K**. |
| **Measured on** | Claim exposure and reserves; insurance cost; dispute count and outcome; outside-counsel spend. |
| **Budget authority** | Real, and in a **different, less price-sensitive budget** (legal/risk, not IT). |
| **Buys today** | Outside counsel; claims consultants; Document Crunch (contract review, now Trimble); eDiscovery. |
| **Why yes** | The only persona who reads *Van Oord v Allseas* correctly. **"Poorly drafted or incomplete and unsubstantiated claims" was the #1 global cause of construction disputes** ([Arcadis 2022, p.11](https://media.arcadis.com/-/media/project/arcadiscom/com/expertise/global/contract-solutions/cb063f2cbe31410c9807d7a9bf16f6662022globalconstructiondisputesreportsuccessfullynavigatingthroughtur.pdf)). |
| **Why no — and this is the non-obvious killer** | **Discovery risk.** A system that scores your own evidence as "incomplete" creates a contemporaneous, discoverable record that you knew your claim was weak. Counsel will see that instantly. Add UPL/E&O exposure for an AI interpreting clauses (Procore already carries a standing UPL risk factor; it is worse for us). Also, this role only exists above ~$500M revenue. |
| **Sales cycle** | **4–9 months**, with a privilege/records-retention review you will not win alone. |
| **Design consequence** | Ship the completeness score as **"evidence you can still collect"**, forward-looking and remediable, never as a retrospective verdict on a submitted claim. Offer a customer-controlled retention/purge setting from day one. |

**Reading of the table.** The three buyers a solo founder can actually close inside a quarter are #1, #2 and #3.
They are also, usefully, three *different* motions: a channel (consultancy), an association-mediated vertical
(state DOT heavy civil), and a professional community (CFMA). None depends on inbound, on the Procore
marketplace, or on an API key.

**The most important structural fact in the table:** the two buyers with the highest *pain* scores
(sub owner/president, DOT PM) are not the buyers with the highest *authority-plus-reach*. **Do not sell to the
person with the most pain. Sell to the person who can sign and whom you can find.**

# 3. ADJUDICATION — CONTRACTOR-SIDE RECOVERY vs OWNER-SIDE DEFENCE

## 3.1 The evidence pointing owner-side is strong and must be stated at full strength

1. **The most on-thesis product in the world is evidenced as a defence tool.** Across Gather's eleven
   published case studies, **not one documents a recovered compensation event in £**. The two headline numbers
   are the opposite of what they appear: Network Rail's **£300,000+ is money withheld from a contractor** after
   the *client* used Gather to *"scrutinise labour, plant and time allocation… included in change requests"*;
   Costain A12's headline is ***"15% of claims rejected on the spot."*** Circet's £140,000 is admin labour.
   ([customer stories](https://www.gatherinsights.com/en/customer-stories))
2. **Gather's own positioning concedes it:** *"**We don't take sides.** We provide the objective, timestamped
   evidence that allows Contractors and Clients to agree on fair payment."*
3. **Owners have documented, appropriated budget; contractors do not.** Caltrans District 3 alone is procuring
   **$7,000,000 over three years** for Construction Claims and Scheduling Support (03CONTCLM27). There is **no
   comparable published contractor-side procurement anywhere in this program.** Contractor spend on this is
   salary (Tutor Perini Change Order Engineer $85–120K) and consultant fees — both opaque.
4. **Owners mandate software, and the clause language is standard.** *"The Contractor shall use the Owner's
   Project Management software, e-Builder, as a conduit for all project management tasks… licenses shall be
   provided to the Contractor"* ([LawInsider clause bank](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/e-builder/_1)).
   Gather's own `/en/client` page ships a drafting template for exactly this:
   *"4.7 Commercial Record Management — The Contractor shall use the Employer's nominated Commercial Record
   Management System (Gather)."* **Owner-mandated, owner-funded, contractor-used.**
5. **Owner value is immediate, verifiable and non-adversarial in feel.** Money not paid out shows up in this
   month's application. No attribution problem, no relationship problem, no "prove it recovered something."

## 3.2 The four reasons this still resolves contractor-side

**(a) The Gather evidence is confounded. It is about NEUTRALITY, not about which side has money.**
Gather chose to be neutral *first*, then discovered that a neutral evidence tool sells best to the party who
benefits from neutral evidence — which, on a disputed change request, is the payer. **Gather never ran the
contractor-side experiment.** Their outcomes tell you what happens to *neutral* tools. They tell you nothing
about *partisan* tools, because nobody has built one. This is the single most important qualification on the
whole owner-side case and it has been repeatedly under-stated.

**(b) Neutrality caps price, and the proof is on Gather's own price list.**
Gather publishes **10×–39× ROI** and charges **£500/licence/month flat, "not priced per User", no value
component** ([G-Cloud 14 pricing](https://assets.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud-14/documents/719502/250831346049155-pricing-document-2024-02-28-0942.pdf)).
An assurance tool has no claim on the money it saves, because it never originates a number of its own. The
corollary is the most transferable finding in the program: **only a partisan product can price on recovery.**

**(c) Owner-side assurance is the most copyable thing you could build; contractor-side partisanship is the
least.** Six vendors, five countries, three contract regimes all stop at the identical line — Procore,
Trimble, Autodesk, Oracle, Clearstory, Gather and the NEC six. Datagrid (a Procore company) says it in words:
*"entitlement and approval stay with the responsible project professionals."* But note **what** they stop at:
they stop at *taking a side*, not at *checking records*. Every one of them **can** ship an owner-side
assurance feature without breaking neutrality — "does the application match the record?" is assurance, and
assurance is the half of the market they already serve. **An owner-side product is built in the incumbents'
blast radius. A contractor-side priced position is not.**

**(d) The pain data, the margin arithmetic and the write-off statistics are all contractor-side.**
77% write-off incidence; 33.4% of contract budgets claimed across $2.433tn (HKA CRUX 8th); commercial GC 4.4%
net, sub 7.7%, heavy civil 8.3%. Owners do not have a margin problem; they have a budget-variance problem,
which is slower, more diffuse, and already staffed.

## 3.3 Can one product serve both? Yes — asymmetrically, and only in one order

**The substrate is genuinely two-sided.** Ingest → commercial-event detection → contemporaneous evidence graph
→ **evidence-completeness score**. That last step is the one dimension that is *symmetric by construction*:
**an evidence-completeness score is a claim-strength score to the claimant and a rejection basis to the
payer.** It is also the only capability scoring **0 across every incumbent AND the DIY substitute stack**, and
the only gap the industry does not culturally defend.

**The divergence is at the final two stages: the number and the artefact.**

| Stage | Contractor-side | Owner-side |
|---|---|---|
| Quantum | *"The priced, evidenced position"* — Eichleay, measured mile, MCAA factors, prolongation | Not needed; the contractor supplies the number |
| Artefact | Caltrans PCR / FAR REA / COR with substantiation, aimed at the other party | **Deficiency schedule** — what is missing or unsupported in the application |
| Pricing basis | Recovery | Money withheld |
| Price ceiling | Uncapped (partisan) | Tooling rates (neutral) |

**The order is one-way and it is the decisive constraint.** Take the owner's money first and you can never
credibly sell the contractor product — you will have been paid to make contractors' claims fail, and every
contractor prospect will find out. The reverse is not true: a contractor-side tool that later offers owners a
"review" mode is a normal, precedented extension. **Verisk's Xactimate sells the price book to the carriers
*and* to the contractors fighting them** (CM-A). Sequence, not compatibility, is the binding issue.

## 3.4 VERDICT

> **Contractor-side is the MARKET. Owner-side is a DISTRIBUTION STRATEGY that becomes available in year 2–3,
> and only if you have not taken owner money first.**

Concretely:
- **Sell contractor-side first** (fast, credit-card, partisan, priced on recovery, job-costable).
- **Never contact the owner from the product** — the relationship objection is real and documented, and a tool
  that fires a notice at an owner will be rejected outright.
- **Year 2–3, once you have named project references and SOC 2**, pursue the owner/program mandate — because a
  single owner mandate distributes the contractor-side product to every contractor on that program at zero
  marginal CAC, which is the cheapest distribution mechanism that exists in construction.
- **Do not pursue direct state-DOT sales at any point in the first 20.** Reach the DOT through the prime
  consultant holding the on-call claims contract (buyer #1), who is already funded and already delivering.

**The honest cost of this call:** owner-side has the *better-evidenced budget* ($7.0M Caltrans D3 vs nothing
comparable contractor-side) and the *worse-fitting sales motion* (9–24 month procurement vs the BRIEF's solo
founder constraint). If the founder's constraint were "capital-rich, patient, two-year runway," this
adjudication would flip. Under the stated constraint it does not.

**What would falsify this verdict:** a contractor paying a premium (>$1,500/mo, or any success fee) for
recovered entitlement, sustained for four quarters. Nobody has produced that evidence — **including the company
best positioned in the world to produce it.** Getting one such customer is therefore the highest-information
experiment in the whole plan, and it should be run in the first 90 days.

# 5. MESSAGING TEST

**The two tests every statement must pass:**
1. **The practitioner test** — survives *"This is like GC 101 and no one needs more software for anything.
   CONTRACTING is in the name."*
2. **The eSUB test** — does not sell "document better," which was marketed verbatim to exactly this buyer for a
   decade and produced the smallest company in the category.

## 5.1 THE THREE THAT SURVIVE

### #1 — To the claims consultant / DOT on-call prime

> **"You bill 200 to 600 hours of chronology per matter and write most of it off against the fixed fee. Send
> me the document dump. You get the linked chronology and the missing-evidence schedule back the same day —
> and you can bid the sub-$5m matters you currently turn away."**

**Why it survives the practitioner test:** *"we already know how to do this"* is agreement, not resistance.
They absolutely know how — the labour *is* the cost. The objection has no purchase.
**Why it survives the eSUB test:** it never mentions documentation discipline. It prices hours already being
burned, and it adds revenue (matters they currently decline) rather than adding a habit.
**Grounded in what they're measured on:** utilisation (FTI FLC 57%, Exponent 73%, Diales 71.6%), realisation,
write-off against fixed fee, engagement margin (Diales: **3.3% operating**).

### #2 — To the California / heavy-civil contractor

> **"Caltrans gives you five business days for the Initial PCR and fifteen more for a Supplemental PCR with an
> itemised cost estimate and a Time Impact Analysis. Miss it and §5-1.43A waives the claim and bars
> arbitration. We produce the itemised estimate and the TIA from the daily reports you already file."**

**Why it survives the practitioner test:** "GC 101" is answered by the buyer's own spec book. The requirement
is external, statutory and non-negotiable — arguing with it means arguing with Caltrans, not with a vendor.
And the deliverable it demands (a costed, itemised estimate *plus* a TIA inside 20 days) is one that no
contractor can actually produce by hand at that speed, which is why claims get waived.
**Why it survives the eSUB test:** it sells a **mandated artefact**, not a habit. Nothing about "capture more."
**Grounded in what they're measured on:** claim recovery on this job; spec compliance; job margin at 8.3% net.

### #3 — To the sub CFO / GC CFO

> **"Your unapproved change orders are unbilled WIP. Tell me what you wrote off last year and what's sitting
> past 60 days without backup. I'll show you which of those had the evidence to bill and which didn't — from
> your own closed files, before you buy anything."**

**Why it survives the practitioner test:** it is not addressed to the practitioner. It is addressed to the
person the practitioner reports to, in that person's own units, and it makes no claim about how anyone should
behave in the field.
**Why it survives the eSUB test:** it never proposes documenting better. It proposes an *audit of the past*, on
data the buyer already has, with a verifiable answer. That is also the strongest available answer to
*"another shitty GPT wrapper"* — the first deliverable is checkable against their own ledger.
**Grounded in what they're measured on:** Days in A/R **55.2** · Underbillings-to-Equity **8.1%** · Days of
cash **27** · WIP accuracy · **77% write-off incidence**.

## 5.2 THE THREE THAT FAIL

### ✗ "Never miss a notice deadline again."

Fails both tests, and three independent findings kill it. (a) **It is the lawyer's frame sold to the PM's
budget** — across every practitioner thread reached, people discussed *approval* and *proof*, essentially never
*notice periods*. (b) **It is already solved where it is enforceable**: Oracle Aconex auto-calculates
response-required due dates in working days and flips Outstanding→Overdue per recipient. (c) **Levelset proves
the economics**: the alert was free bait and the **chargeable filing** ($59/notice, $349/lien) was the revenue —
and contractual notice has no filing, no clerk and no fee. Procore then deleted "Lien Rights Management" from
its product catalogue between the FY2023 and FY2024 10-Ks and modelled Levelset customer relationships at a
**4-year life vs 10 years** for its other acquisitions.

### ✗ "AI-powered documentation for construction teams — capture everything, protect your claims."

This is eSUB's proposition almost verbatim (*"It's not about the work you did; it's about the work you
documented"*), aimed at the same buyer, over the same period, and it produced ~16k users and ~60 staff. It
also triggers the documented hostility precisely, because it asks the practitioner to change behaviour and
offers them a cost: *"Your entire goal is to extract value out of these people to create some software."*

### ✗ "Know if you have a claim — AI detects entitlement across your projects."

Fails on three separate grounds. (a) **Detection is gone**: Procore's Change Analysis agent went GA 23 Jul 2026
(*"identifies scope impacts, cost exposure, schedule risk, and required follow-up actions"*), and Trunk Tools
gives clause/deadline extraction away free as lead-gen. (b) **Attribution is standards-blocked**: AACE RP
29R-03 §1.2(f) — schedules *"in and of themselves, do not demonstrate root causation or responsibility for
delays"* — and §1.1 exists to *"minimize the need to contend with 'black-box' or 'voodoo' analyses."* An
automated verdict hands opposing counsel a ready-made attack. (c) **It addresses the step practitioners say is
easy**: identifying extra work is not the hard part; substantiating and pricing it is.

## 5.3 The three-line positioning that all three survivors share

- **Never ask anyone to document better.** Price hours already burned, or produce an artefact already demanded.
- **Never assert a verdict.** Assert *"here is what the record supports, and here is what is missing"* — which
  is forward-looking, remediable, and survives both the AACE attack and the GC's discovery objection.
- **Never contact the owner.** The moment the software addresses the owner it stops being software and becomes
  an accusation.

## 6.2 Four design rules that follow

1. **Never per-seat.** Per-seat is the commoditised, capped, G&A-charged shape, and it also *penalises* the
   ingestion the product depends on (you need the whole project team's daily reports and email).
2. **Price the artefact, not the software.** Easyclaim and Levelset are the two proofs that a chargeable
   artefact monetises where a subscription does not.
3. **Make it job-costable.** Per-project pricing escapes the 0.26%-of-revenue cage entirely by moving spend
   from G&A to the job — and, given that 58.8% of contractors recover nothing from owners today, an
   explicitly job-costable, owner-recoverable design is genuine commercial differentiation, not a tactic.
4. **Take a value component only where attribution is defined.** A matter has a defined scope; a prevented
   write-off does not, until you have the customer's own history.

## 6.3 THE ARCHITECTURE — three SKUs, one engine

### **SKU 1 — MATTER PACK** *(targets: claims consultancy partner, DOT on-call prime, construction law firm)*
**This is the first-revenue SKU.**

| Item | Price |
|---|---|
| Per matter, up to 25,000 documents | **$7,500** |
| Per matter, 25,001–100,000 documents | **$15,000** |
| Above 100,000 documents | **$0.10/document** |
| Annual pre-buy: 10 matters | **$60,000** (20% off — this is the ARR conversion) |

**Deliverable:** linked chronology; notice and entitlement register; evidence-completeness score per event;
missing-evidence schedule; source-cited exhibit set.

**Why these numbers:** the buyer's alternative for the same work is **200–600 hours at a ~$400 blended rate =
$80,000–$240,000**. At $7,500 you are **3–9% of the thing you replace**, which is small enough to be signed by
one partner without a business case and rebilled as a disbursement, and large enough to sit clearly above the
$29–300/month prosumer band that killed the demoware cohort. It never triggers a procurement.

### **SKU 2 — PROJECT LICENCE** *(targets: heavy-civil project executive, GC project executive, specialty-trade CFO)*
**This is the contractor-side prevention SKU and the strategic one.**

| Item | Price |
|---|---|
| Per project, per month, **unlimited users**, 6-month minimum | **$1,200** |
| 5 projects | **$4,500/mo** (~$900/project) |
| 15 projects | **$11,000/mo** (~$730/project) |
| **Company-wide cap** | **$6,000/mo = $72,000/yr** |
| One-off configuration (state spec book / contract form) | **$2,500**, waived on annual commitment |

**Deliverable:** continuous ingest of daily reports, forwarded email, RFIs and schedule snapshots; commercial-
event flags; evidence-completeness score; **a drafted same-day confirming email for a human to send**; and the
jurisdiction-specific claim record — Caltrans PCR (Initial / Supplemental with itemised cost + TIA / Full and
Final), FAR REA, or a substantiated COR — with time separated from money.

**Why $1,200:** CEMAR proves **£435/licence/month (~$550)** works under a hard notice regime, and Gather's
£500 flat is the *floor* set by neutrality, not by willingness to pay. $1,200/project/month = **$14,400/yr per
project** — which is 7–11% of a $50M contractor's *entire* technology purse if charged to G&A, and therefore
impossible there, but is **0.07% of contract value on a $20M heavy-civil job** if job-costed, which is where it
must go. The **$72,000/yr company cap** is deliberately set inside the $200M contractor's $25–75K incremental
band so that a portfolio deal never becomes a board item.

**Never bill per seat on this SKU.** The unlimited-user term is not generosity; it is the precondition for the
daily-report and email ingestion that makes the product work at all.

### **SKU 3 — RECOVERY FEE** *(a rider, contractor-side only, from ~customer #10 onward — NOT in V1)*

| Item | Terms |
|---|---|
| Fee | **8% of amounts recovered** on events the system flagged *and* that fall inside the customer's own defined write-off baseline |
| Cap | **$50,000 per event** |
| Credit | 100% credited against SKU 2 fees already paid |

**Legal position:** contingency is **permitted for claim preparation and already in live use** — FTI, a
NYSE-listed firm, discloses success-fee and contingent-fee engagements in its 10-K and attributes revenue
growth to them. It is **barred for testifying experts** (CJC Guidance ¶88; *Factortame No.8*: *"a rare case
indeed"*). **The industry already separates the two roles** — in *Van Oord* the claim was built by claims
consultants who never testified, with a separate quantum expert. **That seam is where performance pricing is
legitimate.** Contract with the contractor's commercial function, never with counsel, and never let the vendor
be positioned as or contract as the expert.

**Why it is deliberately deferred:** attribution requires a baseline you do not have in month one, and
offering it early invites precisely the *"you're a GPT wrapper taking a cut"* reaction documented in raw/15 §9.
Introduce it only after twelve months of the customer's own write-off history defines "otherwise written off."

## 6.4 What NOT to price

- **No $29–300/month tier.** That band is occupied entirely by demoware with zero named customers and signals
  a prosumer toy for a six-figure consequence.
- **No per-user pricing at any tier.** It caps you at $50–150/user/mo and puts you in a DocuSign/Copilot
  comparison you lose.
- **No free tier of the *same* artefact.** The free thing must be a *different, smaller* artefact (the PCR
  Readiness Check), or you train the market to expect the chronology for nothing.
- **No annual-only enterprise contract in year one.** It forces a procurement and a security review, which is
  exactly what the founder constraint penalises.
