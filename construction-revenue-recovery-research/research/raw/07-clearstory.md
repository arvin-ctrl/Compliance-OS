## 2. EXACTLY WHAT CLEARSTORY DOES — PRODUCT SURFACE

### 2.1 Digital T&M Tags (the field wedge)
Replaces triplicate carbon-copy paper tickets. Captures **labor, material, equipment, misc.** against pre-loaded customizable rate lists; time-stamped photos bundled into a single PDF; multi-day tracking; company logo branding; internal draft review before it goes out ("anyone on your team can edit and review T&M Tags prior to submitting a perfect Tag for customer signature"); **remote digital signature — the GC does not need a Clearstory account to sign**; instant PDF to the customer; auto-logged in a real-time T&M log; one-click conversion to a COR; multiple tags combinable into one COR.
https://www.clearstory.build/digital-time-and-material-tags

### 2.2 Change Order Requests (CORs)
Standardized COR templates with auto-pricing from rate libraries; auto-generated PDF with backup attached; built-in **markup/annotation editor** for reviewing the counterparty's COR PDF; in-app comment thread on the COR itself (explicitly replacing "fragmented email communication").
https://www.clearstory.build/learn/construction-change-orders

### 2.3 The Live Shared Change Order Log (the network artifact)
One cloud log, updated in real time on both sides, **shareable by link to non-users**. Claimed **70%+ reduction in COR processing time between stakeholders**. This is the actual product: a shared, mutually-visible ledger of contested money.
https://www.clearstory.build/

### 2.4 Change Notifications / Pricing Exercises
GC (or owner) pushes a design change to trades, sets a deadline, collects cost impacts, and **tracks who received it and who responded with a cost impact**.
https://www.clearstory.build/general-contractor-software ; https://www.clearstory.build/pricing-exercises-general-contractors

### 2.5 Allowances & Contingencies (rebuilt Jun/Jul 2026)
Live balances per allowance/contingency bucket, tagged to CORs, across trades and projects; timeline view; original vs. current amounts; **allowances can now go negative** to reflect overdrawn buckets.
https://www.clearstory.build/construction-blog/july-2026-product-updates

### 2.6 Backcharges
Backcharge management is a named capability.
https://www.clearstory.build/learn/construction-change-orders

### 2.7 Analytics & Insights / Portfolio
Dashboards for aging, exposure, submitted/at-risk CORs, project health; **"Leverage our API to connect historical and real-time data to your internal dashboards."**
https://www.clearstory.build/owner-software

## 3. ★ THE CENTRAL COMPETITIVE READ: WORKFLOW ANGLE vs ENTITLEMENT ANGLE

This is the most important section of this report.

Clearstory and the thesis product attack **the same economic wound** — *the sub does the extra work and doesn't get paid for it* — but they attack **different points in the causal chain**, and the difference is not cosmetic.

### 3.1 Clearstory's model of the wound

Clearstory's theory of loss is **friction, latency and evidentiary sloppiness**, not contractual right. The whole product is built on the premise that *the money is owed, everyone basically agrees it's owed, and it leaks out through paper, delay and disorganisation.* Their own published causal chain:

- Paper tickets get lost: "Subcontractors estimate that anywhere from **2-5% of T&M Tickets go missing**." https://www.clearstory.build/construction-time-and-materials/7-biggest-problems-with-paper-tm-tags
- Processing is slow: **22 days** from signed T&M tag → priced COR submitted; **26 days** COR → signed change order; **nearly two months** total; **more than half of CORs need 2+ revision cycles**. https://www.clearstory.build/construction-blog/2026-sc-change-order-report
- Backup is missing: **53% of GCs cite insufficient backup documentation** as a reason for withholding/reducing payment. ibid.
- Therefore: digitise the tag, standardise the COR, share the log, compress the cycle → the money arrives.

Their remedies are all **process remedies**: faster signature, better photos, standardised template, shared visibility, aging alerts, ERP push.

### 3.2 The line Clearstory explicitly refuses to cross

Clearstory has *published a doctrine* on this and it draws the boundary with unusual precision. From "Why We Call Them Change Order Requests and Time and Material Tags":

> "A Time & Material Tag **only proves the work happened and the hours are correct**. You still need to submit a formal Change Order Request so the office team can price the work and adjust the contract."

> "A Change Order Request **is your proposal**. A Change Order **is the signed agreement** that comes back after the owner or GC approves the request."

https://www.clearstory.build/construction-blog/why-we-call-them-change-order-request-and-time-material-tags

Read that carefully. Clearstory positions the T&M Tag as **evidence of occurrence** (quantum-of-work verification) and the COR as **a proposal**. It is scrupulously silent on the question that actually determines whether the sub gets paid: **was the contractor *entitled* to be paid for it, and did they preserve that right?** Clearstory documents *what happened*. It does not opine on *whether you're owed for it*.

### 3.3 Where the entitlement angle lives — in the gap Clearstory's own research exposes

Clearstory's own 2026 Dodge research quantifies the residue their workflow product does **not** eliminate. This is the strongest evidence in this report, because it is *the incumbent's own data on their own customers*:

- **66% of GCs cite disputed pricing** as the primary reason for withholding or reducing payment — vs 53% citing insufficient backup. **The larger cause is disagreement, not documentation.** https://www.clearstory.build/construction-blog/2026-sc-change-order-report
- **91% of GCs report sometimes not paying the full change order amount.** https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260623740974/en/Only-1-in-3-Contractors-Say-Their-Change-Order-Process-Works-New-Research-Shows-What-the-Best-Ones-Do-Differently
- **More than half of GCs don't pay the full requested amount on 20%+ of change orders.** https://www.clearstory.build/construction-blog/2026-sc-change-order-report
- **77% of specialty contractors have written off change order work as bad debt.** ibid.
- **48% have had disputes escalate to arbitration or legal process.** ibid.
- **97% of trades begin work before official COR approval; 42% do so more than half the time.** ibid.

That last one is the entitlement bomb. **97% of subs are performing work without an executed change order** — i.e. their right to payment rests entirely on contract terms (changes clause, notice provision, waiver/no-damage-for-delay, constructive change doctrine) and on contemporaneous notice, not on a signed COR. Clearstory makes the *record* of that work beautiful. It does nothing about the *right*.

Similarly: **"more than half of all CORs require two or more revision cycles"** and **64–66% of disputes are pricing disputes**. Revision cycles and price disputes are argued on *entitlement and quantum*, not on whether the photo was time-stamped.

### 3.4 Precise characterisation of the difference

| | **Clearstory (workflow/communication)** | **Entitlement/AI angle (thesis)** |
|---|---|---|
| Theory of loss | Friction, latency, lost paper, poor backup | Unclaimed rights, missed notice, unpriced impacts, weak causation |
| Unit of work | The **T&M Tag** and the **COR** — documents that already exist | The **commercial event** — often not yet recognised by anyone |
| Trigger | A human decides "this is extra" and opens a tag | Detection: the system decides "this *was* extra" from RFIs/emails/dailies |
| Governing document | The rate table | The **contract** |
| Question answered | "Where is my COR and how old is it?" | "Am I owed, under which clause, by when must I notice, and how much?" |
| Evidence purpose | Persuade the GC to sign quickly | Survive a **dispute** — attribution, causation, quantum |
| Time dimension | Aging of a document (days outstanding) | Delay, disruption, productivity loss, schedule impact |
| Counterparty stance | **Cooperative** — both sides want the log | **Adversarial** — the GC/owner is the payer being pressed |
| Adoption gate | Both sides must adopt (network) | Single-sided; the sub can buy alone |
| Where the money is | Speed of collection on *agreed* amounts | Recovery of *contested / never-claimed* amounts |

**The sharpest way to say it:** Clearstory has industrialised the **submission** of extra-work claims. It has not touched the **adjudication** of them. It shortens the pipe; it does not increase what enters the pipe, and it does not help you win the argument at the far end. Their own data says the far end is where 77% write-offs and 48% arbitrations live.

**The counter-argument you must hold honestly:** Clearstory's *implicit* claim is that most of the loss is caused by the pipe, and that fixing the pipe is sufficient for the majority of dollars. Their Accurate Firestop case study is the strongest evidence for that: **"After introducing Clearstory, our T&M write-offs were reduced by about 66%"** from a baseline of **30% of T&M revenue previously lost**. https://www.clearstory.build/customers/accurate-firestop — If a workflow fix genuinely recovers two-thirds of a 30% leak, the entitlement layer is fighting over the residual ~10%. That is a real, uncomfortable finding for the thesis, and it is sourced from the incumbent's marketing, so treat the magnitude as promotional but the direction as credible.

### 3.5 Does Clearstory do entitlement, contracts, notice, delay, or claim packaging? — Evidence both ways

| Capability | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Entitlement analysis** | **NO** | No entitlement, "owed," or contractual-right language anywhere on product pages. Their own doctrine post explicitly separates proof-of-work from contract adjustment. https://www.clearstory.build/construction-blog/why-we-call-them-change-order-request-and-time-material-tags |
| **Contract review / clause extraction** | **NO** | Product pages and AI page contain no contract-analysis capability. CMiC integration syncs *contract metadata* (contract, budget, vendor records) — not contract documents. https://www.clearstory.build/clearstory-ai ; https://www.clearstory.build/construction-blog/july-2026-product-updates |
| **Notice deadlines (contractual)** | **NO** | Nothing on notice provisions, cure periods, or time-bars. Their "Change Notifications" feature is a GC→trade *broadcast of a design change with a response deadline*, which is a workflow deadline, not a contractual notice clock. https://www.clearstory.build/general-contractor-software |
| **Delay / schedule impact** | **NO** | No schedule integration (no P6, no MSP), no time-impact analysis, no productivity-loss quantification anywhere in the surveyed surface. Integration list contains zero scheduling tools. https://www.clearstory.build/integrations |
| **Evidence packaging for claims** | **PARTIAL — and this is the nuance** | They generate a **COR PDF with all backup bundled** (time-stamped photos, signed tags, rates), and the **COR Review Agent "confirms backup documentation."** That is a genuine, high-quality *contemporaneous evidence artifact* — arguably the best in the industry — but it is packaged to *get a signature*, not to prove a claim. No causation narrative, no clause citation, no chronology, no quantum build-up. https://www.clearstory.build/digital-time-and-material-tags ; https://www.clearstory.build/clearstory-ai |
| **Blog/legal content on claims** | **NO** | Full blog index scan: content is operational (logs, backcharges, COR delays, AI features, research). **No posts on contract clauses, notice requirements, dispute resolution, claims, delay entitlement, or legal liability.** https://www.clearstory.build/construction-blog |

**Read on intent:** this is *deliberate*, not an oversight. A three-sided network whose value depends on GCs and owners voluntarily joining **cannot** ship a product that helps subs build claims against GCs and owners. The moment Clearstory adds "we found you a claim against Turner," Turner stops being a customer. **Their network is the reason they cannot cross this line** — see §8.

**But four things sharply limit foreclosure:**

1. **The network is deliberately bypassable — by design.** T&M Tags can be signed by a GC **with no Clearstory account**, and COR logs are **shareable by link to non-users**. https://www.clearstory.build/digital-time-and-material-tags ; https://www.clearstory.build/subcontractor-software — and the sub page states the log works "whether the GC uses Clearstory or not." 2. **A contractor sits on many networks simultaneously.** Subs already run Procore (GC-mandated), Siteline, Flashtract, GCPay and an ERP concurrently. Adding one more tool that serves a *different job* (entitlement) is normal behaviour, not a displacement fight.

Everything upstream — RFIs, emails, dailies, minutes, schedule — is outside it. **A new entrant competing on *detection* is not competing for the same data or the same moment.**
4. **Neutrality is a structural cage.** The network is Clearstory's asset *and* its constraint. Every incremental capability must be acceptable to GCs and owners. Entitlement advocacy is not.

**Verdict: the network forecloses the *COR-workflow* wedge almost completely — do not build a T&M tag app.** It does **not** foreclose an entitlement/detection wedge, and it arguably *creates* one, because Clearstory has spent 8 years training the industry that extra-work money is a tracked, quantified, board-level number — while remaining constitutionally unable to fight for it.

## 11. ADJACENCY TEST — HOW HARD FOR CLEARSTORY TO SHIP "event detection → entitlement matching → evidence → claim package"?

### **VERDICT: HARD** (specifically: technically MEDIUM, strategically HARD)

**Data access — MEDIUM/EASY.** They already hold the richest contemporaneous extra-work dataset in US commercial construction: $2.1B/month of CORs, both sides of the exchange, time-stamped photos, signatures, and AI-classified reason codes. They have Procore, Autodesk, CMiC, Vista and Sage pipes. They have Gemini in production across six agents. **They could technically build entitlement matching faster than anyone.** What they lack: contracts (they never ingest them), schedules (zero scheduling integrations), and the upstream event streams (RFIs, email, dailies).

**Org incentive — HARD. This is the binding constraint.** Their business model requires GCs and owners to *want* to be on the network. 13 of the top 25 GCs are customers. Owners are a named ICP. Shipping "we detected a $400K claim you have against your GC" converts their two biggest customer segments into defendants. **They have already published the doctrine that keeps them on the safe side of the line** ("A T&M Tag only proves the work happened... You still need to submit a formal Change Order Request"), and their blog has zero legal/claims content across its full index. That is not an accident.

**GTM motion — MEDIUM/HARD.** They sell an operational-efficiency SaaS to PMs and ops leaders (self-serve free tier, per-seat, bottom-up viral). Entitlement/claims sells to legal, risk, contracts and executives on a value/risk narrative with different buyers, cycles and proof burden. Their 2026 Dodge research shows they *understand* the risk narrative — but their motion is "ease of use" (their own data: **96% cite ease of use as the top adoption factor**).

**Legal exposure appetite — HARD.** Nothing observed suggests appetite. Their AI governance stance is conspicuously conservative: **"Clearstory AI never acts without human review."** A product that asserts entitlement carries advice-adjacent liability they have shown no interest in taking.

**Past M&A and shipping behaviour — MEDIUM.** No acquisitions found (`UNVERIFIED` — no M&A activity located). Shipping cadence is strong and disciplined but strictly *in-lane*: every 2025–2026 release (Allowances rebuild, six AI agents, Change Notification redesign, Procore Project Import, CMiC contract mapping) deepens the existing COR workflow. **Zero lateral moves toward contract, schedule, or claims in three years of observable roadmap.** They have expanded *sideways across stakeholders* (sub → GC → owner), not *forward into adjudication*.

**Most likely actual path for them:** benchmarking and "change order intelligence" — using AI Reason Categories across $2.1B/month to tell a GC "your CORs run 18% above network median for this trade." That is monetisable, network-defensible, and **neutral**. Watch for it. It is *not* entitlement.

## 16. ANSWERS TO THE THREE KEY QUESTIONS

**(1) Has the "sub doesn't get paid for extra work" wound already been claimed by a workflow product with a network?**
**Yes — the *documentation and submission* half of it, decisively.** Clearstory owns the field-to-COR pipeline with a compounding three-sided network ($2.1B/mo, 14k+ contractors, 13 of the top 25 GCs, free tier for the other side). **No — the *entitlement and adjudication* half remains unclaimed.** Clearstory's own 2026 research documents that after all their process improvement, 77% of subs still write off change order work, 91% of GCs still short-pay, 66% of non-payment is pricing dispute, and 48% of subs still end up in arbitration. They have claimed the wound's *symptom management*; the *cause* is open.

**(2) Is the AI/entitlement angle a genuine upgrade, or a worse solution to a solved problem?**
**A genuine upgrade — but only if it is positioned strictly downstream of the workflow, never as a replacement for it.** Three tests:
- *Is the problem solved?* No. Their own data says the biggest cause of non-payment (66%, disputed pricing) is untouched by faster tags.
- *Does the entitlement layer need what workflow provides?* Yes — and Clearstory conveniently produces it. Entitlement without contemporaneous evidence is unpersuasive; Clearstory has industrialised the evidence.
- *Can the workflow incumbent absorb the entitlement layer?* **No — structurally barred by network neutrality.** This is the crux.
**Honest counterweight:** the Accurate Firestop numbers (30% of T&M revenue lost → 66% of that recovered by workflow alone) suggest the workflow layer may capture the majority of the recoverable dollars, leaving the entitlement layer fighting for a smaller residual than the thesis assumes. The entitlement wedge must therefore justify itself on **dollar magnitude per event** (large contested/never-claimed items, delay and disruption) rather than on volume of small T&M tickets — the latter is Clearstory's ground and it is well held.

**(3) Partner, channel, or roadkill?**
**PARTNER.** Roadkill only if you build a COR workflow or T&M tag product — do not. As an entitlement/detection layer consuming their output, the relationship is stable and non-competitive by construction, because their network economics permanently forbid them from advocating for one side. Channel potential is real but secondary and only under neutral framing.

## 17. THE THESIS-THREATENING FINDING

**It is not Clearstory. It is Document Crunch.**

Clearstory turned out to be a clean partner: they own evidence, they cannot own entitlement, and their research funds the thesis's own sales narrative. But **Document Crunch already does contract clause extraction, obligation tracking, and generates notices and RFIs — for 500+ companies, with logo overlap into the same ENR contractors** (https://www.documentcrunch.com/). That is the *upper half* of the thesis pipeline, already shipped, already distributed, already trusted, and with **no neutrality constraint** stopping it from moving downstream into event detection and claim packaging.

The thesis assumed the contest was with workflow tools. **The contest is with contract-AI tools moving downstream.** Clearstory cannot come up the stack. Document Crunch can come down it. Re-prioritise accordingly.
