## 1. SNAPSHOT — WHAT THIS INDUSTRY IS

**What it is.** A professional-services industry that sells human hours to reconstruct, argue, quantify and
defend construction claims *after* a commercial event has already gone wrong. Three products are sold:

1. **Delay / programme analysis** — forensic reconstruction of what delayed the works and who owns it.
2. **Quantum** — pricing the delay, disruption, prolongation and variation entitlement.
3. **Expert witness / testimony** — an independent, court- or tribunal-facing opinion on 1 and 2.

Around these sit smaller lines: contract advisory, dispute avoidance, claims governance, eDiscovery,
project controls, and (rarely) drafting notices.

**Who they sell to.** Overwhelmingly *law firms* and *the legal/commercial function of contractors and
owners* — not the site team. HKA states it "works with law firms, contractors, owners, operators, and other
professional service providers" ([CRUX 7th annual report, p.67, Sep 2024](https://www.hka.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/CRUX-Insight-2024-Changing-the-Narrative.pdf)).
FTI's Forensic & Litigation Consulting segment "provides law firms, companies, boards of directors,
government entities, private equity firms and other interested parties…"
([FTI FY2025 10-K, filed 2026-02-26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/887936/000088793626000013/fcn-20251231.htm)).
**The buyer is the lawyer or the commercial director, and the trigger is a dispute, not a project.**

## 2. THE ANNUAL DISPUTE REPORTS — THE HARD DATA (this is the core of this report)

### 2.1 Arcadis Construction Disputes Report — the value/duration time series

Arcadis has published this annually since 2011. **Critical caveat: from the 14th edition (2024) onward the
report is North-America-only, and the 16th edition (2026) states "Only input from the United States was
included in the 2026 report."** The last genuinely *global* edition with regional splits was the 2022 report
(2021 data). Anyone quoting "global average dispute value" from a post-2023 Arcadis edition is wrong.

- **Average dispute length 2025: 12.2 months** (report also states 12.16) — down **2.40%** from 2024.
- Highest single dispute reported by respondents: **US$100 million**.
- **57.9%** of respondents saw more disputes in 2025; **75%** expect more again in 2026.
- **54%** participated in ADR in the past year; **57%** are beginning to incorporate AI into project management.
- Full value time series (US$m, North America / US): **2013 $34.3 | 2014 $29.6 | 2015 $25.0 | 2016 $21.0 |

  followed closely by owner-directed changes and differing site conditions.

  reviews; 3. Risk management.
- **Most important factors in mitigation/early resolution, 2025:** 1. Transparent communication and willingness
  to compromise; 2. **Accurate and timely schedules and reviews by project staff or third parties**;
  3. **Contractor transparency of cost data in support of claimed damages**.
- On technology: "AI assisted eDiscovery and large language model review compress weeks of document analysis
  into days, **cutting costs by as much as 85%**"; and the ICDR "has introduced an AI powered arbitrator for

**Methodology caveat (important):** Arcadis's data comes from *its own Contract Solutions caseload plus an
industry survey of its network*. It is a self-selected sample of disputes that reached Arcadis. It is not a
census. The 2026 report's methodology section is two sentences long.

### 2.3 Turner & Townsend, RLB, Driver Trett/Diales Digest — what these actually are

- **Turner & Townsend** publishes the *International Construction Market Survey* (cost/labour inflation, crane
  and tender price indices). **It does not publish a disputes report.** Its relevance here is as a cost-consultant

**Conclusion on the data landscape: there are exactly TWO serious annual datasets — Arcadis (value + duration

in the "construction disputes report" genre is derivative or is a cost/market index.**

## 3. HOW THE WORK IS ACTUALLY DELIVERED

### 3.1 When the consultant is hired

**Almost always after the fact.** The structural evidence:

- CRUX's inclusion rule — a project enters the dataset once HKA has spent **>30 hours** on a **claim or dispute**
  ([7th annual, Methodology](https://www.hka.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/CRUX-Insight-2024-Changing-the-Narrative.pdf)).
  HKA's entire flagship research asset is built from *post-event* engagements.
- Arcadis structures Contract Solutions into three buckets — **Dispute Avoidance / Dispute Mitigation /
  Dispute Resolution** — but the avoidance offering is described as "schedule reviews, risk analysis, value
  management, and cost estimating", i.e. pre-construction consulting, not live commercial-event monitoring
  ([Arcadis Contract Solutions](https://www.arcadis.com/en-us/expertise/contract-solutions)).
- HKA markets **Claims Governance** ("proactive frameworks designed to identify emerging risks early…
  standardize monitoring processes across portfolios") and **Dispute Avoidance**
  ([hka.com/services/claims-dispute-management](https://www.hka.com/services/claims-dispute-management/)) —
  but these are minority lines and are sold to portfolio owners, not to the contractor's project team.

**I could not find any published number for the prevention-vs-cure split of consultancy revenue.**
`UNVERIFIED` — no firm discloses it, and it is not in any of the three annual reports. The best available
proxy is structural: every dataset the industry publishes is built from disputes. If early advisory were a
large share of revenue, someone would publish an "avoided disputes" dataset. Nobody does.

**Practical timing pattern (from the primary documents, not from a survey):** the HKA case study in the 7th
annual CRUX report describes the canonical engagement — *contract review to establish liability → review of
the project team's preliminary report → SWOT/gap analysis → interim claim submission → stage two:
documentation review → delay analysis → quantum analysis → detailed claim package*. That sequence begins
after the loss has been suffered.

### 3.2 The workflow, in the order it is actually done

1. **Instruction** — usually via the law firm; conflict check; scoping call.
2. **Document harvest** — the client dumps everything: contract + amendments, P6 baselines and updates,
   daily reports, RFI/submittal logs, minutes, correspondence, PST email archives, cost ledgers, photos.
3. **eDiscovery / document reduction** — HKA runs **Nuix Neo Discover** with **Continuous Active Learning**
   and **CognitiveAI privilege detection**, plus sentiment analysis, thread reconstruction, near-duplicate
   analysis and conceptual clustering ([hka.com/services/ediscovery](https://www.hka.com/services/ediscovery/)).
   Note: not Relativity, at HKA.
4. **Chronology build** — the labour black hole. Consultants build a contemporaneous narrative linking events
   → notices → instructions → schedule impacts → costs. **This is still substantially a manual, analyst-hours
   exercise.** No firm markets a product that does it; every firm markets *people* who do it.
5. **Delay analysis** — BRG lists the methods it uses: **time impact analysis; as-planned versus as-built;
   time slice / windows analysis; half-step analysis; collapsed as-built**
   ([BRG Schedule & Delay brochure](https://media.thinkbrg.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/26150305/BRG_Delay_Construction_2021.pdf)).
   Method choice is driven by "the circumstances of the dispute, **documentation available**, and contractual
   requirements" — i.e. **record quality determines which analysis is even possible**.
6. **Quantum** — prolongation, disruption, loss of productivity (measured mile / discrete damages /
   modified total cost), variations, escalation, finance costs.
7. **Report** — claim submission, or a CPR-35 / IBA-compliant expert report.
8. **Testimony** — adjudication, DAB, arbitration, litigation.

- **HKA Tech** ([hka-tech.com](https://www.hka-tech.com/)) — implements enterprise software for capital

  but proof that a large built-asset consultancy will stand up a real software P&L.

**They are STRUCTURALLY THREATENED at the bottom of the pyramid, not the top.** The economics say so:
- FTI FLC utilisation is **57%** and Exponent's is **73%**; Diales' is **71.6%**. These firms sell hours.

entirely — but that resistance matters little, because the client who needs a testifying expert will still
hire one. **A startup that reduces the 400 analyst hours before the expert opens the file is selling to the
consultant. A startup that catches the event 18 months earlier is selling around them, to a different buyer,
at a different price.**

## 7. IS THERE EVIDENCE THAT CLAIMS ARE LOST OR ABANDONED FOR POOR RECORDS / MISSED NOTICE?

**This is the thesis-critical question. Here is the honest answer, in three parts.**

### 7a. There is NO published industry-wide quantification. Say this plainly.

I searched the three annual dispute reports, the consultancies' own research, academic indexes (Crossref),
and the funders' research. **No source publishes "X% of construction claims fail for want of contemporaneous
records" or "$Y of entitlement is written off annually for missed notice."** If someone quotes such a number
at you, ask for the source; it almost certainly does not have one.

### 7b. The best *aggregate* proxies that DO exist

### 7c. The strongest evidence that exists: a court judgment where it happened, with numbers

**Van Oord UK Ltd & Sicim Roadbridge Ltd v Allseas UK Ltd [2015] EWHC 3074 (TCC), Coulson J, 12 Nov 2015**
([full judgment](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/tcc/2015/3074))

This is the single best-documented instance in the common-law world of a large construction claim failing
*specifically* because of contemporaneous records and notice. The facts:

- **Claim value: ~£10 million** at the start of trial ("itself a reduction from the original sums sought"),
  reduced to ~£8m by closing.
- **Outcome: the claims failed in their entirety.** Not only did the contractor recover nothing on Claims 1,
  3 and 4, it was ordered to **repay £1,895,349.89** in respect of Claims 1 and 3, plus a **minimum of
  £588,882.98** on Claim 2. The only success was £333,226.52 on two line items relating to proximity agreements.
- **Records.** The claim collapsed on the Daily Progress Reports:
  > "That the reports and other contemporaneous documents in this case make so few references to standing time
  > or disruption, and the fact that detailed claims were not made in the large amounts now advanced until
  > months, even years, after the period in question, are plainly factors undermining the credibility of OSR's
  > claims in these proceedings." (¶54)

  > "…the contemporaneous documents do not emphasise to any degree the matters upon which OSR now seek to rely.
  > In these proceedings OSR were claiming £10 million (now £8 million)… And yet there is little indication in
  > the contemporaneous documents that, at the time, OSR put any great emphasis on these matters… they
  > comprehensively fail that test." (¶52)

  > "…unless a disputed factual element of the claim is unequivocally supported by contemporaneous material
  > and/or the evidence of the AUK witnesses and/or the expert evidence, it will be most unlikely to find
  > favour with the court." (¶71)

- **Notice.** The contract required notice within 5 days and financial information within 7:
  > "I consider that… AUK are right, and that OSR failed to give timely or adequate notice." (¶175)
  Every notice relied on was **out of time** or was a notice under the wrong clause (¶176–181).
- **The claims consultant's product failed at trial.** The claim "had been prepared by **Dal Sterling, claims
  consultants who did not give evidence**" (¶83). The claimant's quantum expert:
  > "he prepared his report by only looking at the witness statements prepared on behalf of OSR… In some
  > instances, this process culminated in Mr Lester cutting and pasting controversial parts of the OSR
  > statements into his report as if they were in some way a contemporaneous record of events. His report and
  > his evidence were therefore inevitably biased in favour of OSR." (¶82)
  > "…the OSR claim… was potentially a 'try-on', relying as it did on calculated rates and all manner of
  > assumptions…" (¶84)

**The mirror-image case: Walter Lilly & Co Ltd v Mackay [2012] EWHC 1773 (TCC)**
([judgment](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/tcc/2012/1773)) — the contractor **won** a large
loss-and-expense claim precisely *because* it had resource-level contemporaneous records:
> "…detailed analysis which pick up on allocations of time for staff and resources at particular times and
> applies to such allocations costs obtained from WLC's 'COINS' computerised record keeping system. This was
> all supported by reliable evidence from WLC's witnesses… **What WLC has produced is not on analysis any
> global or total cost claim. It has sought to identify the specific additional or extended resources and to
> link them to the events**…" (¶490–491)

**Same court. Same judgeship tier. Opposite outcomes. The difference is whether the events were linked to
resources and costs in contemporaneous records.** That is the thesis, evidenced, with money attached.

### 7d. The practitioner literature says the same thing, qualitatively

Long International, "The Impact of Poor Contemporaneous Project Records on Claims Preparation and Expert
Analyses" (Downing, Avalon PE PSP, Foster) —
[link](https://www.long-intl.com/articles/contemporaneous-project-records/):
> "The effects of poor record keeping on the claims preparation effort and related expert analyses can be
> profound, both during project execution… and after project completion when the contractor submits a formal claim."
> "**Claims founded on demonstrably unreliable records are far more easily discredited.**"
> "Owners typically react with significant skepticism to such lack of transparency and **routinely reject such
> poorly prepared claims** for failure to meet the contract requirements."
> "Poor record keeping also makes the expert's job much more difficult, which in turn **can increase the cost
> of preparing and/or defending claims**."
> "In the author's 20 plus years of experience analyzing labor productivity on dozens of process industrial
> projects, **there has not been a single instance where job conditions or project records would allow the
> proper 'textbook' use of the Measured Mile Method**."

Long International, "The Importance and Value of 'Notice' Provisions" (Michael Harris PMP FCIArb LLM) —
[link](https://www.long-intl.com/articles/notice-provisions/):
> "If a contractor fails to comply with the notice provisions, **the worst-case scenario under a strict
> application is that the claim will be thrown out irrespective of proof of any merit**."
Under the "no-prejudice rule", "the owner does not have to prove prejudice… to deny the contractor's claim."

**Both articles are explicit that they contain no quantified data.** The industry's own experts describe the
mechanism precisely and quantify it not at all.

## 8. LITIGATION FUNDING IN CONSTRUCTION — AND WHAT IT IMPLIES FOR PERFORMANCE PRICING

**It exists and is mainstream at the large end.**

- **Burford Capital** (NYSE: BUR) runs a construction & real-estate campaign and publishes construction
  arbitration content, including "Building the future: A roundtable on construction arbitration"
  (29 Jan 2026, Christiane Deniger & Joe Durkin) and "Shifting landscape of construction arbitration"
  (Burford Quarterly 2025 Issue 4). Its 2024 industry research reports **85% of construction company lawyers
  have considered funding for legal fees and expenses**, from a GLG survey of **400 senior financial officers
  and in-house lawyers**. Burford also cites **208 funded arbitrations** in the 2023 GAR 100 survey and an
  average arbitration duration of **2.5–4.5 years** (ICC/ICSID data).
  ([Burford construction & real estate research](https://www.burfordcapital.com/2024-industry-research/construction-real-estate/))
- **Omni Bridgeway** (ASX: OBL) funds arbitration. Its published screening criteria include **"anticipated
  awards exceeding 10x the requested funding amount"**, "strong prospects of… successful results", and "clear
  ability for the opposing party to pay". Terms are described as bespoke; **no published percentage of proceeds**.
  ([Omni Bridgeway arbitration financing](https://omnibridgeway.com/litigation-funding/arbitration-financing))

**What this implies for a startup's pricing.** Positive but bounded:
1. **Third-party capital already prices construction claim outcomes.** The concept "someone gets paid a share
   of a recovered construction entitlement" is established and legal in the major seats.
2. **But the 10x screen is brutal.** Funders want a claim ~10x their spend. That excludes almost everything
   under ~$5m — i.e. **~40% of US disputes by count** are below the funders' floor, exactly where the
   incumbent consultancy is also uneconomic. **That is a real, double-vacated market segment.**
3. **The funder's cost of capital is the ceiling on any performance-priced product.** If a funder takes
   30–40% of proceeds for bearing full downside, a software product bearing no downside cannot credibly ask
   for more than low single-digit percentages.

## 9. DOES CONTINGENCY / SUCCESS-FEE PRICING EXIST HERE, LEGALLY AND PRACTICALLY?

**Split the answer in two. This distinction is the single most commercially important legal fact in this report.**

### 9a. TESTIFYING EXPERTS — effectively barred

**England & Wales.** Civil Justice Council, *Guidance for the Instruction of Experts in Civil Claims*
(Aug 2014, amended Dec 2014), **paragraph 88**
([PDF](https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2014/08/experts-guidance-cjc-aug-2014-amended-dec-8.pdf)):
> "**Payment of experts' fees contingent upon the nature of the expert evidence or upon the outcome of the case
> is strongly discouraged.** In *ex parte Factortame (no8)* [2003] QB 381 at [73], the court said 'we consider
> that it will be a rare case indeed that the court will be prepared to consent to an expert being instructed
> under a contingency fee agreement'."

Paragraph 90 adds that "a professional instructing an expert, or an expert, may be subject to sanction for
misconduct by their professional body/regulator."

**United States.** The same rule operates through professional-conduct and admissibility routes rather than a
single statute; contingent expert-witness compensation is widely treated as contrary to public policy and
grounds for exclusion or disqualification. *(I did not obtain a primary US source in this session —
`UNVERIFIED` as to the precise citation; do not rely on my characterisation of US law without checking.)*

**Practical consequence:** the person who signs the expert report cannot be paid on outcome. **This is a hard
ceiling on performance pricing for anything positioned as expert evidence.**

### 9b. NON-TESTIFYING CLAIMS CONSULTANCY AND SOFTWARE — permitted and already used

- FTI, a NYSE-listed firm, discloses in its 10-K that engagements "may also contain **success fees or
  performance-based arrangements** in which our fees are based on the attainment of contractually defined
  objectives with our client", and separately warns about "**contingent fee or success fee assignments**"
  and clients shifting "revenue-generating potential to back-end contingent and success fee arrangements"
  ([FTI FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/887936/000088793626000013/fcn-20251231.htm)).
  FTI attributes part of its 2025 Corporate Finance revenue growth to "an increase in success fees".
- Litigation funders take a share of construction arbitration proceeds (§8) — the outcome-linked economics
  are already accepted in this market.
- Van Oord itself illustrates the market structure: the claim was **prepared by claims consultants (Dal Sterling)
  who never gave evidence**, with a *separate* testifying expert. **The industry already separates the
  claim-builder from the witness.** That separation is exactly the seam a performance-priced software product
  would occupy.

**Verdict on Q4: YES, performance pricing is legally and practically available — but only on the
claim-preparation / entitlement-identification side, and it must be firewalled from anything that becomes
expert evidence.** A product that says "we find the entitlement and build the evidence pack; a separate
independent expert opines" can price on outcome. A product that says "we are the expert" cannot.

## 12. THE FOUR KEY QUESTIONS, ANSWERED DIRECTLY

### Q1 — Are claims consultants the incumbent, and are they beatable by software or complementary to it?

**Yes, they are the incumbent — and the honest answer is: complementary at the top of the market, beatable at
the bottom, and displaceable in the middle only by changing the buyer.**

They are the incumbent because there is no other paid substitute. When a contractor needs to know whether it
has entitlement, what it is worth, and how to prove it, it hires one of these firms. HKA alone runs ~2,000
engagements a year across 6,500 clients ([hka.com/about-us](https://www.hka.com/about-us/)). Their capability
scores in §10 are 3s across the entire recovery pipeline. This is not an under-served problem — it is an
**expensively-served, late-served problem**.

**Where software cannot beat them:** the testifying expert. A tribunal needs a cross-examinable human.
CJC ¶88 blocks outcome-linked payment for that human. HKA has 500+ of them; Diales has 51 of 180 fee earners.
That top layer is a durable moat.

**Where software beats them decisively:**
- **The sub-$5m dispute.** ~39% of US disputes by count in 2025, ~73% under $25m
  ([Arcadis 16th annual](https://media.arcadis.com/-/media/project/arcadiscom/com/expertise/global/contract-solutions/2026/2026_construction-disputes-report.pdf)).
  At a $400/hr blended pyramid with a 30-hour engagement floor, most of these are unservable. Litigation
  funders won't touch them either (10x screen). **Structurally abandoned.**
- **The pre-dispute window.** By construction, every consultancy engagement begins after a loss. Nobody sells
  continuous commercial-event detection. That is not an oversight — it is incompatible with the billing model.
- **The chronology.** The single most labour-intensive step, with no product anywhere in the industry.

**Where they are complementary:** the analyst layer. Arcadis is already telling its own clients that
AI-assisted review cuts document analysis "by as much as 85%". A tool that turns 400 chronology hours into 40
is something a consultancy will buy and rebill. **That is a real, near-term channel — but it is a
sell-to-the-competitor motion with all the attendant risk.**

### Q2 — What does a full delay/quantum claim cost to prepare, in dollars and hours?

**No firm publishes this. Nobody in the industry publishes it. Here is a defensible range built from sourced
inputs, with the method fully exposed so it can be attacked.**

**Sourced rate inputs:**
- Exponent published band: **$225–$1,375/hr** (10-K FY2025)
- Exponent blended realised: **~$396/hr** `DERIVED` ($582.0m ÷ 1,468,000 hrs)
- FTI FLC average billable rate: **$442/hr** (10-K FY2025), up 13.3% YoY
- FTI Economic Consulting: **$583/hr**
- Diales blended realised: **~£165–£200/hr** `DERIVED`
- CRUX engagement floor for dataset inclusion: **>30 hours**

**Sourced total-cost anchor:** the employer in *Walter Lilly* stated in a contemporaneous email that he had
"spent £750,000 on Knowles" — a claims consultancy — on a **single high-end residential project**
([¶71](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/tcc/2012/1773)). That is a real number from a real
project, in a court record, for one party's claims-consultancy spend on one dispute. (2007–08 money.)

**`DERIVED` modelled ranges** — my estimate of consultant hours by dispute size, priced at a $400/hr blended
US rate. **This is a model, not a citation. Do not present it as sourced.**

| Dispute size | Delay hrs | Quantum hrs | Doc review + chronology hrs | Report/review hrs | Total hrs | Cost @ $400/hr |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **< $2m** (subcontract / single-event) | 40–80 | 40–100 | 60–150 | 30–60 | **170–390** | **$70k–$155k** |
| **$5–25m** (the modal dispute) | 150–400 | 150–400 | 200–600 | 100–250 | **600–1,650** | **$240k–$660k** |
| **$25–100m** (large, arbitrated) | 400–1,200 | 400–1,200 | 600–2,000 | 250–700 | **1,650–5,100** | **$660k–$2.0m** |
| **>$100m megaproject** (delay + quantum + technical + testimony) | — | — | — | — | **5,000–20,000+** | **$2m–$8m+** |

**Cross-checks on the model:**
- Against the *Walter Lilly* £750k anchor: the modelled $660k–$2.0m band for a large arbitrated dispute
  brackets it. Plausible.
- Against Arcadis's **$56.0m average US dispute value (2025)**: a $660k–$2.0m consultancy spend is
  **1.2%–3.6% of the disputed sum**. That is consistent with the industry's informal rule of thumb of
  "a few percent of claim value" — though **I could not verify that rule of thumb from any citable source**
  (`UNVERIFIED`), so I derive it rather than assert it.
- Against the CRUX >30-hour inclusion floor: even the smallest engagements clear 30 hours easily, which is
  consistent with a 170-hour bottom of my range.
- Legal fees typically sit **on top** of these figures and are usually the larger number; QMUL respondents
  named cost minimisation as the top driver (65%) for choosing expedited procedures.

**The headline to carry forward: a properly-evidenced delay-and-quantum claim on a typical $5–25m dispute
costs roughly $240k–$660k and 600–1,650 consultant hours, of which the single largest block —
200–600 hours — is document review and chronology construction.** That chronology block is the target.

### Q3 — Is there evidence that claims are lost or abandoned due to poor records or missed notice?

**Direct industry-wide quantification: NO. It does not exist. State this plainly and stop looking for it.**

**What does exist, in descending order of strength:**

1. **A court judgment where it happened, with the money on the page.** Van Oord v Allseas: a **~£10m claim
   reduced to ~£8m and then lost entirely**, with the contractor ordered to **repay £1,895,349.89 + £588,882.98**.
   Coulson J found (a) the Daily Progress Reports "make so few references to standing time or disruption" that
   they undermined credibility, and (b) "OSR failed to give timely or adequate notice" — every notice was out
   of time or under the wrong clause. **This is one case, not a statistic — but it is a complete,
   quantified, judicially-tested instance of exactly the failure mode the thesis names.**
2. **The mirror case.** Walter Lilly won a large loss-and-expense claim because it linked "specific additional
   or extended resources… to the events" using its COINS record-keeping system, and explicitly avoided a
   global claim. **Records determined the outcome in both directions.**
3. **The last global Arcadis edition ranked "poorly drafted or incomplete and unsubstantiated claims" as the
   #1 cause of construction disputes worldwide (2021 data).** This is the strongest *aggregate* published
   statement in existence that bad claim evidence drives dispute outcomes — but note it measures *dispute
   causation*, not *claim failure rates*.
4. **CRUX's "Claims were spurious" category: 13.6% of 1,801 projects** (6th annual). The closest thing to a
   percentage of claims that are unsustainable — but "spurious" conflates opportunism with poor evidence.
5. **CRUX's contract management/administration failure: 19.5% → 18.0% → <9% post-2020.** The category that
   contains notice and records failures, but is not decomposed.
6. **Practitioner literature is unanimous and unquantified.** Long International: claims on unreliable records
   are "far more easily discredited" and are "routinely reject[ed]"; missed notice means "the claim will be
   thrown out irrespective of proof of any merit". **Both articles explicitly contain no numbers.**

**What would settle it, and what to do about it:** the answer sits inside HKA's CRUX questionnaire (39 causes,
2,200+ projects) and inside adjudicator/arbitrator award databases. Nobody has published a cut of "claim
outcome vs. record quality". **A startup that builds this dataset — even from 50 real matters — would own the
only quantified answer in the industry and could publish it as the wedge.** That is a genuine, defensible
content moat, and it is currently unoccupied.

### Q4 — Does contingency / success-fee pricing exist in this market, legally and practically?

**Yes for claim preparation. No for expert testimony. The split is the product design constraint.**

- **Barred for testifying experts:** CJC *Guidance for the Instruction of Experts in Civil Claims* ¶88 —
  contingent expert fees are "strongly discouraged"; *Factortame (No.8)* — "a rare case indeed".
  Professional-body sanction is available (¶90).
- **Permitted and in live use for consulting work:** FTI, a NYSE-listed firm, discloses success-fee and
  contingent-fee engagements in its 10-K risk factors and attributes revenue growth to them.
- **Third-party capital already prices these outcomes:** Burford and Omni Bridgeway fund construction
  arbitration; Omni's screen is a 10x award-to-funding ratio.
- **The market already separates the roles:** in Van Oord the claim was built by claims consultants (Dal
  Sterling) who never testified, with a separate quantum expert. **That seam is where performance pricing is
  legal.**

**Design rule for the founder:** price on outcome for *entitlement identification and evidence assembly*;
never let the product or its vendor be positioned as, or contract as, the expert witness. If the product's
outputs will be exhibited to an expert report, the vendor's fee arrangement becomes a disclosable and
attackable fact — so keep the pricing arrangement with the contractor's commercial function, not with counsel.

## 13. ADJACENCY TEST — how hard for THEM to ship "event detection → entitlement matching → evidence → claim package"?

**Verdict: MEDIUM to build the technology, HARD to actually ship it. Net: HARD.**

| Axis | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Data access** | **MEDIUM-HARD.** They have the deepest domain data in the world (CRUX: 2,200 projects, 39 coded causes) but **zero live project-system access**. Their data arrives as post-hoc exports from counsel, under privilege, matter by matter, with client confidentiality that forbids pooling. They cannot legally build a cross-client training corpus from engagement documents. |
| **Org incentive** | **HARD.** This is the binding constraint. FTI FLC bills at **$442/hr with 57% utilisation**; Diales runs a **3.3% underlying operating margin on £43m**. Partnerships allocate profit per fee-earning hour. A product that removes 400 chronology hours from a $600k engagement destroys ~$160k of revenue and returns, at best, a licence fee an order of magnitude smaller. **Nobody's compensation plan rewards that.** |
| **GTM motion** | **HARD.** They sell partner-to-partner into law firms on a matter-by-matter basis. Selling a subscription to a contractor's commercial director is a different buyer, cycle, contract and support model. Arcadis Gen and HKA Tech show it *can* be stood up — but as a **separate entity**, which is itself the tell. |
| **Legal exposure appetite** | **HARD.** Their entire brand is independence. A product that tells a contractor "you have $2.4m of entitlement" is advocacy, and it contaminates the firm's ability to serve as expert on the same matter. Conflicts management alone would kill most deals. |
| **Past M&A / shipping behaviour** | **MEDIUM.** They buy relentlessly — HKA acquired The Kenrich Group (2019), Probyn Miers (2020), Robert Breeze (2021), TM Financial Forensics (2022), Credibility International, ASQ, GMR Aviation (all 2023); J.S. **Why HARD is the right verdict despite MEDIUM technical capability:** they have had the data, the domain

BRG, Kroll) or publicly listed (FTI, Exponent, CRA, Arcadis, Diales) with quarterly utilisation to defend.

## 14. STARTUP POSTURE — PARTNER, CHANNEL, or ROADKILL?

**CHANNEL first, PARTNER structurally, and never ROADKILL.**

**Not ROADKILL.** They cannot kill a startup here because they cannot ship the competing product without
cannibalising themselves, and they cannot acquire-and-kill cheaply because their M&A muscle is tuned to
buying expert teams, not software. Nine years of roll-up with zero claims-software acquisitions is strong
revealed preference.

**CHANNEL is the fastest revenue, and it fits the solo-founder constraint.** The long tail of small and mid-sized claims
consultancies, plus the analyst layer of the big firms, are all doing 200–600 hours of chronology construction
per matter, by hand, on document sets that arrive as file dumps. **This is precisely a file-upload V1.**
No Procore integration, no API, no enterprise procurement — a lawyer or consultant uploads a matter's
documents and gets back a linked chronology with notice/entitlement flags. The buyer already has budget
(it is a disbursement or a write-off against a fixed-fee engagement), already understands the deliverable,
and already rebills software to the client. **Sell the tool that makes their pyramid cheaper; let them keep
the margin.** Beachhead, reference logos, and — critically — the labelled training data that nobody else can
legally accumulate.

**PARTNER is the durable position.** The testifying expert is not going away; CJC ¶88 guarantees it.
The end-state product is not "replace HKA" — it is "be the system of record that HKA's expert opens on day one",
with the consultancy as the credentialing layer that turns your output into admissible evidence. **The expert
is a feature of the market, not a competitor.**

**The one motion that is NOT a channel play — and is the bigger prize — is prevention.** Selling continuous
commercial-event detection to the *contractor*, before a dispute exists, is a different buyer with a different
budget line. The consultancies structurally cannot follow you there (§13). But note the tension: **prevention
is a harder first sale** (no budget line exists, no incident to react to), which is why the channel play is
the right V1 and prevention is the right V2.

**The risk to name honestly:** selling to consultants trains them to do the work faster, which may *lower* the
price of the incumbent substitute rather than displace it. If the product's only value is hours-reduction,
the consultancies capture the surplus and the startup ends up as a $50k/seat tool vendor to a low-margin
industry (Diales: 3.3% underlying operating margin). **The escape is to own the pre-dispute detection layer,
where the consultancy has no product and no incentive to build one — and where the value is measured against
lost entitlement, not against saved analyst hours.**
