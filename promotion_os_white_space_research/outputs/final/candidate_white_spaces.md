## 0. Methodology note

**Matrix verification.** The master capability matrix (15 companies × 100 squares) was mechanically assembled from the Section 12 score blocks of the approved reports with all 45 category-manager overrides applied (A: 5, B: 17, C: 5, D: 18). I ran a full mechanical re-verification (script: scratchpad `verify_matrix.py`), not just spot-checks: all 1,500 `agent_score` values in the long CSV match the report Section 12 blocks; all 45 overrides match the manager normalization blocks and are correctly applied; `final_score` in the long CSV equals the wide CSV in all 1,500 cells; no squares are missing. I additionally hand-checked 12 raw report lines (Votigo B03, Sweeppea C02, Talon.One J03, Voucherify G04, Open Loyalty G08, GeoComply E04, Persona E04, Socure B10, OPA D02, Cedar F07, Cerbos J10, Permit.io B03) against the parsed values. **Result: CLEAN — no assembly errors found.** All scores cited below are post-normalization (`final_score`).

**Synthesis discipline applied.**
- Only **company gaps** (buyer jobs) are advanced; feature gaps flagged by managers (idempotency, webhook polish, reason-code taxonomies, retention tiers, review-outcome API shims, effective-dating alone, managed OPA control planes) were folded into candidates or rejected, per the manager-brief distinction.
- Per AGENTS.md anti-bias rules: the original Promotion OS thesis was **not** preserved by default — the closest descendant (Candidate 4) is scored honestly and ranks below two differently-shaped opportunities; two manager-passed gap candidates are **rejected** with scores below 60; the episodic-promotions version of the original thesis is rejected without scoring (Section 3.7).
- Services and counsel are treated as first-class substitutes throughout (they defeat the thesis outright in episodic promotions — Manager A).
- Every scored candidate carries the enterprise displacement sentence and an explicit hard-gate check per `research/WHITE_SPACE_SCORING.md`.
- Adjacent threats flagged by managers (Sumsub, Taktile, PlainID, Axiomatics, AWS AgentCore/Dogwood) were verified against official pages before any gap was declared open (Section 2). Two additional landscape checks (Vixio, PerformLine) were performed on my own initiative; one exposes a category the study did not cover (Section 6).

**Scoring recap** (from `research/WHITE_SPACE_SCORING.md`): 10 dimensions scored 0–5, weights 15/15/15/10/10/5/10/10/5/5 (%), normalized to 100. Hard gates: competitive gap ≤2, WTP ≤2, switching ≤2, no identifiable buyer, unreviewed-legal-advice dependence, "generic rules engine + counsel + config covers it cheaply," UI-only moat. Labels: ≥90 exceptional, 80–89 strong, 70–79 investigate, 60–69 weak/feature, <60 reject.

### 3.1 Candidate 1 — Counsel-governed policy lifecycle for regulated digital conduct ("legal change → production, provably")

**Gap statement.** When a multi-state regulated-commerce enterprise (gaming/sweeps operators, fintechs with incentive programs, cannabis/alcohol/telehealth commerce) needs to convert a counsel-approved legal position into production authorization logic — with proof of who approved what, when, and what it changed — current products fail because every policy pipeline is engineer-shaped (git PRs over YAML/Rego/Cedar) and every decisioning vendor lacks publish approvals entirely or offers admin-grade approvals with verified bypass paths, causing weeks of legal/product/engineering coordination per change, unprovable sign-off in audits, and stale rules running in production after law changes.

- **Buyer:** General Counsel / Chief Compliance Officer (economic buyer) + VP Platform Engineering (technical buyer). Budget lines: legal ops / compliance ops; engineering co-sponsorship.
- **Job-to-be-done:** "Take counsel's approved position (e.g., 'NY now requires X for bonus offers effective July 1') and get it into every enforcement point, on the effective date, with an attestation record and a pre-deployment impact preview — without a ticket queue."
- **Current process/stack:** counsel memo in email/DocuSign → Jira ticket → engineers hand-encode into Talon.One rules / Socure workflows / Cerbos-OPA policies / internal engines → QA → deploy whenever it ships → screenshots and email threads at audit time (Manager B/C/D convergent finding; Live Nation improvised a legal gate from ShortStack publish permissions — demand signal at the DIY end; RTM sells the same thing as headcount at the services end).
- **Where it fails (verified):** No publish-approval workflow exists at Persona or Socure (verified in official docs); Socure reason-code-list edits alter live decisioning with no version and no audit entry (verified verbatim); Talon.One revisions have documented bypass paths and one-admin finalization; Voucherify's approval feature is the category best and still admin-semantics, one-approver-suffices, with no documented approval-history retention; all four policy engines delegate approval to git; effective-dated activation exists in **zero** of 15 products (C04 ≤ 2 uniformly); D05 (human approval history) ceiling is 3 (Persona/Socure, case-level not policy-level).
- **Consequence:** change latency measured in weeks per jurisdictional change × dozens of changes/year for multi-state operators; audit findings for ungoverned change control; exposure when stale rules run after effective dates; GC personally certifies controls they cannot evidence.
- **Proposed wedge:** a counsel-native control plane above installed engines: legal-readable policy representation with side-by-side diff, quorum/sequenced attestation (counsel role as first-class), effective-dated activation and scheduled rollback, impact preview (replay of recent traffic + formal diff where the engine allows), and compile-down to Talon.One/Voucherify configs, Socure workflows, Cedar/Rego/CEL, and internal engines. **Embed, don't compete** (Manager D packaging rule): the engines are commodity; the governed pipeline is the product.
- **Frequency test:** passes only in multi-jurisdiction regulated verticals (regulatory change + campaign/program change = weekly-to-monthly cadence); fails for brands running 2–4 promotions/year (S7 absorbs them).
- **Regulatory legitimacy (test 10):** product carries the *customer's counsel's* decisions; it makes no legal interpretations itself. Clean separation is mandatory and is what keeps the unreviewed-legal-advice gate green.
- **Why current vendors don't solve it:** engines are engineer-DNA (Cerbos G04=1, OPA G04=0, AVP G04=0, Permit G04=2 runtime-only); decisioning vendors' buyers are marketing/fraud, not legal (I01 ≤ 1 at 11 of 15); GeoComply's model actively resists exposing the pipeline (its tuning moat); PlainID/Axiomatics prove the shape but sit in access control (verified); Adyen/Talon.One roadmap is payments (verified); Voucherify roadmap is AI offer optimization.
- **Most dangerous incumbent:** Socure (RiskOS could add approval gates in a quarter — Manager C: "Persona/Socure could add approval gates in a quarter — but the counsel semantics… are an editorial/legal-ops muscle none has"). **Most dangerous substitute stack:** S6 (RiskOS + internal + counsel) for identity-gated actions; S4 (Cerbos + git PRs + counsel) for engineering-led orgs; and above all **inertia** — email + Jira currently passes most audits (Manager B). That inertia is the real sales enemy and the reason WTP is capped at 3.
- **Evidence for pain/frequency/budget:** Live Nation building a gate out of publish permissions (A); Voucherify monetizing approvals as an Enterprise gate (B — proves paid demand for governance); RTM selling counsel-in-the-loop as headcount on every project (A); the universal "legal reviews YAML in a PR" concession (D); GC-office unfunded-mandate finding (D §4).

### 3.2 Candidate 2 — Maintained jurisdictional policy content network ("machine-readable legal content with provenance")

**Gap statement.** When the compliance/legal function of a multi-jurisdiction consumer enterprise needs to know — and keep current — what promotional/incentive/eligibility conduct is permitted per jurisdiction, per product type, per action, current products fail because **no vendor sells maintained, provenance-linked, machine-readable rules** (every decisioning vendor is documentedly neutral or content-opaque, and every regulatory-intelligence vendor stops at prose), causing every enterprise to re-derive the same NJ/NY/CA/Quebec rules with its own outside counsel, hand-encode them without provenance, and bear staleness risk between law change and rule change.

- **Buyer:** GC / CCO / Head of Regulatory Affairs (economic buyer); consumed by compliance engineering. Secondary OEM buyer: decisioning/engine vendors themselves.
- **Job-to-be-done:** "Give my counsel a citation-linked, current rule set for sweepstakes/bonusing/incentive conduct in the 50 states + CA/UK/EU markets we operate in, in a form my counsel approves once and my engineers consume as versioned diffs — and alert us with an impact readout when a rule changes."
- **Current process/stack:** outside counsel memos per jurisdiction (the promotion-law-boutique + bonding-agent labor market — Manager A) + Vixio/law-firm alerts read by humans + engineers encoding into whatever engine is installed + annual re-review. Every enterprise duplicates this privately (Manager C: "each enterprise re-derives the same NJ/NY/CA rules with its own counsel — this duplication across enterprises is the network-effect opening").
- **Where it fails (verified):** C06/C09/C10 ≈ 0–1 across all 15 companies (C10 max = 1); Socure's neutrality is documented in its own docs, twice; Persona deliberately makes no legal claims; GeoComply monetizes content by keeping it opaque (managed service; Gov Relations team); Sumsub's content is prose guidance about its own product's legality with no execution linkage (verified this session); Vixio ships prose obligations with no executable form (verified this session); the four policy engines ship zero content by design.
- **Consequence:** five-to-six-figure counsel spend per jurisdiction-domain per year, duplicated across every operator; encoding errors with no provenance to audit against; staleness windows between effective dates and deployed rules; no defensible answer to "what legal authority does this rule implement?"
- **Proposed wedge:** jurisdiction packs for a narrow, high-pain domain first (promotional conduct: sweepstakes/contests, bonusing, incentive marketing in regulated verticals) — each rule carrying citation, effective date, confidence/interpretation notes, and a counsel-review record; delivered as versioned, diffable, engine-compilable artifacts (Cedar/Rego/CEL/vendor configs); update service driven by change monitoring. Structured so **customer counsel reviews and adopts packs** (the product supplies provenance-linked propositions, not legal advice); optionally distributed with/through law-firm partners.
- **Frequency test:** passes — law and interpretation change continuously; packs are consumed on every decision.
- **Regulatory legitimacy (test 10):** the central design constraint. The company must not practice law: content ships as counsel-reviewable propositions with provenance; adoption is the customer counsel's act. Red team must stress UPL and liability structure (flagged, Section 5).
- **Why current vendors don't solve it:** documented refusal (Socure), deliberate avoidance (Persona), monetized opacity (GeoComply), engineer DNA and no legal editorial ops (OPA/Cedar/Cerbos/Permit — Manager D: "no engine vendor will build it… different liability posture, a legal buyer none of them serves, I01 ≤ 1 everywhere"), prose-only models (Vixio, law firms, PerformLine's rulebooks are ad-content-review scoped).
- **Most dangerous incumbent:** **Vixio** (owns content ops + the gambling/payments buyer; would need to build execution) and **GeoComply** (owns jurisdiction content ops + the buyer in the beachhead vertical; would need to expose its black box, which cannibalizes its tuning moat). **Most dangerous substitute stack:** S8 (Cerbos + Vixio + counsel) — beats the candidate everywhere except the seam itself; and the incumbent labor market (promotion-law boutiques) which any counsel-workflow/content product competes with first (Manager A).
- **Evidence for pain/frequency/budget:** the C-row's "defended-by-refusal" pattern (C §3 gap 2); Sweeppea's C01=3 proves software-encoded jurisdiction thresholds are buildable and valued even at 3-person scale; GeoComply's whole business proves jurisdiction-content-as-service commands compliance budget at >90% market penetration; Vixio subscriptions prove content budget exists; Manager D pricing baseline explicitly includes "outside counsel retainer per jurisdiction per year."

### 3.3 Candidate 3 — Evidence-grade decision archive with as-of replay ("the regulatory decision evidence plane")

### 3.4 Candidate 4 — Promotional-conduct authorization for the regulated-adjacency seam (the reshaped Promotion OS)
