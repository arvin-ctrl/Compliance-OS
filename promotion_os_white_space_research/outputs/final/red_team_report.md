### 2b. UPL / liability — is the counsel-adoption/provenance model viable?

**Yes — the structure is viable; the gate is a design problem with four working precedents, and it actually got stronger under attack.** But the precedents also define exactly what promotions lacks.

- **Droit (FIS)** — vendor-maintained regulatory text compiled to machine-executable logic, real-time "can I trade?" decisions, every decision traceable to source regulation text; bought by the most conservative buyers on earth (tier-1 bank compliance) since ~2012; acquired by FIS (Mar 2026). Independently corroborated this session: "encodes regulatory obligations as machine-executable logic… in real time," "converts live regulatory text into transparent, auditable logic models"; FIS says the engine will extend to "cross-border rules, digital assets and **prediction markets**" [RT-10; REGINTEL-022/-023]. Liability posture: traceability + auditability, no indemnity.
- **Apiax** — binary "do's and don'ts without grey zones" rules for 190+ countries via REST API and MCP, covering **marketing/solicitation/onboarding** conduct; content digitized from law-firm country manuals, with named legal-content partners carrying interpretive weight. Homepage re-verified this session ("Embedded Compliance," "marketing, sales and relationship management," "promoting financial services") [RT-11; REGINTEL-027/-029]. Liability posture: named law firms stand behind substance.
- **Avalara/Sovos/Vertex** — executable tax law at planetary scale, re-verified this session: "more than 900,000 tax rules across 12,000+ U.S. jurisdictions and 190+ countries," 54B+ API calls/year, 200K customers [RT-12]. Liability posture: a bounded Accuracy Guarantee — penalties/interest capped at the lesser of the assessment or prior 12 months' fees [REGINTEL-035].
- **The proposed counsel-adoption structure** (vendor ships provenance-linked propositions; customer counsel reviews and adopts) is a fourth variant that pushes vendor exposure lower still — the vendor never applies law to a specific promotion; adoption is the customer counsel's act. Thomson Reuters Practical Law proves counsel happily consume vendor-maintained legal content at scale in prose form.
- **The enforcement boundary is now marked**: FTC v. DoNotPay (final order Feb 2025, $193K) — the violation was selling law-shaped outputs while having "not hire[d] or retain[ed] attorneys to test the quality and accuracy of its service's law-related features" [RT-13]. Licensed editorial review inside the operation is the compliance floor; every working precedent has it.

**What is genuinely different in promotions — three residual constraints, one of them serious:**
1. **No mandatory computation point** (the deepest one). Tax computes to complete every invoice; Dodd-Frank created a computation point banks could not legally skip. Promotional conduct is checked only when someone fears enforcement. Nothing in my 2a research changes this: the mandates that exist (geolocation, KYC, self-exclusion suppression) already have owners. Demand for promo-conduct answers stays discretionary.
2. **Liability is not cappable Avalara-style.** A wrong "this sweep is lawful in FL" costs a license or an AG action, not an assessable dollar amount. Consequence: the viable postures are Droit's (provenance + auditability) and Apiax's (named counsel), never indemnity. This is workable but caps the "guarantee" as a selling point.
3. **The beachhead's hardest questions were contested law, not settled-but-churning law.** The 2025–26 wave answered "is the dual-currency sweeps model legal?" with statewide bans. A binary rules vendor would have had to answer the exact question state AGs were litigating — any answer was wrong or useless. Executable content works where law is settled but churning (RMG bonusing/RG rules, state sweepstakes mechanics for lawful promotions, fintech UDAAP/TILA conduct), and must refuse contested-legality questions by design. Apiax's "without grey zones" is achievable in cross-border banking *because the grey zones were adjudicated long ago*; a promotions vendor must scope to the adjudicated subset.

**Ruling:** the unreviewed-legal-advice gate **passes on precedent** (upgrade from synthesis's "conditional pass" — this is the one gate that strengthened under red-team attack). UPL is not the kill vector. The kill vectors for C2 live elsewhere (Section 3.2).

## 3. Candidate attacks

### 3.1 Candidate 1 — Counsel-governed policy lifecycle. Verdict: **KILLED** (standalone)

- **Strongest argument FOR:** The absence is real and universal — verified at all 15 vendors plus both supplements: no publish-approval workflow exists at Persona or Socure; Socure reason-code edits bypass versioning and audit logs; Talon.One revisions have documented bypass paths; effective-dated activation exists in zero of 15; every engine routes legal review through git PRs. Voucherify monetizes approvals as an Enterprise gate and Red Oak sells governed approval pipelines to 1,800+ firms — governance workflow demonstrably commands budget *somewhere*.
- **Strongest argument AGAINST (the kill):** Three stacked, each sufficient. (1) **WTP gate fails on evidence** (2a): no enforcement action anywhere prices missing sign-off, versioning, or attestation; the proven approval-workflow budgets (Red Oak, Veeva PromoMats) exist only where a regulator mandates review of the artifact (FINRA ad review, FDA MLR) — no regulator mandates attestation of promo-policy changes. C1 therefore sells efficiency against an inertia stack that passes audits; Manager B's finding stands unrefuted. WTP re-scores 3→2 → hard-gate failure. (2) **The core guarantee is unkeepable on the installed base.** C1's promise is "no ungoverned change reaches production, provably." But it can only govern changes that flow through it, and the engines it compiles to have *verified* ungoverned side doors: Talon.One coupons/referrals/stores/achievements bypass revisions entirely; Socure reason-code-list edits alter live decisioning with no version and no audit entry. The audit story collapses to "governed, except where the substrate leaks" — which is the status quo's audit story too. (3) **The mechanics are inside the 18-month window** (2c): Socure has shipped four platform releases in 21 months and already markets "Policy Agility" and auto-generated audit documentation; Manager D rated effective-dating "roadmap-sized" for Cerbos/Permit; AgentCore/Dogwood commoditizes NL authoring and temporal semantics.
- **Most dangerous incumbent:** Socure (mechanics fast-follow at demonstrated cadence, already using the pitch language). Perception-level: Red Oak ("we already have an approval workflow").
- **Most dangerous substitute stack:** S6 (RiskOS + internal rules + counsel) for identity-gated actions; S4 (Cerbos git PRs + counsel) for engineering-led orgs; and above all inertia — email + Jira + Voucherify-style approvals, which currently passes audits.
- **Easiest internal build:** designate counsel as a required approver in git/Voucherify/Move-to-Live permissions + a DocuSign attestation folder + a change-calendar for effective dates. 1 engineer, 2 sprints, covers the audit conversation as it exists today.
- **Buyer objection:** GC/CCO historically buys content and services, not infrastructure; the software line item this resembles is already named PerformLine/Red Oak at fintech accounts; two-headed GC+platform sale with no established channel.
- **Implementation objection:** compile-down to N proprietary engines is a permanent integration tax against moving targets whose vendors have no incentive to cooperate (Adyen's roadmap is payments; GeoComply actively resists exposure) — and each connector inherits the target's bypass paths, i.e., the integration burden buys an incomplete guarantee.
- **Legal/liability objection:** mild (it carries the customer counsel's decisions) — this is the one clean dimension; not enough to save it.
- **Vector sweep (remaining):** *Services substitution* — promotion-law boutiques + RTM-style counsel-as-headcount already sell the outcome. *Low frequency* — jurisdictional change is weekly-to-monthly only at multi-state regulated operators; the mid-market brand cadence (2–4/year) doesn't clear the bar. *Data moat* — attestation graphs are customer-private; no cross-customer compounding. *Regulatory contraction* — indifferent. *Switching* — must beat "good enough" with no priced pain behind it; fails the enterprise-switch standard.
- **Gate re-test:** competitive gap 4→3 (counsel-semantics approval workflow exists at category level — Veeva MLR, Red Oak — for assets; mechanics addable in quarters); **WTP 3→2 (HARD-GATE FAIL)**; "generic covers cheaply" now fails for the thin version buyers will actually compare. Re-score ≈ 66. 
- **What would change my mind (for the record):** an exam finding or enforcement action naming ungoverned rule-change control as a violation (e.g., a state gaming lab audit or MA 205 CMR internal-controls citation for promo rules), plus ≥3 GCs committing real budget numbers in validation interviews. Until then C1 is a *feature* of the composed fusion, not a company.

## 6. The overall opportunity space — strongest argument left standing on each side

**FOR:** The five-layer absence is real, verified to an unusual standard (15 primary vendors + 11 marketing-compliance vendors + 10 regintel vendors, with documented refusals rather than mere silence), and the exact product shape that monetizes such absences — maintained executable regulatory content + runtime decision API + decision provenance — is not a hypothesis: it has been built, bought by the most conservative compliance buyers in existence, and exited to the infrastructure incumbent, three separate times (Droit, Apiax, Avalara-trio). The promotions/gaming-adjacent vertical has the ingredients those domains had — churning multi-jurisdiction law, priced enforcement of runtime failures, duplicated counsel spend — and no occupant: the content incumbent has no API, the signal incumbents refuse content in writing, and the marketing-compliance incumbents stop at words.

**AGAINST:** Every prior success of that shape rode a **mandatory computation point** — the invoice that cannot issue without a tax amount, the trade that cannot execute without a Dodd-Frank eligibility check, the wager that cannot be accepted without a court-recognized geolocation check — and promotions has none. My enforcement research shows regulators price runtime eligibility failures (whose budget the signal vendors already own) and never the governance, content, or evidence increments these candidates actually sell; the one segment generating a synchronizing shock (sweeps casinos) is being answered with bans, shrinking the beachhead rather than mandating the product. Until some regulator or platform makes a promo-conduct check mandatory, demand stays discretionary, the buyer count stays in the low hundreds, and the fast-followers who own the transaction path (GeoComply, Socure, now FIS/Droit) can wait, watch, and buy the winner — which caps this space at a good acquisition, and only if the unproven WTP materializes at all.
