## 5. What the software determines vs. what licensed counsel must approve

Stated once more, standalone, because it is the load-bearing legal boundary of the entire pivot:

- **Software determines (mechanical, post-adoption):** which adopted rule versions apply to a given action/jurisdiction/product tuple; the tri-state verdict and reason codes with citations; stateful eligibility arithmetic over verified signals; effective-date activation and scheduled expiry of adopted pack versions; the evidence record and its integrity; diffs/impact readouts when packs change.
- **Licensed counsel approves (interpretive, always):** the substance of every rule proposition — authored under vendor-side licensed editorial review (the FTC v. DoNotPay floor: law-shaped outputs without retained attorneys testing them drew a federal order [RT-13]) with named law-firm partners standing behind content (Apiax structure); customer counsel's adoption of each pack before it can activate (the act that keeps the vendor out of applying law to specific promotions); and every contested-legality question, which the product must refuse and route.
- **Never:** bespoke legal advice on a specific promotion; indemnity or accuracy guarantees; any answer to "is this business model legal?" — the question class the 2025–26 sweeps wave proved fatal to answer.

If discovery reveals that buyers will only pay when the product crosses this line (i.e., they want the vendor's lawyers to replace theirs), the unreviewed-legal-advice gate fails and the verdict converts to KILL. This is kill criterion K3.

Instrument: a design-partner offer (paid pilot or LOI with committed pricing) for counsel-adopted executable packs consumed through the decision API. - **Pass:** ≥5 signed paid design-partner commitments at real ACVs (≥$50K/yr class, consistent with the counsel-spend displacement and six-figure-ACV market math), with a named budget line, for the executable increment — not prose.
- **Cost class:** founder time + travel + design-partner legal paperwork; low-to-mid five figures USD; zero engineering hires.
- **Kill criteria (K1):** fewer than 5 commitments by day 90; or the modal buyer maps the product onto existing budget names (PerformLine/Red Oak/Sedric/Vixio/counsel retainers) and declines to fund the increment; or discovered pricing lands at counsel-memo levels (<$25K) that cannot carry licensed editorial ops. Any one → KILL.
