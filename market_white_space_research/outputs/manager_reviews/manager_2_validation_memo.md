## 5. Cluster adjudications

### 5a. The payer-defense bundle — H01 (85) + H02 (80.8) + H03 (73) + H09 (80); H20 (77) as collision twin

**Is the bundle a stronger company than H01 alone? Yes — but it is not a stronger *wedge*, and Wave 3 must attack it as H01-led.**

- The **company** case: the bundle is what makes H01's moat and ceiling credible — same buyer (H02's anchor artifact carries H01's pain in one sentence; billing companies sell all three jobs; the shared channel is r/CodingandBilling + HBMA + AAPC), and the expansion path runs to the 4–10%-of-collections services line itself. The engine overlap is real but **partial** and must not be oversold: H01/H09 share the 835-ingestion + deadline-state-machine + recovery-ledger spine; H02/H03's build surfaces (CAQH/PECOS/payer portals; EHR read) are substantially disjoint. What genuinely compounds across all four is the buyer relationship, payer-quirk data, and channel — not one codebase.
- **Sequencing ruling (H02's validator proposed H02-first; overruled):** **H01 leads.** Reasons: (1) forcing shape — H01's compulsion is continuous per-claim; H02's is front-loaded at the solo tier by its own evidence ("once you're set up it's fine"); (2) threat asymmetry — H01's in-segment entrant is unfunded and confirmed missing the ERA layer, while H02's (Assured, $19M Insight-led five weeks ago, agents already navigating payer portals) is one pricing page from head-on; (3) technical risk — H02's core motion leans on portal/CAQH/PECOS delegated access surviving MFA/anti-bot ToS at scale (its own R1); H01's packet-not-portal design deliberately avoids that wall; (4) the 2026 downcode wave is a live, dated demand event with a legislated 18-month window. H02 becomes expansion module #1 — **unless Assured ships published pricing ≤$200/provider/mo before build start, in which case H02's standalone case dies and the module reorders behind H03.**
- **H03** enters only as module #2, behind the Humata GA kill-trigger, in the assembly-first procedural slice with the therapy/drug/athena exclusions.
- **H09** is a **vertical sibling, not a module** — different buyer pool, different channels (state associations), deterministic rather than discretionary denials. It shares the engine spine and the thesis, so it advances inside this cluster as the designated second vertical (Texas beachhead), started only after H01 traction or as the fallback lead if red team kills H01's window.
- **H20** shares the *shape*, not the operation (no PHI, different data formats, different channels). It is a portfolio hedge, not a bundle member — it gets its own slate slot precisely so the program is not concentrated in healthcare if H01 breaks.

### 5b. The Exit Factory — H16 (81) + H15-B (77) + H17 (69.8, folded) + H19-NRx watchlist

**Is the composed platform stronger than H16 alone? Yes — the cluster is the answer to H16's only real weakness.**

- H16's lowest scores are expansion ceiling (3) and durability (3): window economics. The cluster converts an event-window business into a serial-sunset conveyor + archive annuity: QBD (executed dates, now) → RE7/Bonterra (official late-summer-2027 date verified from Blackbaud's own community, in-writing SQL extraction surface, dateless renewal ratchet) → catch-up/cleanup (perennial inflow through the same accounting-firm channel, shared verification harness and "ties out or you don't pay" mechanic).
- **Sequencing:** H16 builds first (only wedge with executed dates + firm-concentrated distribution). H15-B is validated as conveyor #2 by month 6–9 — which is exactly H16's own kill criterion ("no second sunset vertical validated by month 9"), so the cluster and the kill test are the same event. H17 folds in as the engagement line where the firm channel overlaps; it does not get standalone investment. H19's NRx stays a **watchlist candidate requiring a data-access spike first** (no open extraction surface, PHI/controlled-substance archive burden, InfoWerks incumbent, unsized cohort) — it is evidence *for* the conveyor thesis, not a 2026–27 build.
- Honest structural caveat for the red team: the two lead conveyors sell into **different channels** (accounting firms vs. nonprofit development offices/consultants) — the "same factory" claim is true of the harness and playbook pattern, less true of distribution. Founder-capacity across two channels in year 1 is an attack surface, not an assumption.
