## 2. Target 1 — Payer-Defense Bundle (H01 lead; H02/H03 modules; H09 vertical sibling)

**Steelman (strongest FOR):** The program's best-evidenced asymmetry. The counterparty automated first at scale (BCBSIL batch downcoding live 7/2026, 2,400 claims/mo at one practice; Aetna/Cigna automated screens; CCC-class asymmetry in every thread), the defense is a hand-worked forfeiture machine (35–60% of denials never resubmitted while ~70% of appealed denials overturn), the labor is priced in public ($37.5–50.5k specialists; 4–10% of collections; 20–35% contingency), the forcing function is continuous and per-claim with statutory-grade deadlines, and — re-verified today — **no vendor at any tier ships ERA-native batch downcode/underpayment defense at a self-serve price.** Illinois legislated an 18-month window in which defense is the only option. The same buyer then buys credentialing, PA, and eventually the billing-company services line itself — the only slate target whose expansion is sold to the *same* person.

**Strongest AGAINST:** This is now a visibly hot category in which capability is commodity. Four-plus entrants circle the same buyer from different angles (Muni per-letter → now calls/EHR/managed-ops; Aegis EOB→packet→portal for billing teams; Waystar/Adonis/AKASA above; Revecore's hospital-side underpayment AI; content-farming startups like RapidClaims/Honey Health), Waystar's published results prove the drafting layer is table stakes, and the wedge's whole defensibility is a 2–6-quarter GTM/economics gap plus data that takes 12–24 months to accumulate. A solo founder with no reputation is asking practices to upload PHI while racing funded teams to the same tier.

**Most dangerous incumbent:** Waystar — capability fully proven (40% more overturns, 90% faster, batch appeal packages), public-company distribution; the only thing keeping it out of the tier is its own ACV/implementation economics. A self-serve SKU decision ends the window. (Checked today: no such SKU; still custom-quoted sales-led.)

**Most dangerous substitute:** **Muni Health's trajectory** — not its $20 letters but its new stack (calls from $499/mo, custom-quoted *managed payer operations*, an EHR with appeal evidence built in). Muni is assembling the "AI-native billing company" — H01's own §9 endgame — from the services side while H01 would build it from the software side. If Muni raises on that story, it hits the same buyer with an already-running trust motion. Watch monthly.

**Easiest internal-build/DIY:** A biller pasting a denial into ChatGPT gets a competent appeal letter today — which is why the wedge must never be letters. The ERA-history variance baseline, per-payer deadline state machine, and 200-claim batch reconsideration workflow are beyond casual DIY, and the practices in evidence can't even keep the Excel tracker alive at 2026 volumes. DIY attack fails against the gated wedge; it kills the me-too variant Manager 2 already killed.

**Buyer objection:** "Prove it's worth $299–599/mo before I trust you with our remits." Practices anchor on visible cost, not invisible forfeiture; billing companies are labor businesses whose margin *is* the hidden inefficiency — some will resist software that makes their labor legible, and a few will DIY-copy the workflow. Counter: the recovery ledger is the product's own proof-of-ROI artifact, and the 10–15%-of-recovered campaign option converts skeptics without a subscription decision. The billing-company channel is genuinely unproven and must be tested, not assumed.

**Implementation objection (the sharpest one landed):** **The pilot measures the wrong denominator.** Packet-not-portal leaves the human doing exactly what the anchor threads complain about — portal archaeology, status-chasing ("recons and appeal in progress status"), phone queues, submission. If the dominant time sink is status/submission rather than classify/research/draft, a ≥60%-minutes-saved bar may fail even while the product recovers real money by killing the forfeiture failure mode (deadline machine + batch). The R2 pilot must be re-denominated: recovered-$ per staff-hour and %-of-denials-worked-before-deadline vs. baseline, with minutes as a secondary metric. If *neither* the dollar delta nor the time delta clears, that is the honest kill.

**Liability/trust objection:** PHI to a no-name solo vendor on BAA-only posture. Real, partially rebutted by Muni's demonstrated no-credit-card traction at this tier and by billing companies' higher risk tolerance; unresolvable except by the 10-call test. Guardrails hold: drafts under provider signature, no autonomous portal bots (Availity ban is a *competitor's* compliance overhang — Aegis advertises Availity submission), no medical judgment practiced.

**Timing objection:** The 2026 downcode wave is the marketing hook, and it is transient by construction (IL ban 2028; MD order; possible payer retreat). Attack fails to kill: the denial/underpayment/deadline core predates payer AI (denial rates ~12–15% and 1–3% underpayment leakage are pre-wave numbers; Rivet sells underpayment detection alone at $6k/yr), and the unit economics must be — and per the dossier are — modeled to clear at pre-2026 baselines with the downcode module as one of six features. What transience really costs is the same-week outreach hook, i.e., higher CAC after 2027.

**H09 sub-brief verdict: WEAKENED.** The wedge's product logic held every check (deterministic pre-claim matching; state-surface reads; Reeve still pre-traction). But two things moved against it today: (1) Paradigm/Careswitch's closing integration *names* agency self-sufficiency and pre-claim error-spotting — the incumbent with 4,000 logos has publicly pointed its AI team at the wedge's core; (2) H.R.1 arithmetic (3–7% reimbursement shortfalls; ~9.4% eligibility contraction by 2034) is thinning and distracting the buyer pool at ~10% margins. R6 (operator-voice thinness on the named reconciliation task) also remains unresolved. Keep H09 strictly as the sequenced second vertical behind H01 traction; it is no longer a credible *fallback lead* if H01 dies — H20 or H16 fills that role better.

**Bundle-logic ruling:** The shared buyer is real at the billing-company level (they sell all three jobs) and at the 1–12-provider specialty practice (one admin owns payer-facing everything); it is loose at the solo-behavioral tier (credentialing is a front-loaded event; denial defense is continuous; PA mostly N/A). H01 stands alone on its own LTV at the specialty/billco tiers regardless — the bundle is upside, not load-bearing. Manager 2's H01-first sequencing ruling is affirmed on the same grounds it was made, now with fresh confirmation that H02's threat (Assured) is one pricing page away while H01's in-segment entrant remains unfunded and ERA-less.

### Verdict: **SURVIVES**
- **What got tightened:** (a) R2 pilot re-denominated in recovered dollars (see §8 test 1); (b) H09 demoted from fallback-lead to sequenced-option; (c) entrant watch cadence monthly with Muni's EHR/managed-ops explicitly on the board; (d) unit economics must clear at pre-2026 denial baselines.

## 3. Target 2 — Exit Factory (H16 lead; H15-B conveyor #2; H17 fold)

**Steelman (strongest FOR):** The rare sunset with budget proof on every side and the compulsion already *executed*, not promised: stop-sell done (9/2024), Desktop-2023 services killed (5/31/2026), two price waves in 12 months with the third landing **October 1 — five weeks from today** — and Intuit's own transfer guide documents the losses its free path inflicts. The RE7 conveyor (official summer-2027 date, in-writing SQL surface, 23%/24-month renewal ratchet) is verified feedstock #2, and today's checks show both clocks holding.

**Strongest AGAINST:** It is a windowed business whose platform escape hatch is unproven, and its hardest technical claim sits exactly where the dossier waved its hands. Migration demand decays ~2028; the annuity (archive attach ≥50%) and the conveyor (a *second* channel — nonprofit development offices — validated by month 9) are both untested, and one founder running accounting-firm *and* nonprofit channels in year 1 is the founder-capacity assumption Manager 2 already flagged as an attack surface. Meanwhile payroll-YTD extraction — the difference between a $499 verified migration and a $225 mill — runs through an SDK whose payroll surface is restricted to third parties (V11).

**Most dangerous incumbent:** Intuit — not by competing but by *fixing*: a fidelity upgrade to the free tool (sales orders/price levels/payroll preserved) or a quiet Desktop-2024 extension converts the cliff into a plateau. Checked today: neither shipped; three years of stable drop-list; incentive points at QBO price-mix. The kill trigger stays armed monthly.

**Most dangerous substitute:** The $225 conversion mills bolting "verification" *marketing* (not discipline) onto their price point, plus the buyer's own accountant with Claude — the threads already show a full-year reconciliation improvised "in about 6 minutes." The defense is the thing DIY can't fake: the signed diff pack and the archive. If buyers can't tell verified from claimed-verified, price collapses toward the mill — this is why the money-back tie-out gate is the *marketing*, not just the QA.

**Easiest internal-build/DIY:** For the firm: assign a staffer + Intuit's free tool + 20–40 billable hours — the status quo. Attack fails on cost (the labor line is the displacement target) but succeeds partially on *motive*: see buyer objection.

**Buyer objection (the reframe that landed):** **Migration labor is firm revenue, not firm cost.** A 60-client firm's 1,200–2,400 hours are a $120–480k billable line; a $499 factory doesn't cut their cost, it deletes their billings — unless the pitch is capacity: "the window compresses all 60 clients into 18 months you can't staff; run 3× the migrations at flat COGS and keep your bill rate on advisory." Evidence supports capacity-constraint during the crunch, but the sales narrative in the dossier is priced-against-labor and must be rebuilt as margin-expansion for firms (and price-relief only for direct SMBs). The $499 WTP pilot is therefore genuinely open, not a formality — and the <$150/file kill trigger is correct.

**Implementation objection (hardest tech risk in the slate):** The fidelity wall. qbXML/SDK payroll access is restricted (V11: "Payroll System Only" walls; paycheck detail via reports only), attachments live outside the file, audit-trail extraction is report-shaped. "Payroll YTD within $0.01 at <60 human-minutes/file, 100% tie-out on 20 ugly real files" is the right gate but is currently an *aspiration*: a payroll-extraction feasibility spike (report-scrape path proven end-to-end) must precede the 90-day build, and the triage gate must not quietly route out every payroll-bearing file — most target SMBs *have* payroll, and a factory that refuses them is a mill with better fonts.

**Liability/trust objection:** A wrong diff signed off is an E&O event consumed by a professional of record; the "ties out or you don't pay" mechanic transfers the QA burden honestly, and the firm-as-professional-of-record structure holds. Archive custody of a business's complete books is a security/permanence promise a solo founder must actually engineer (retention, encryption, exit export) — cost, not blocker. Passes with insurance + engagement-letter work.

**Timing objection:** Attack fails against the wedge — the Oct-1 wave and the 2027 services mechanics force decisions on Intuit's calendar regardless of the unverified exact month, and a date slip *delays* rather than kills (hostage/archive pain is dateless). Attack lands against the company: if the founder starts H16 in Q4-2026, the peak revenue window and the month-9 conveyor test both land inside 2027 — there is no slack for a second bet alongside it.

## 4. Target 3 — H05 Accessibility Conformance Factory

**Steelman (strongest FOR):** A published services price list ($100–250/page, $1,850–4,450 VPAT bundles), a record-pace US litigation machine (≈514 suits/month, 79% e-commerce, 64% of defendants <$25M), a federally discredited cheap substitute whose presence now *correlates with getting sued* (113 of July's 401 defendants had widgets), procurement money-gates on VPATs, and the industry's own leaders publicly validating the hybrid-audit architecture while none of them ships SMB-priced *signed* audits today. The AI layer is genuinely the right shape (fixed public 55-criterion rubric; machine-verifiable evidence; standardized artifact) — the anti-Bench.

**Strongest AGAINST (two fresh hits):** (1) **The EU half of the thesis is deterrence, not compulsion.** Fresh check (V15): *zero* fines issued under any EAA-transposed law anywhere to date; the German Abmahnung wave came from opportunistic firms under competition law and specialist German counsel assesses most letters as legally weak; the only court action (Carrefour) hit a national grocery major. The "two A-grade pumps" scoring is now one A pump (US litigation) + one A money-gate (procurement) + one C+ deterrence story (EAA). (2) **The paperwork layer is commoditizing before launch:** the segment's price-list leader already sells AI VPAT generation at **$19/mo** inside its live Tracker platform (V14), with hybrid audits still dated Q1-2027. The wedge's price must be carried entirely by the signed-audit + evidence-custody layer inside a ~2-quarter window.

**Most dangerous incumbent:** Accessible.org — it defined the quality bar, holds the SMB price list, ships the Tracker platform *now*, and has pre-announced hybrid audits with "lower audit prices" for Q1-2027. It is simultaneously the thesis's best validation and its dead man's clock.

**Most dangerous substitute:** AudioEye's $49–799/mo widget-plus-"expert-testing" hybrid — not because it's good (its own report admits 38.5% of sued businesses had a solution installed) but because it anchors the SMB's mental price for "accessibility handled" an order of magnitude below a $1,500 signed audit. The sale must be made against that anchor with litigation-evidence framing.

**Easiest internal-build/DIY:** Run axe/WAVE + ask ChatGPT. Covers the automatable ~13–31% of criteria and produces exactly the false confidence the plaintiff's bar harvests. DIY attack fails on the merits but succeeds commercially wherever the buyer can't distinguish scan from audit — same literacy problem as the AudioEye anchor.

**Buyer objection:** "I already pay $490/yr for a widget and haven't been sued." The buyer is only rational *after* the letter arrives — which is why the demand-letter/defense-firm channel and the VPAT-deadline channel (where money is gated *now*) must lead, and preventive SMB sales are the expansion, not the wedge.

**Implementation objection (benchmark design flaw — attack landed):** The go/no-go gate (≥90% recall vs *two consultancy audits*) has an unstable ground truth: inter-auditor agreement in accessibility is notoriously low; two consultancies will not find the same issue set. As designed, the gate is either gameable (compare against the weaker audit) or unpassable (union of both). **Fix before it can gate anything:** adjudicated-union ground truth across ≥2 sites including one JS-heavy dynamic app, recall *and* precision measured against the adjudicated set, methodology published. Reviewer-ops economics (>8 sustained hours = Bench) remain the other honest unknown, resolvable only in pilot — and reviewer supply gets bid up at exactly the Q1-2027 deadline spike by every competitor converging on the same IAAP pool.

**Liability/trust objection:** A signed audit that misses what a plaintiff finds is a liability machine, and the FTC has already policed overselling in this exact category — the product inherits accessiBe's regulatory shadow the moment marketing drifts from "audit + evidence" to "protection." E&O, marketing counsel, and the no-guarantee line are a real standing cost. Passes with discipline; the discipline is not optional.

**Timing objection:** The ~2-quarter window to Accessible.org's hybrid (and Deque's auto-IGT expansion) held on today's check — but the $19/mo AI-VPAT release shows the incumbent will ship *pieces* of the stack early. A founder who starts today sells signed audits for roughly two quarters before the segment leader sells them cheaper with a five-year trust head start. The counter: the factory's channels (Shopify app store, defense firms, Abmahnung-adjacent agencies) are not Accessible.org's channels, and evidence-custody switching costs accrue to whoever lands first. It is a sprint with a visible finish line.

### Verdict: **WEAKENED**
- **What specifically got weaker:** EU pump downgraded to marketing surface (zero fines; weak Abmahnungen — V15); paperwork layer commoditized early ($19/mo AI VPATs — V14); benchmark design unfit to gate as written; forcing-function score of 5 no longer defensible (4 on the US-litigation + procurement pair).
- **What restores it:** (a) the **US-only carry test** — the model must clear on US-litigation + VPAT-procurement revenue alone, with EU treated as free content-marketing until a documented SMB/non-EU enforcement action exists; (b) redesigned benchmark passed (≥90% recall / <5% false findings vs adjudicated union; <5 reviewer-hours); (c) 10 paying signed audits ≥$990 within 60 days of the benchmark, before the Q1-2027 wall.
- **Kill conditions:** benchmark fails twice → PARK (as gated); credible <$2k signed-audit product from Accessible.org/Deque/AudioEye/TestParty before 10 customers; reviewer-hours >8 sustained at engagement 20; national suit volume −40% YoY for 2 consecutive quarters.

## 6. Target 5 — H12 Club Money Layer (conditional entry; H13 named replacement)

**Steelman (strongest FOR):** The only slate target with founder-domain distribution (program rule 4), the cheapest kill test in the program (10 AR audits, ≤30 days, pre-registered thresholds), and a wedge that verifiably remains unshipped anywhere after today's checks: TeamSnap has released nothing on recovery since July (V19 — invoicing only), the Genstar rollup is mid-integration churn with a surprise-fee class action (V20), and the recovery layer (retries + card-updater + restructuring + offline reconciliation + AR governance) exists in no club platform's docs. **Strongest AGAINST:** The structurally weakest business on the slate wearing the best distribution. Forcing 2/5 and durability 2/5 are unchanged; the buyer is a seasonal volunteer; the ACV is $1.5–4k against a leak that Baseline's own data says shrinks to ~2–4% once a club adopts a modern stack — meaning the wedge's best segment is the legacy/offline long tail, which is also the hardest to reach and the least software-buying; v1 is a Stripe-Billing wrapper for two quarters; and the incumbents need only ship the retry/card-updater line item that is the obvious next entry in TeamSnap ONE's launchnotes cadence. The club-shaped remainder (rosters, sibling/hardship policy, benching artifacts, offline ledger, governance reports) is real but thin armor for two quarters.

**Easiest internal-build/DIY:** Same as above — this wedge has the lowest DIY wall on the slate.

**Buyer objection:** Volunteer treasurers don't buy software; directors buy registration platforms, not AR layers; and the emotional cost of dunning families (benching a 12-year-old over a dead card) makes some clubs prefer quiet leakage. The founder's community standing partially answers this — and partially *raises* the cost of aggressive collection features in his own network.

**Implementation objection (test-design flaw — attack landed):** The Week-1/2 audit test as specified will overstate the market twice: (1) the founder's network skews to academies/clubs like his own (year-round, card-forward — the *best* case for the wedge), and (2) clubs that volunteer for an AR audit self-select for known AR pain. **Fix:** stratify the 10 audits (≥4 legacy/offline-heavy, ≥4 modern-stack; mix of seasonal clubs and year-round academies) and report recoverable-$ by stratum. A pass on a biased sample is a false green light into a thin market.

**Liability/trust objection:** First-party structure (club's name, club's Stripe, club's policy) is the correct FDCPA safe harbor, and no-custody design avoids MTL. **But the 10–15% success fee on recovered aged balances is the exposed leg:** contingency compensation for collecting consumer debts looks like collection-agency activity in several states regardless of whose name is on the email, and some state regimes (e.g., California's Rosenthal framework) reach first-party conduct. Expect the legal memo to kill or heavily restrict the success-fee model → the upside pricing collapses to flat SaaS at $99–249/mo, which further shrinks the ceiling. Budget for that outcome now.

**Timing objection:** The Genstar churn window is real but it fuels the *replatform* race (TeamLinkt/Waresport/Sketchplay et al.), not this wedge; the wedge's timing case is merely "incumbents haven't shipped recovery yet," which is a countdown, not a wave.

**The pairing question (mandated): is H12-alongside-a-main-bet viable for one person, or a focus trap?** Split ruling:
- **The audit test alone: viable.** Days of founder time, zero build, uses existing relationships, produces pre-registered kill/pass data and warm pilot LOIs. Information per unit effort is the highest in the program, exactly as Manager 2 argued.
- **Anything beyond the test: focus trap.** A "3–6 week" v1 build + support + pilot ops lands in exactly the 90-day window when the main bet needs the founder's whole capacity — and the main bet (Target 1) carries PHI trust-building, pilot ops, and monthly competitive watches of its own. Two products in the first six months for one founder fails the founder-capacity reality check this program wrote for itself.
- **Ruling: TEST-THEN-PARK.** Run the stratified audit test in weeks 1–2. If it fails its thresholds → H12 dies, H13 promotes to first alternate *on paper* (do not start its interview-heavy tests either). If it passes → record the result, collect LOIs, and **park until the main bet passes or fails its 90-day gates.** No parallel build under any result.

**H13 (named replacement) status check:** Both of its kill-risk watches came back cold today — Clio has shipped no AI reconciliation (trust recon remains report-level, V21) and TrustBooks' done-for-you tier remains human-capacity-shaped. H13's window is intact; it remains the stronger *business* (better forcing, better durability, priced displacement at $500–2,500/mo bookkeeping) and the weaker *distribution* story. It stays first alternate; promotes only per the pre-registered mechanism.

### Verdict: **SURVIVES — as a conditional cheap option under a test-then-park constraint**
