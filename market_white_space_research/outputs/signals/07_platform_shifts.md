## S07-1: Amazon's reimbursement regime flip — 60-day claim windows + manufacturing-cost payouts
- Who hurts: Amazon FBA sellers of all sizes (strongest for 7-8 figure private-label and wholesale resellers), plus the agencies/bookkeepers who run their back office.
- The pain: In Oct 2024 Amazon cut the window to claim lost/damaged inventory from 18 months to 60 days, and from March 2025 it reimburses only "manufacturing cost" (excluding freight, duties, prep, labeling) — by default Amazon's own estimate unless the seller uploads documented sourcing costs per SKU. What used to be an occasional 18-month lookback audit (often outsourced) is now a permanently rolling 60-day detection-and-filing discipline plus a new cost-documentation upkeep chore, with denials that must be appealed with invoices and tracking proof. Sellers report ~5% of units going missing in FBA warehouses and reimbursements ~30% below their true landed cost.
- Frequency: Continuous — every shipment, every return, every warehouse "lost" event opens its own 60-day (returns: 60–120-day) clock; cost data upkeep per SKU per sourcing change.
- Economics today: Recovery services charge 25% contingency (Getida standard rate; 10–18% negotiated at volume) — i.e., sellers already hand over a quarter of recovered dollars; payout basis change cuts recoveries "by up to 60% for high-margin products" (SellerMagnet/ecomengine analyses); one complainant: ~5% of units lost with reimbursement ~30% under sourcing cost.
- Current solutions: Getida, Carbon6/Seller Investigators, Refunzo, sellerboard alerts; Amazon's own "automated reimbursements" (sellers on the announcement thread distrust it: "Something tells me that in the matter of 'empty boxes' there will be no change"); spreadsheets + Seller Central reports for the rest. Sources say services now have less to find per case (manufacturing-cost basis) while the 60-day clock punishes anyone auditing quarterly.
- Forcing function: Hard per-claim deadlines (60 days, forfeit after) + direct money movement. Strong.
- Why now: Regime changed Oct 2024–Mar 2025; the old "annual cleanup audit" model is structurally dead; documentation requirement (invoices, tracking, cost files) is new recurring work as of 2025.
- Evidence:
  1. Amazon official announcement + seller replies, "Update on Reimbursement Automation and Eligibility Window" — 18-month window cut to 60 days effective Oct 23, 2024; seller: "Initially, there were no time restrictions... Amazon changed to an 18-month window, and now it's just 60 days." https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/81c3235d-4c44-47ba-96c5-883cecab3244 (2024-08 announcement; replies 2024)
  2. Seller Central thread "New Reimbursement Policy and its consequences for Small Sellers": "this amount is roughly 30% lower than what I actually pay to source these"; "around 5% of my units consistently go missing within Amazon's warehouses"; "about half the time the requests are denied with a vague response." https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/1ce28534-f796-4f93-af3f-11fc33465c91 (2025)
  3. Value Added Resource (news, 2024-12-18): announcement thread drew 60+ downvotes/50+ replies; sellers: "we will give you next to nothing excluding all your branding, packaging, handling and shipping costs"; "A great tool to harvest the Costs of Goods Sold from every seller!" https://www.valueaddedresource.net/amazon-fba-inventory-reimbursement-2025/
  4. Getida pricing — free to join, 25% of recovered funds (10–18% enterprise), first $400 free. https://getida.com/how-we-help/ + https://revenuegeeks.com/software/getida/pricing (2026)
  5. Policy mechanics corroboration (60-day windows by claim type, manufacturing-cost basis, documentation requirements): https://www.ecomengine.com/blog/fba-reimbursement-policy (2025)
- Platform-hostage flag: SEVERE — product would live entirely inside Amazon SP-API/Seller Central and its reimbursement policy could change again at will.
- Scout's confidence: HIGH — official policy text + multi-author seller complaints + an existing 25%-contingency services market to displace.

## S07-2: AI-search traffic collapse — SEO-dependent businesses can't see or influence AI answers
- Who hurts: Publishers (small/independent hit hardest), SEO-dependent SMBs and e-commerce brands, and the SEO agencies whose deliverable (organic traffic) is evaporating and whose clients demand "AI visibility" answers.
- The pain: Google AI Overviews/AI Mode answer queries on-SERP; zero-click share of searches went from ~56% (May 2024) to ~69% (mid-2025) and 68% in early-2026 studies; news-site organic visits fell from ~2.3B to <1.7B in a year; small publishers saw Google Search pageviews −34% Dec 2024→Dec 2025 (Chartbeat via Axios). Businesses have no native measurement (Search Console doesn't break out AI-surface citations) and no playbook, so they're buying "GEO/AEO" retainers and monitoring tools, and re-doing content/schema work continuously. The UK CMA has now forced Google to offer AI-Overview opt-outs and AI engagement metrics — creating a brand-new decision + reporting workflow (opt out and lose citations, or stay in and lose clicks) that every publisher must now operate.
- Frequency: Continuous — monthly client reporting, per-algorithm/AI-model update; CMA consultation/controls landing 2026 add per-site configuration decisions.
- Economics today: GEO retainers $2,000–$8,000/mo mid-market (entry ~$1,000–2,500; enterprise $10k–25k+); in-house: AEO Manager postings at $131k–$171k (Citizens Bank), $75k–95k (Odoo), aggregate $102k–130k (ZipRecruiter); freelance $80–200/hr; and the loss side: 20–40% traffic declines for retailers/news/agencies.
- Current solutions: AI-visibility monitors (Profound, Peec, Otterly, Semrush AI toolkit — a fast-crowding venture category), GEO agencies, DIY llms.txt/schema experiments, and litigation/regulatory pressure (Independent Publishers Alliance/Foxglove CMA + EU complaints). Sources' complaint: monitoring tools tell you you're invisible but not what to do; agencies sell process without proof.
- Forcing function: No compliance deadline — the forcing function is existential revenue loss (and, for UK publishers, a dated CMA control rollout in 2026). Honest label: strong "burning platform," weak calendar.
- Why now: AI Overviews scaled to most queries in 2025; AI Mode launched 2025; CMA requirements (Jan 2026, consultation to Feb 25, 2026) create the first formal opt-out/metrics regime; ad-hoc GEO spend already at agency-retainer scale.
- Evidence:

  2. ## S07-3: Email-platform authentication mandates — Google/Microsoft rules turn deliverability into recurring compliance ops
- Who hurts: SMBs and mid-market senders (e-commerce, SaaS, nonprofits), the MSPs managing dozens–hundreds of client domains, and anyone with fleets of scan-to-email devices/LOB apps on Microsoft 365.
- The pain: Google (Feb 2024, 5k+/day senders) then Microsoft (May 5, 2025) began hard-rejecting unauthenticated bulk mail — Outlook now bounces non-compliant mail at SMTP with "550 5.7.15 Access denied" (no junk-folder grace), so senders discover mid-campaign that revenue mail is dead. Compliance means per-domain SPF/DKIM/DMARC setup plus *ongoing* DMARC report monitoring and sender-source hygiene — a new permanent ops loop. Separately, Exchange Online is disabling Basic-auth SMTP by default at end of Dec 2026 (final removal announced 2H 2027), silently breaking multifunction printers/scanners and legacy apps that email — a per-device migration project MSPs must run across every client.
- Frequency: Per-domain onboarding, then continuous (DMARC RUA report triage weekly/monthly; every new SaaS sender added breaks alignment); device/app migration wave with a Dec 2026 deadline.
- Economics today: MSP-market pricing: $100–$300 setup per domain, $50–$100/domain/mo managed; "done-with-you" enforcement programs at $3,900/mo; multi-tenant platform wholesale $5–50/domain/mo (70–90% MSP margins); non-compliance cost = outright rejected campaigns to Outlook/Hotmail/Gmail consumer inboxes.
- Current solutions: dmarcian, EasyDMARC, PowerDMARC, Red Sift, Valimail, DMARC Report (MSP white-label); SMTP relays (smtp2go-class) and Microsoft HVE for devices; manual DNS + spreadsheets at small MSPs. Sources' gap: tools produce reports someone still has to interpret and remediate per client; device OAuth migration is unowned manual work.
- Forcing function: Hard platform deadlines — May 5, 2025 (passed, enforcement live) and end of Dec 2026 (SMTP AUTH default-off) — plus immediate revenue mail rejection. Strong.
- Why now: Microsoft joining Google (2025) made compliance universal; the Dec 2026 SMTP-auth cliff (timeline re-confirmed Jan 27, 2026) puts a dated migration in front of every M365 tenant with a scanner.
- Evidence:
  1. Microsoft official: "Strengthening Email Ecosystem: Outlook's New Requirements for High-Volume Senders" — 5,000+/day senders must pass SPF/DKIM/DMARC; enforcement switched to outright rejection from May 5, 2025 (550 5.7.15). https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftdefenderforoffice365blog/strengthening-email-ecosystem-outlook%E2%80%90s-new-requirements-for-high%E2%80%90volume-senders/4399730 (2025-04/05)
  2. Microsoft official: "Updated Exchange Online SMTP AUTH Basic Authentication Deprecation Timeline" — unchanged through Dec 2026; disabled by default for existing tenants end of Dec 2026; new tenants can't enable; final removal announced 2H 2027. https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/exchange/updated-exchange-online-smtp-auth-basic-authentication-deprecation-timeline/4489835 (2026-01-27)
  3. MSP practitioner artifact (Marco, 2025-07-01, updated 2026-01): scan-to-email/MFP fleets break; five remediation paths (OAuth per device, relay, HVE, etc.); "modernizing now is cheaper than an emergency project." https://www.marconet.com/blog/microsoft-smtp-auth-deprecation-to-cause-mfp-scan-to-email-disruption
  4. Rejection-wave explainer (Security Boulevard, 2025-04): "550 5.7.15 Access denied, sending domain does not meet the required authentication level" — permanent 5xx, never retried. https://securityboulevard.com/2025/04/microsoft-sender-requirements-enforced-how-to-avoid-550-5-7-15-rejections/
  5. Sender confusion artifact (Microsoft Q&A, 2025-07-03): high-volume sender asking how DKIM temperror interacts with enforcement — community answer explicitly "not an official Microsoft position." https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/4748398/outlook-s-new-requirements-for-high-volume-senders
  6. MSP pricing economics: Red Sift MSP packaging guide + DMARC Report MSP program — $100–300/domain setup, $50–100/domain/mo, $3,900/mo done-with-you, 50% MSP list discount. https://redsift.com/guides/how-to-price-package-dmarc-services-msp + https://dmarcreport.com/msp-partner-program/ (2025–2026)
- Platform-hostage flag: LOW-MODERATE — rules set by a Google/Microsoft/Yahoo cartel, but the work product (DNS, DMARC, device migration) is standards-based and cross-platform.
- Scout's confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH — official mandates + dated cliff + existing paid services; caveat: DMARC SaaS itself is crowded, so the space is the MSP/agency ops layer and device-migration wave, not another DMARC dashboard.

## S07-4: TikTok Shop's back office doesn't exist — settlements, creator commissions, and fee churn reconciled by hand
- Who hurts: TikTok Shop sellers past ~$20–50k/mo GMV, multichannel brands (TikTok + Amazon + Shopify + Walmart), and the e-commerce bookkeepers/CPA firms who inherit their books.
- The pain: TikTok Shop settles net of a fee stack that keeps changing (US referral fee 2%→~8% mid-2024; commission raises Sept 2025; EU rates 5%→9% Jan 2026; co-funded free-shipping subsidies phased out late 2025), with creator/affiliate commission deducted inside each transaction. CPA firms report booking net deposits as revenue understates sales (example: $22k gross → $14k deposit, revenue understated $8k), "nine simultaneous accounting errors per settlement," and creator-commission attribution that "degrades over time" — reconstructing which creator drove which sale months later is "a genuinely painful project." Most sync tools still can't read TikTok settlements, so sellers/accountants run spreadsheet exports "that take hours to format."
- Frequency: Per-settlement (weekly/biweekly payouts), monthly close, quarterly/annual tax (1099-K), plus re-work at every fee change.
- Economics today: Accountant hours on manual settlement splits (described as the largest source of material misstatement in ecommerce financials — ECOM CPA); connector tools $50–300/mo per channel (A2X/Link My Books class); mis-stated books surface in loan underwriting and M&A QoE "within hours"; fee increases themselves compress margins (8% + $0.30/order baseline 2025).
- Current solutions: A2X, Link My Books, Webgility, ConnectBooks (QBO/Xero connectors — TikTok coverage recent/partial), Finaloop/Settle (platform lock-in per ECOM CPA), CPA-built spreadsheet templates, or nothing ("the channel most brands reconcile last, worst, or not at all").
- Forcing function: Tax filings/1099-K, lender and acquirer diligence, and per-settlement money movement; fee churn forces re-mapping. Moderate-strong.
- Why now: TikTok Shop went from zero to marketplace-scale US GMV in under three years; US ownership deal resolved late 2025 removed the "wait and see" excuse; 2025–2026 fee restructurings made margin/fee visibility acute; incumbent connector tools lag the channel.
- Evidence:
