# Scout 11 — services_to_software (Productized-services collapse)

**How I hunted (5 lines):** Started from published price lists of services SMBs already buy (DOT compliance mills, credentialing firms, PA outsourcers, grant writers, accessibility auditors, I-9 services) as WTP proof, then chased independent pain artifacts: fetchable forums (TruckersReport, Hacker News via Algolia API), official/regulator texts (govinfo, FMCSA/CMS/FTC actions, state provider-network docs), salary/job data, and 2025–26 enforcement news. WebSearch budget exhausted mid-hunt; pivoted to WebFetch of known URLs per brief. Reddit, ICE.gov, USCIS.gov, federalregister.gov (HTML), Upwork, and most state AG sites are bot-blocked — noted where this thinned a candidate. Richest vein by far: **health-plan administrative middleware** (credentialing + prior auth: per-unit service prices, forced money movement, fresh CMS deadlines) followed by **small-carrier FMCSA compliance** (recurring filings + 2025–26 registration-system upheaval). ## S11-1: Small-carrier FMCSA/DOT compliance filings & driver files
- Who hurts: Owner-operators and small motor carriers (1–20 trucks) — especially new authorities in their first 12 months, and the ~800,000 existing registrants facing FMCSA's new identity re-verification. Buyer = the owner who drives the truck.
- The pain: Getting and keeping authority means a standing lattice of filings and files: BOC-3, UCR (annual), MCS-150 (biennial), quarterly IFTA, driver qualification files (application, MVR, med cert, CDL copy, pre-employment drug test, Clearinghouse query), drug/alcohol consortium enrollment — then a New Entrant Safety Audit inside 12 months where a single missing paper in 16 regulation areas is an automatic fail that can end the authority. Meanwhile new carriers are bombarded by predatory filing mills charging $95–$170 for filings that are free or ~$46 at the source, so the trust vacuum is itself part of the pain.
- Frequency: Quarterly (IFTA) + annual (UCR, due Dec 31) + biennial (MCS-150) + per-hire (DQ file) + continuous (random testing pool, Clearinghouse) + one-time audit in months 1–12 + a 2026 re-verification/Motus migration event for every registrant.
- Economics today: Compliance services price at $50–$250/driver/month (basic vs full-service; 5-truck fleet $500–$1,250/mo) with $500–$2,000 setup fees (Moving Authority pricing page). UCR filing resold at $99/yr against a ~$46 state fee for 0–2 vehicles (start4truckers). Scam mills extract $95–$170 per unnecessary filing (TruckersReport). Failing the new entrant audit costs the operating authority itself.
- Current solutions: J.J. Keller/Foley-class service subscriptions (Foley sells driver-file management, consortium, physicals, MVR monitoring; its own customer testimonial says the service "cuts the time I spend managing driver files in half, at least" — i.e., still a half left); third-party filing mills of varying legitimacy; DIY binders + FMCSA portals, which forum posters say is free but confusing enough that scams thrive.
- Forcing function: Hard. Federal deadlines with fines and out-of-service/authority-revocation consequences; automatic-fail audit in year one; UCR enforcement at scale houses. New in 2025–26: IDEMIA identity verification (from Apr 1, 2025) and the Motus registration system (soft-launched Dec 2025; all regulated entities Q2 2026; legacy systems retired May 14, 2026) force every carrier through a new digital gate.
- Why now: FMCSA's fraud crackdown roughly halved newly published authorities in 2025 (cleaner, more verifiable market); Motus migration + planned re-verification of ~800k registrants creates a one-time compliance surge and obsoletes old filing-mill workflows; enforcement climate raises the price of sloppy files.

## S11-2: Payer credentialing & enrollment for small practices
- Who hurts: Solo/small medical, behavioral-health, and therapy practices; practice managers of small groups; every newly hired clinician whose start date slips. (Regulated-practice gate: this is administrative enrollment work, NOT the practice of medicine — no licensure gate on the service itself.)
- The pain: Every provider × every payer requires a CAQH profile kept re-attested, a payer-specific application, document collection (license, DEA, malpractice, work history), then 90–180 days of status-chasing phone calls before the provider may bill. A therapist's DIY guide describes the workflow as visiting each insurer's site, completing CAQH, then "proactively contact[ing] insurers via phone and email" until contracts arrive. Reinstatement after a lapse can restart the whole cycle. State regulators document the damage: Nevada's provider-network review records credentialing delays of 6–9 months before providers can see patients.
- Frequency: Per provider-payer pair at every hire; CAQH re-attestation ~every 120 days; recredentialing every 2–3 years; continuous roster/directory maintenance.
- Economics today: Services charge $100–$300 per provider per payer application (Medwave); $1,500–$3,500 for initial multi-payer credentialing of one provider (PayerReady); outsourcing runs "$150 per payer application to $500+ per provider per month" (Physician Practice Specialists). The enrollment gap is the real bill: vendor-published estimates of $6,000–$15,000+/month of lost billings per waiting provider. In-house alternative: a credentialing specialist at ~$46k average salary (Indeed; Robert Half range ~$43,750–$57,000).
- Current solutions: Outsourced credentialing firms (per-app pricing above); in-house specialist + spreadsheets + CAQH; enterprise platforms (Medallion, Verifiable, CertifyOS) which sell to health systems and digital-health companies, not 2-provider practices; for therapists, Headway/Alma do paneling as a loss-leader in exchange for running billing through their networks — the DIY guide's stated reasons to credential independently include "protection against potential platform shutdowns."
- Forcing function: Money movement — a provider generates $0 from insured patients until enrollment is approved; payer and CAQH deadlines; recredentialing lapse = claim denials.
- Why now: Payer timelines worsened post-2020 while small groups multiplied (behavioral-health boom); enterprise credentialing SaaS got funded but left the long tail unserved; LLM agents can now do the actual work (form assembly, portal follow-up, status calls) that per-app services bill piecework.
- Evidence:

  2. "$1,500 to $3,500 for initial credentialing across all payers"; enrollment-gap revenue loss "$8,000-$15,000 per month for a primary care provider and over $30,000 per month for specialists" — https://payerready.com/blog/how-much-does-credentialing-cost-per-provider (accessed 2026-08-27)
  3. "$150 per payer application to $500+ per provider per month" outsourced — https://physicianpracticespecialists.com/credentialing/how-much-does-physician-credentialing-cost/ (accessed 2026-08-27)
  4. ## S11-3: Prior-authorization operations in small specialty practices
- Who hurts: Small/independent practices in PA-heavy specialties (imaging, oncology, rheumatology, dermatology, GI, behavioral health) and the MAs/billers who run their PA queues. 40% of physicians report staff who work on PA exclusively.
- The pain: Per order: check if PA is required, assemble clinical documentation to payer criteria, submit via portal/fax, chase status, schedule peer-to-peer when denied, resubmit. AMA's 2025 physician survey (n=1,000): 40 PA requests per physician per week consuming 13 hours of physician+staff time; 94% say PA contributes to burnout; 74% say denials have increased over five years. (Gate note: submission/status work is clerical; clinical-necessity argumentation for appeals borders on practice of medicine — a systematized offering keeps the clinician signing.)
- Frequency: Per-order / per-claim; ~40/week per physician (AMA), i.e., daily operational load.
- Economics today: Outsourcers bill $5–$15 per PA request or $1,000–$1,500+/month for a dedicated offshore PA specialist; in-house handling is estimated at $80–$100 per request all-in; at AMA's 13 hrs/wk, a practice burns roughly a third of an FTE per physician on PA.
- Current solutions: Manual payer portals + fax; outsourcing firms (per-request pricing above); payer-side and health-system ePA platforms (Cohere-class) that don't sell to a 3-doctor practice; physicians report fear that payer AI increases denials (6 in 10 concerned, AMA). The June 2025 insurer "reform pledge" is, per AMA's 2026 survey release, not yet felt by physicians.
- Forcing function: Per-claim money movement (no PA = no procedure revenue, or clawback) + regulation: CMS-0057-F requires impacted payers to give specific denial reasons and decide in 72 hours (urgent) / 7 days (standard) from Jan 1, 2026, and to expose FHIR Prior Authorization APIs by Jan 1, 2027 — creating, for the first time, a standard machine interface a small vendor can build against.
- Why now: CMS-0057-F API deadline (Jan 2027) converts portal/fax labor into a programmable surface; AMA data shows the burden still rising through 2025 despite pledges; outsourcing prices prove per-unit willingness to pay.
- Evidence:
  1. AMA 2025 survey: 40 PAs/physician/week; "13 hours" weekly physician/staff time; "two in five physicians (40%) employ staff dedicated exclusively to prior authorization"; 94% burnout link — https://www.ama-assn.org/press-center/ama-press-releases/ama-survey-prior-authorization-reform-pledge-falls-short-physicians and survey PDF https://www.ama-assn.org/system/files/prior-authorization-survey.pdf (released 2026-05, 2025 data)
  2. AMA practice-management feature: "Nearly 40 prior authorizations a week is way too many" — https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/prior-authorization/fixing-prior-auth-nearly-40-prior-authorizations-week-way (accessed 2026-08-27)
  3. Outsourcing price: "vendors typically charge $5–$15 per request"; in-house "$80 and $100 per request" — https://www.hireinsouth.com/post/prior-authorization-outsourcing (2026)
  4. Dedicated-specialist price: "a dedicated white-label PA specialist starts around $1,000–$1,500+/month" — https://www.verimedix.com/prior-authorization-outsourcing-costs-staffing-2026/ (2026)
  5. CMS-0057-F: 72-hr/7-day decision windows + specific denial reasons effective Jan 1, 2026; four FHIR APIs incl. ## S11-4: Grant writing for small nonprofits (+ SBIR startups)
- Who hurts: Nonprofits under ~$1M budget (52% of small orgs are all-volunteer per State of Grantseeking data) where the ED writes grants after hours; also SBIR-seeking startups buying $10k+ proposal consulting.
- The pain: A foundation proposal takes 20–40 hours, a federal one 60–200+ hours, and most content is structured reuse — need statements, logic models, budgets, bios — reassembled per funder portal and deadline. Orgs face a documented catch-22: "organizations need funding to hire staff, yet require staff to secure that funding" (Foundation Group), and the commission workaround is ethically barred and unallowable under federal rules (FAR 31.205-33(f) for SBIR). Submitting more is statistically decisive — 25% of one-application orgs won nothing vs 6% of 3–5-application orgs — but capacity caps volume.
- Frequency: Per-deadline, recurring; portfolios of several to dozens of applications per year; SBIR agency cycles (NIH/NSF/DoD) 1–3×/yr each.
- Economics today: $1,500–$5,000+ per proposal freelance; retainers $1,800–$3,800/month; in-house grant staff support $87,000–$117,000+/yr (2025); federal specialists $150–$250/hr; SBIR: Grantify $10k+ flat, or contingency 4–10% of award ($10,240–$25,600 on a $256k Phase I) that FAR makes unallowable from award funds.
- Current solutions: Freelancers/consultants (rates above); Instrumentl-class discovery subscriptions; new AI drafters (Grantable, Grantboost); training programs telling orgs that can't afford writers to "focus instead on building your capacity and skills." Sources' complaint: services are per-proposal expensive, commission is prohibited, and in-house staff is a $87k+ commitment small orgs can't make.
- Forcing function: Deadline-shaped but discretionary — nothing fines an org for not applying; the compulsion is financial survival, not regulation. (Honest: weakest forcing function among my candidates.)
- Why now: 2024–26 federal funding volatility pushed nonprofits toward more, smaller applications; LLMs already write passable first drafts, but the service line (price lists above) hasn't repriced — a margin gap an agent-heavy team could collapse. Category is crowding with AI drafting tools; the unserved wedge per evidence is the full outsourced pipeline (discovery→draft→submission→reporting) at sub-consultant prices.
- Evidence:
  1. "Freelance or project-based grant writing often ranges from $1,500 to $5,000+ per proposal"; "Retainer-based grant consulting often ranges from approximately $1,800 to $3,800 per month"; in-house "$87,000–$117,000+" (2025) — https://www.carpenternonprofitconsulting.com/how-much-does-it-cost-to-hire-a-grant-writer (accessed 2026-08-27)
  2. ## S11-5: Accessibility audits & conformance paperwork (WCAG/ADA/EAA + VPAT/ACR)
- Who hurts: E-commerce SMBs (~70% of 2025 digital-accessibility suits), SaaS vendors selling into government/enterprise (VPAT/ACR demanded in procurement), agencies servicing both, and any company selling digital services into the EU post-EAA.
- The pain: Expert audits are "custom and fully manual" — per-page/per-screen review against WCAG, issue write-ups, remediation guidance, re-test, then conformance paperwork (VPAT→ACR; EAA conformance documentation per member state). Litigation exposure is recurring, not one-shot: 1,427 of 2025's 5,000+ suits hit companies already sued before (46% of federal cases are repeat defendants), and an estimated 35,000–50,000 demand letters dwarf the suit count. The EAA adds EU-wide obligation with country fine schedules and market-withdrawal powers; French lawsuits began Nov 2025 and non-filers of conformance reports were prioritized for spring-2026 audits.
- Frequency: Per-release/per-redesign audits; annual re-certification norms; per-procurement VPAT requests; litigation/demand-letter events continuous (tens of thousands/yr).
- Economics today: Published price list: $100–$250 per primary page/screen, $25–$100 light pages, typical engagement $1,250–$2,750, VPAT +$350 (Accessible.org, updated Jul 2026); manual audits range to $12,500+ and enterprise audits $50k+; EAA fines up to €1,000,000 (Spain), €1,260,000 (Hungary), 5% of turnover (Italy); US suits settle atop defense costs.
- Current solutions: Overlay widgets — discredited: FTC ordered accessiBe to pay $1M (Jan 2025, final Apr 2025) for claiming its AI widget made sites WCAG-compliant "within 48 hours" when it "failed to make many basic website components accessible"; automated scanners (axe-class) that leave manual review to consultants; boutique audit firms and enterprise vendors (Level Access) priced above SMBs; law firms for the demand letters.
- Forcing function: Hard and double-jurisdiction: EAA in force since 2025-06-28 with active 2026 enforcement (fines, audits, naming); US ADA private-litigation machine (5,000+ suits, 35–50k letters/yr); procurement gates (no VPAT, no deal).
- Why now: EAA enforcement began in earnest 2025–26; FTC killed the "cheap overlay" escape hatch in Jan 2025, re-routing demand toward real audits; audit work itself (crawl → judge against fixed criteria → write findings → fill VPAT) is precisely the repetitive expert labor agent teams can compress under human sign-off.
- Evidence:

  2. Manual-labor structure of audits: "manual accessibility audit costs generally range from $500 to $12,500 and beyond"; audits are "custom and fully manual" — https://www.digitala11y.com/how-much-does-a-web-accessibility-audit-cost/ (2026)
  3.   4. ## S11-6: Catch-up & cleanup bookkeeping (the post-Bench mess)
- Who hurts: SMBs and startups months-to-years behind on books at tax time, raising or selling (diligence), or stranded when their provider dies — Bench's Dec 27, 2024 shutdown cut off thousands of customers from their books and tax docs mid-engagement. (Gate note: bookkeeping is unlicensed in the US; signed tax returns require PTIN/EA/CPA — a software-with-service model needs a credentialed tax partner for filings, recorded per brief.)
- The pain: Reconstruct months of transactions across bank/card feeds, categorize, reconcile, and produce financials clean enough to file taxes or survive diligence — under deadline. Stranded Bench customers: "I paid them ~$4500 to do all my taxes and book keeping... What recourse do I have to get that money back?" (HN). Migration pain locks customers into failing providers ("I was going to stay the coming year solely because of the timing to move and set up elsewhere"). Competitors confirmed the demand instantly: Pilot's founder on the thread: "we can do 2024 catch-up work," adding "it's worth building some bespoke stuff for this."
- Frequency: Episodic per business but a constant market inflow: every tax season, every funding/diligence event, every provider failure/churn; monthly close cadence once caught up.
- Economics today: Bench's relaunched list prices: $199/$399/$599 per month tiers ($1,910–$5,750 billed annually); QBO-certified bookkeeper $55/hr + $1,200 onboarding; the HN customer's ~$4,500/yr all-in datum. Bench raised $113M and still failed on human-delivery economics — the services line exists, priced, and structurally broken.
- Current solutions: Bench-class human services (fragile — insolvency, fire-sale to Employer.com); local bookkeepers/CPAs at hourly rates; DIY QBO; new AI bookkeeping startups. Insider/engineer verdicts on why services-at-software-prices failed pre-agents: "a software company... [and] a division of bookeepers... These tasks are at odds with one another" (self-described insider); execs "delusional about how much of a human process could be automated" (mjr00).
- Forcing function: Tax filing deadlines (Mar/Apr + extensions), lender/investor diligence, provider-collapse events. Moderate — real deadlines, but no regulator fines books that are merely late until tax filings slip.
- Why now: Bench's Dec 2024 collapse is a natural experiment: demand at $250–$500/mo is proven, human cost structure failed, and 2025-era agent stacks attack exactly the categorization/reconciliation labor the insiders said couldn't be automated. Crowding caution: AI bookkeeping is a hot 2024–26 category; the evidenced underserved slice is deadline-driven catch-up/cleanup projects, which incumbents treat as bespoke.

## S11-7: I-9 / employment-eligibility file compliance for SMB employers
- Who hurts: SMB employers in enforcement-exposed sectors — construction, hospitality, food processing, staffing, agriculture, trucking — with paper I-9 files and high turnover. - Frequency: Per-hire continuous; reverification deadlines; audit events episodic but campaign-driven in 2025–26 enforcement climate.
