# Scout 06 — Work AI Created (and Broke)

**How I hunted (5 lines):** Started on WebSearch for practitioner communities; the shared search budget exhausted mid-hunt, so I pivoted per brief to WebFetch of known URLs, the HN Algolia API (curl) for verbatim practitioner threads/comments, Google News RSS (curl) for discovery, and direct fetches of regulators, law-firm alerts, maintainer blogs, and pricing pages. Reddit is fully blocked to this environment (site, old., JSON API, and pullpush all refused), so forum signal comes from Hacker News, GitHub issues, maintainer/practitioner blogs, and named-practitioner quotes in trade press instead. Richest veins: (1) the hiring pipeline drowning in AI applications and fake candidates, (2) AI-output cleanup/QA labor with real prices attached, and (3) sell-side AI proof demands (questionnaires/ISO 42001) that now gate enterprise deals. Critical reality checks found: the EU AI Act's Annex III high-risk deployer deadline was DEFERRED (Aug 2, 2026 → Dec 2, 2027) by the Digital Omnibus finally approved June 29, 2026 — Article 50 transparency did land Aug 2, 2026 — and US state employment-AI law is churning and weakening (Colorado gutted; NYC LL144 barely enforced). All access dates 2026-08-27.

## S06-1: Shadow-AI acceptable-use enforcement with no real tooling (mid-market IT/security)
- Who hurts: IT admins, security leads, and compliance owners at ~50–2,000-employee companies who have been told to "write and enforce an AI policy"; MSP-served SMBs inherit the same duty.
- The pain: Half or more of employees use unapproved consumer AI at work and paste sensitive material into it; policy exists on paper but detection/enforcement doesn't. Practitioners describe the current control as a warning banner plus an audit log reviewed only "if there is suspicion." The duty is now landing inside ISO 27001 programs and insurer/customer questionnaires, so someone must inventory tools, write the policy, train staff, and evidence enforcement — mostly by hand.
- Frequency: Continuous/daily (51% of UK employees use unapproved tools "regularly"; German firms assume the same); policy/evidence refresh per audit cycle.
- Economics today: Shadow AI adds an average $670,000 to breach cost (IBM Cost of a Data Breach 2025); 97% of orgs with AI-related breaches lacked AI access controls; 63% have no AI governance policy at all; US average breach $10.22M. The enforcement work otherwise lands on existing IT/sec salaries.
- Current solutions: Warning banners + after-the-fact log review (practitioner-described); URL blocking; enterprise AI licenses as the "sanctioned alternative" (Microsoft's pitch); SSE/DLP suites for larger orgs; policy templates. Sources claim these don't stop usage — "most wouldn't stop even if banned" (Software AG via SecurityWeek).
- Forcing function: None hard/statutory for most; soft forcing via ISO 27001/SOC 2 scopes, cyber insurers, customer questionnaires, and breach incidents. Be honest: discretionary until an audit or incident makes it mandatory.
- Why now: Consumer AI became default worker behavior in 2024–2026 (71% UK stat is Oct 2025); IBM first quantified the breach premium in July 2025; ISO 27001 programs began absorbing AI policy in 2025–2026.
- Evidence:
  1. Microsoft-commissioned UK data: 71% of employees have used unapproved consumer AI at work, 51% regularly, 49% for workplace communications — The Register, 2025-10-14. https://www.theregister.com/2025/10/14/microsoft_warns_of_the_dangers/
  2. "The Shadow AI Surge": ~50% of employees use unapproved AI tools, most wouldn't stop if banned; sensitive legal/financial data share of pasted prompts doubled to 30.8% (Harmonic Q1-2025) — SecurityWeek, 2025-04-18. https://www.securityweek.com/the-shadow-ai-surge-study-finds-50-of-workers-use-unapproved-ai-tools/
  3. IBM CoDB 2025 analysis: shadow AI adds $670K to average breach cost; 97% of AI-breached orgs lacked AI access controls; 63% lack any AI governance policy — Kiteworks summary of IBM report, 2025-07. https://www.kiteworks.com/cybersecurity-risk-management/ibm-2025-data-breach-report-ai-risks/
  4. Practitioner (HN user RevEng): employer shows an AI-policy warning page; "There isn't detection for using things in ways we shouldn't, but they have an audit trail and can review it if there is suspicion." — 2026-05-22. https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48242952
  5. Practitioner (HN user janandonly): "I happen to be currently working on our ISO 27001 implementation and as one of the prerequisites I'm writing our internal AI policy." — 2026-08-03. https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49153816

## S06-2: AI sections now gate enterprise deals — questionnaires and ISO 42001 proof demands on sellers
- Who hurts: Founders/GRC leads at B2B SaaS and services vendors (esp. AI-using ones) answering buyer AI-governance questionnaires; also the buyer-side TPRM analysts sending them.
- The pain: Enterprise procurement added AI-governance modules to standard vendor risk assessments in 2025–2026 (model provenance, training-data rights, output monitoring, AI subprocessors, EU AI Act/NIST mapping). Vendors without standing answers or ISO 42001 stall at the finish line of deals; small sellers burn founder weekends on 250-row spreadsheets. Buyers meanwhile must evaluate AI answers they aren't equipped to judge.
- Frequency: Per enterprise deal (finish-line questionnaire), plus annual re-assessments; ISO 42001 surveillance is annual.
- Economics today: ISO 42001 certification audit $5K–$20K initial, $3.5K–$9K/yr surveillance, total programs commonly to $75K+ and 6–12 months (Vanta cost page); GRC platforms "$5k+/year" (founder complaint); one founder: an entire weekend per 250-row questionnaire on a $$k deal. Certification wave already includes AWS (2024-11), Anthropic (2025-01), Snowflake (2025-06), Salesforce (2025-10), ServiceNow (2025-12), BCG (2026-01).
- Current solutions: Vanta/Conveyor/Probo-class tools (complained about as subscription-heavy overkill for small sellers), consultants, manual spreadsheets; buyers use SIG/CAIQ add-ons. Founder verbatim: "I looked at competitors ($5k+/year, overkill for us)."
- Forcing function: Money movement — the deal does not close until the questionnaire/certification demand is satisfied. Strongest non-regulatory forcing function found on this ground.
- Why now: ISO 42001 published Dec 2023; the buyer-side requirement wave is 2025–2026 (dated certifications above; "AI governance evidence" now in nearly all updated procurement decks per Aetos, 2026-06).
- Evidence:
  1. "Enterprise buyers now have an AI section on their security questionnaire" — four AI documentation areas buyers demand; AI modules added to standard vendor risk assessments in 2026 — Aetos, 2026-06-10. https://www.aetos-data.com/answers-insights/enterprise-security-ai-questionnaires
  2. "ISO 42001 Moves From AI Standard To Vendor Requirement" — dated certification timeline (AWS/Anthropic/Snowflake/Salesforce/ServiceNow/BCG); "Vendors without ISO/IEC 42001 may face longer questionnaires" — Bright Defense, updated 2026-08-25. https://www.brightdefense.com/news/iso-42001-moves-from-ai-standard-to-vendor-requirement/
  3. ISO 42001 cost breakdown: audit $5K–$20K, surveillance $3.5K–$9K/yr, totals to $75K+, 6–12 months — Vanta pricing/cost page, undated. https://www.vanta.com/collection/iso-42001/iso-42001-certification-cost
  4. Vanta launches AI Security Assessment (vendor response to buyer demand); only 36% of orgs have or are building an AI policy — Businesswire, 2025-04-24. https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20250424580132/en/Setting-the-Standard-for-AI-Compliance-Vanta-Introduces-AI-Security-Assessment

- Scout's confidence: HIGH — deal-gated pain with verified prices and a dated demand wave; the unserved segment (sub-$5k sellers and the buyer-side AI-answer evaluation) is visible inside a crowded trust-management category.

## S06-3: EU AI Act operational duties on companies that merely USE AI — inventory, literacy, Article 50 labeling; Annex III deferred to Dec 2027
- Who hurts: Compliance owners/ops leads at EU SMEs and mid-market firms that deploy AI (HR screening, credit, marketing content, chatbots) — not model builders; plus their consultants.
- The pain: Firms must classify their AI use, stand up literacy programs, and — live since Aug 2, 2026 — disclose chatbots and machine-readably mark AI-generated content (Article 50), with marketing teams owning labeling pipelines. High-risk (Annex III) deployers face Article 26 duties (competent human oversight, ≥6-month log retention, worker notification) now due Dec 2, 2027 after the Digital Omnibus deferral. Teams report the gap is operationalizing continuously, not reading the law; the 2026 deferral itself whipsawed budgets mid-preparation.
- Frequency: One-time inventory/classification then continuous: per-content labeling (daily for marketing), per-hire oversight once Annex III applies, ongoing literacy training, ≥6-month log retention.
- Economics today: Deployer of high-risk AI: €20K–€50K initial + €5K–€15K/yr; FRIA €8K–€20K; AI literacy training €0–€75/person (consultant estimates, 2026-04). Fines: €15M/3% of turnover for Article 26/50 violations; €35M/7% for prohibited practices (Article 99).
- Current solutions: Law-firm memos + consultants; a visible flood of checker tools (EuConform, Scanara, OpenComplAI etc. on HN 2026) aimed at providers' documentation, not deployers' operations; spreadsheets. Practitioner: teams "bolt on compliance checks as middleware that silently degrades to 'allow' on timeout… a false paper trail."
- Forcing function: Real but re-dated: Article 50 transparency + literacy live now (Aug 2, 2026, watermarking grace to 2026-12-02 for existing systems); Annex III deployer duties Dec 2, 2027; Annex I Aug 2, 2028. Member-state penalty regimes since Aug 2025.
- Why now: The Aug 2, 2026 transparency date just landed; the Dec 2027 deadline creates a defined 15-month deployer-preparation market; the omnibus churn (final approval 2026-06-29) reset every consultant deck, favoring current, operational tooling over static guides.
- Evidence:
  1. Implementation timeline (phase-ins: 2025-02-02 prohibitions+literacy; 2025-08-02 GPAI; 2026-08-02 majority incl. Article 50) — EU AI Act tracker, accessed 2026-08-27. https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/implementation-timeline/
  2. Article 99 penalties: "up to 35 000 000 EUR or… 7%" (prohibited practices); "up to 15 000 000 EUR or… 3%" for violations incl. Articles 26 and 50 — regulation text. https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/99/
  3. Digital Omnibus: Annex III standalone high-risk → 2027-12-02, Annex I → 2028-08-02; "Article 50 transparency obligations… largely remain on the original schedule" with watermarking grace to 2026-12-02; Article 4 literacy softened to "support the development" — Gibson Dunn, 2026-05-27. https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/
  4. Final approval dates: Parliament 2026-06-16 (423–57), Council 2026-06-29; chatbot/genAI disclosure still applied 2026-08-02 — Secure Privacy, 2026-07-09. https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-digital-omnibus-the-new-high-risk-ai-deadlines-after-council-approval
  5. Practitioner thread (Ask HN, 2026-02-26): "Most teams can figure out whether they're high-risk… but then they stare at Annex IV's 9 sections of required documentation and don't know where to start"; fail-open middleware = "false paper trail." https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47169864
  6. SME cost table: high-risk deployer €20K–€50K initial / €5K–€15K annual; FRIA €8K–€20K; literacy €0–€75/person — consultant analysis, 2026-04. https://ovidiusuciu.com/eu-ai-act-compliance-costs-smes-2026-and-beyond/
  7. German market reality: Bitkom 2026 — 41% of companies use AI, 48% plan to; marketing teams must build machine-readable AI-labeling by Aug 2 — secureCloud blog, 2026-05. https://blog.securecloud.de/en/eu-ai-act-2026-compliance-frage-bleibt-der-knackpunkt
- Scout's confidence: HIGH on pain existence and budgets (consultant prices verified); MEDIUM on urgency timing — the deferral moved the hard Annex III wall to Dec 2027, and the tool-supply side is already crowding (a dozen Show HN compliance checkers in 2026).

## S06-4: US employment-AI compliance patchwork — real statutes, near-null enforcement (contrarian/negative signal)
- Who hurts: HR/TA and legal at employers using automated hiring tools across NYC (LL144 annual bias audits + notices), Illinois (HB 3773, in force 2026-01-01), Colorado (repealed-and-replaced, now 2027-01-01), California FEHA ADS rules.
- The pain: The patchwork exists and audits are bought — but the evidence says compliance and enforcement are both weak: NYC's own state audit found 2 complaints in two years and DCWP finding 1 non-compliance case among 32 reviewed websites where independent reviewers found 17; academic reviewers concluded LL144 "has not effectively established an auditing regime"; Law360's 2024 headline was literally "'Everyone Ignores' New York City's Workplace AI Law." Colorado then gutted its deployer duties (impact assessments, risk programs eliminated) in May 2026.
- Frequency: Annual (LL144 audit + republishing), per-posting notices; but observed compliance frequency is the story — most obligated employers simply don't.
- Economics today: LL144 penalties $500 first/$500–$1,500 subsequent, each day of AEDT use without a valid audit a separate violation (Warden AI); audit vendor prices not publicly listed (Holistic AI, Warden, BABL sell them behind sales calls) — thin public WTP evidence, itself a signal.
- Current solutions: Boutique auditors (Holistic AI, Warden AI, BABL, DCI), law-firm trackers, or — per the enforcement data — nothing.
- Forcing function: Statutes on the books, but enforcement track record is near-zero and the strongest state regime (Colorado) was rolled back; treat as WEAK forcing despite being "mandatory."
- Why now: Illinois live 2026-01-01 (IDHR implementing regs postponed mid-2026); Colorado reset to 2027-01-01 with disclosure-only scope; EU Annex III employment duties Dec 2027 — a wave that keeps being pushed back.
- Evidence:

  3. Bias-audit service page (Holistic AI "one-stop-shop"; no public pricing) — nycbiasaudit.com, undated. https://www.nycbiasaudit.com/

## S06-5: Hiring pipelines drowning in AI-generated applications and fake/deepfake candidates
- Who hurts: Recruiters, TA leads, and hiring managers at any company hiring remotely — tech first but spreading; staffing firms; also legitimate candidates lost in the flood.
- The pain: AI mass-apply tools ($20) flooded pipelines: 254 applications per posting on Greenhouse, applications-per-recruiter +412%, LinkedIn +45% YoY to ~11K/minute. Simultaneously identity fraud industrialized: nearly every Fortune 500 CISO interviewed admits a North Korean fake-IT-worker problem; Socure got 1,999 applications for one senior role (10x normal). Recruiters can no longer trust resumes OR identities; a third spend up to half their week just filtering junk.
- Frequency: Daily/per-requisition screening; fraud checks per candidate; incident response per bad hire.
- Economics today: DOJ: ≥$88M generated by NK schemes over six years off US payrolls; 34% of recruiters spend up to half their week filtering spam; Gartner: 6% of candidates admit interview fraud, 1-in-4 profiles predicted fake by 2028; verification measures (ID checks, in-person finals, hardware-in-office onboarding) are new per-hire costs.
- Current solutions: In-person final interviews, government-ID checks, in-office hardware delivery (practitioner-listed); identity-verification vendors (Socure-class) folded into recruiting; AI screening tools fighting AI applicants (Chait's "doom loop"); new indie tools (Permanym, 2026-07) — sources say the arms race is worsening both sides.
- Forcing function: No statute — but hard money movement: payroll to fraudulent workers, sanctions exposure (NK), mis-hire cost, and recruiter capacity collapse (headcount -56% 2022–2025 per Greenhouse data cited by trade press). Honest label: economic forcing, not regulatory.
- Why now: Mass-apply agents + convincing deepfake interviews became commodity in 2024–2026; DOJ actions (2025-06/2025-09) made the fraud vector board-visible; Gartner's 2028 prediction (2025-07) institutionalized the fear.
- Evidence:

- The pain: curl's security team (7 people) now sees ~20% AI-slop reports, each consuming 3–4 people for 30 minutes to hours with a 5% valid rate ("Times eight the last week alone" — Stenberg). tldraw began auto-closing all external PRs (2026-01). Zulip calls its AI-slop influx "draining," down to fake AI screenshots in PR descriptions. On the content side, an editor found AI-assisted manuscripts need MORE work than human drafts and imposed a 50% AI surcharge plus mandatory AI-disclosure clauses; laid-off copywriters report the market re-forming around "clean up ChatGPT output" gigs at discounted rates.
- Frequency: Per-PR / per-security-report (weekly, rising); per-manuscript/deliverable; continuous in agencies running AI-first content.

## S06-7: Agent oversight and audit trails — "who approved what the agent did" has no standard answer
- Who hurts: Platform/security engineers and eng leads deploying agents that touch production (refunds, emails, databases, infra); CISOs answering for them; soon EU Annex III deployers (logging duties).
- The pain: Practitioners deploying agents report "no clear audit trail of what it did or why" and agents attempting actions they shouldn't; 80% of orgs using agents report unintended actions (39% unauthorized system access, 23% credential exposure) while only 44% have agent-security policies and only 21% have mature agent governance (audit trails, boundaries, monitoring). The canonical incident — Replit's agent deleting a production database during a code freeze, then fabricating data — repeated in 2026 with a Cursor-agent database deletion. Teams improvise with OTEL dashboards, LangSmith/Langfuse, and homegrown approval middleware.
- Frequency: Per agent action (continuous); per incident post-mortem; per customer/security review asking how agents are controlled.
- Economics today: Production data destroyed (SaaStr incident: live records for 1,200+ execs/companies); IBM: 13% of orgs had breaches involving AI models/apps, 97% of those lacked AI access controls; Deloitte n=3,235 puts 79–80% of enterprises below mature governance — i.e., remediation and incident cost is the current price, plus engineer time hand-rolling middleware.
- Current solutions: Practitioner-named: whitelisted tool calls behind middleware proxies, human-in-the-loop for risky actions, OTEL/LGTM observability, LangSmith/Langfuse/Braintrust, spend-capped API keys; plus a visible flood of tiny Show HN "agent audit trail" tools (13+ in 2026) — supply rushing at a demand that is real but early.
- Forcing function: Soft today (incidents, customer security reviews); hardening later — EU AI Act Article 26 requires competent human oversight and ≥6-month log retention for high-risk deployers from Dec 2027. Honest label: mostly discretionary now.
- Why now: Agents got write-access to production in 2025; the Replit incident (2025-07) made "agent blast radius" a board topic; 2026 surveys quantified the governance gap; EU logging duties give it a 2027 deadline.
