# Scout 04 — Vertical SMB Operators Complaining in Public

**How I hunted (5-line summary):** Reddit (all subreddits) is hard-blocked for Anthropic's crawler, and ContractorTalk/LawnSite-class forums are Tollbit-paywalled, so I pivoted per brief to fetchable primary sources: Trustpilot review streams (2026-dated, operator-verbatim), BBB complaint records (ServiceTitan, Mindbody), Capterra review/pricing pages read via search, vendor pricing pages (Jobber, AppFolio, Vagaro), and open industry forums (Mike Holt electricians' forum, BiggerPockets, Garage Journal).
The richest vein by far: **2025–2026 Trustpilot + BBB complaints about contract auto-renewal, early-termination fees, and fee-stacking**, which repeat with near-identical language across every vertical incumbent (ServiceTitan, Mindbody, Toast, AppFolio, Kaseya, Vagaro) — this is one pattern (PE/public-market monetization of locked-in SMBs) expressed in six verticals.
Second vein: the **price ladder squeeze** — solo/micro operators priced out of the bottom tier (Jobber $49→$139→$199 gating; Mindbody $250+; AppFolio 50-unit minimum) who confess to running on Excel/QuickBooks/paper invoice books instead.
Auto repair was hunted but the operator forums (AutoShopOwner 403, iATN paywalled, Reddit blocked) were inaccessible; the Garage Journal thread found was feature-talk only — reported honestly as thin, no candidate forced.
WebSearch budget hit the program-wide cap (200/200) mid-hunt; final verification was completed via direct WebFetch. Access date for all records: 2026-08-27.

## S04-1: Mid-size trades shops trapped in ServiceTitan contracts (exit fees as ransom)
- Who hurts: HVAC/plumbing/electrical/irrigation contractors roughly 5–30 techs who "graduated" onto ServiceTitan; owner or office manager is the buyer.
- The pain: Shops sign a 12-month auto-renewing, per-technician contract ($245–$500/tech/mo cited by pricing analyses; all plans quote-only), then hit failed or months-long onboarding, surprise billing doubling, and add-on ("Pro" module) creep. When they try to leave — even shops that never reached go-live — they are quoted five-figure early-termination fees ($22,000, $50,000, $67,230 in 2026 complaints). The workflow dependency (dispatch, invoicing, payments all inside) makes leaving operationally terrifying even when the price is unaffordable.
- Frequency: Daily operational dependency (dispatch/invoice/payments); billing monthly; the acute pain recurs at every annual auto-renewal window and every attempted exit.
- Economics today: $245–$500 per tech per month plus onboarding fees (Projul pricing analysis, 2026); BBB complainants report ~$3,000 unauthorized overcharges in 3 months, $4,802.50 paid up-front for undelivered onboarding, and ETF demands of $22k/$37k/$50k/$67k (BBB + Trustpilot, 2026).
- Current solutions: ServiceTitan itself; escapees name Housecall Pro and "another CRM" but say switching "takes a lot of work and time" (Trustpilot). Capterra pricing page confirms per-user billing, custom quotes, no free trial.
- Forcing function: Contract auto-renewal dates + ETF money movement create hard decision windows; no regulatory deadline — switching itself is discretionary.
- Why now: ServiceTitan IPO'd Dec 2024 (SEC 8-K press release) and the 2025–2026 complaint stream shows post-IPO monetization (price doubling "out of the blue", add-on gating) radicalizing its small-end customers.

## S04-2: Solo/micro trades priced up the FSM ladder, defaulting to paper + Excel + QuickBooks
- Who hurts: 1–5 person electrical/plumbing/handyman/deck shops; owner-operator does the office work at night.
- The pain: The features a one-truck shop actually needs (two-way SMS, automations, quote follow-ups) are gated 2–4 tiers up the ladder — Jobber's Core is $49/mo for 1 user but two-way texting requires Grow at $199/mo, plus $29–$99/mo add-ons — and the tiers keep repricing upward ("$139 a month on the last increase"). Housecall Pro users report $189 surprise re-charges, $800 processing fees, and being oversold 5-user accounts. Result: many stay on (or regress to) Avery paper invoice books, Excel workbooks with VLOOKUP invoice generators, Google Calendar, and QuickBooks — and keep asking forums "any suggestions for a CRM, just starting out?"
- Frequency: Daily — every quote, schedule change, invoice, and payment; price-increase events every 6–18 months force a re-decision.
- Economics today: $49–$199/mo + add-ons (Jobber pricing page) vs. ~$0 DIY; Jobber processing 2.9% + $0.30; one HCP user hit an $800 processing fee on a single large check; the DIY alternative costs owner evenings (invoices "filled in later from home" — Mike Holt forum).
- Current solutions: Jobber, Housecall Pro ($60/mo entry per forum), Markate, paper 2-part invoice books, Excel + QuickBooks + Google Calendar. Complaints: "they try to squeeze every last dime out of users through add-ons" (Mike Holt thread); "they KEEP raising the price" + reporting lock-in (Trustpilot).
- Forcing function: None — discretionary. (Price-increase notices act as episodic forcing events but nothing external mandates adoption.)
- Why now: 2024–2026 tier repricing at Jobber/HCP (documented $60→$139 jumps) plus AI add-on upsells ($29/mo AI Receptionist) widen the gap between free-DIY and first paid tier; the sub-$50 segment is being actively abandoned.

## S04-3: Boutique fitness/wellness studios strip-mined by Mindbody (contracts, rate hikes, 20% marketplace take)
- Who hurts: Yoga/pilates/dance/ballet studio and small gym owners, typically 1–2 locations, $255–$1,000/mo software spend; also med-spa/wellness businesses on the same platform.
- The pain: 12-month auto-renewing contracts sold without disclosing the renewal mechanics or early-termination fee; rates raised mid-relationship "without warning"; downgrading strips "basic reporting" and even product discounting; Mindbody unilaterally added a 20% "finder's fee" on app-originated purchases; cancellation requests are refused or billed past the cancellation ("charged my new card... a charge I never provided or authorized"). Owners describe being steered onto Mindbody's own payment processor at higher rates.
- Frequency: Monthly billing + per-transaction fees; contract pain at each annual auto-renewal; marketplace fee on every new-client booking.
- Economics today: $255/mo (Sierra Nevada Ballet, BBB); "exorbitantly, overpriced" $250–$1,000/mo (Capterra reviews); one owner paid $2,616 in subscription fees and lost basic reporting on downgrade; 20% finder's fee on purchases; BBB shows 105 complaints in 3 years (37 closed last 12 months).
- Current solutions: Mindbody (dominant marketplace + booking); reviewers cite competitors at ~$34/mo; switching blocked by contract + client-data lock-in. (Competitor blogs — WellnessLiving, vibefam — corroborate but were excluded as vendor-interested sources.)
- Forcing function: Contract auto-renewal windows and processor steering (payment flow control); otherwise discretionary switching.
- Why now: Vista-owned Mindbody's 2025–2026 fee expansion (finder's fee, processor migration pressure) is radicalizing its base — BBB stream is dense with 2026 complaints; consumer-marketplace dependence (client acquisition via the Mindbody app) now costs 20% of first-purchase revenue.

## S04-4: Restaurant operators inside Toast's fee machine (per-location fee stack + processing lock-in)
- Who hurts: Independent restaurant owners, 1–3 locations; also small chains negotiating per-location hardware/software bundles.
- The pain: The advertised POS price bears little relation to real spend — "getting the fuller Toast experience could easily put our costs in the range of $500–$1,000 per month, per location" — because software modules, hardware, and non-negotiable payment processing stack up ("Toast charges for everything"). Two-year contracts carry "huge" ETFs. Operational money movement runs through Toast: a simple bank-details change takes 3–4 weeks; ownership transfers stall deposits. Toast has form on unilateral fees: in July 2023 it added a $0.99 consumer fee to online orders without operator consent, triggering a revolt, a congressional query, and a CEO climb-down within days.
- Frequency: Daily (every transaction pays the processing spread); monthly software billing; contract pain at 2-year renewals; payout timing is weekly cash-flow-critical.
- Economics today: $500–$1,000/mo/location claimed by an operator (Trustpilot); a poke shop "saved over 390 a month switching to square"; ETFs on 2-year contracts; the 2023 $0.99/order fee was pure margin skimmed at the operator's counter until reversed.
- Current solutions: Toast; defectors name Square (cheaper, no contract). Complaints center on the bundle being unauditable and support unreachable during revenue-impacting failures.
- Forcing function: Payment processing lock-in = daily money movement through the vendor; contract ETF windows. No regulatory deadline; switching is discretionary but cash-flow interruptions force action episodically.
- Why now: Public-market margin pressure on Toast (2023 fee experiment; continued 2025–2026 fee complaints) plus congressional attention to POS junk fees; operator trust is visibly broken in 2026 review streams.

## S04-5: Small landlords & sub-50-unit property managers stranded between DIY tools and AppFolio's enterprise floor
- Who hurts: Self-managing landlords and small property-management companies with ~5–100 doors; office = the owner plus maybe one admin.
- The pain: AppFolio requires 50 units minimum and a ~$298/mo floor ("Minimum spend and units apply"), so a 50-door operator pays an effective ~$5.60–$5.96/unit — 4x the advertised rate — while sub-50 operators can't sign at all. Buildium, the mid-market refuge, has a decade-long pattern of stacking price increases ($40→$60→$75/mo documented; "tons of complaints on here about Buildium's frequent price increases"), month-long support tickets, and 4–5 day owner-draw delays. AppFolio monetizes both sides: $700/mo tier required to run interim 1099s, $2.49 tenant ACH debit fees, $1,290 cancellation fees, and a cancellation process "deeply hidden, arduous to complete" that took one PM 4 months.
- Frequency: Monthly rent cycle (collections, owner draws) + annual 1099 filing deadline (January) + lease turnover; support failures recur per incident.
- Economics today: AppFolio $1.40–$5.00/unit/mo with $298/mo minimum (pricing analyses); $700/mo for interim 1099 capability (Trustpilot); Buildium "price is really high every month"; tenant-side junk fees ($2.49/ACH) generate resentment PMs absorb; owner draws delayed 4–5 days = float cost.
- Current solutions: AppFolio, Buildium, Rentec Direct ("better support and lower (and stable) pricing"), TenantCloud/RentRedi-class apps, Quicken/QuickBooks + spreadsheets for the smallest. BiggerPockets forums are full of "which one" threads because none fits the 5–100 door segment cleanly.
- Forcing function: MEDIUM-REAL — monthly rent money movement plus the IRS 1099-NEC/MISC January deadline (a recurring regulatory computation point AppFolio itself upsells against); contract cancellation windows.
- Why now: AppFolio's 2025–2026 upmarket push (50-unit floor, quote-only pricing, fee expansion) actively sheds the small end; Buildium's post-RealPage-acquisition price ladder continues; the abandoned 5–100 door segment is growing as small landlords professionalize.

## S04-6: MSPs under PE-rollup vendors: 3-year auto-renew contracts and billing chaos (Kaseya-class)
- Who hurts: Managed service providers, typically 2–50 staff, running their business on Kaseya/Datto/ConnectWise RMM+PSA stacks; owner signs the vendor contracts.
- The pain: Kaseya's EULA (Nov 2020–Oct 2023) auto-renewed subscriptions "for the greater of the expiring term length or three years" — one-year deals silently became three-year deals — and was only softened after public partner revolt. Operators report invoices not sent for a year then the annual rate charged monthly, free trials that billed immediately for unrequested hardware, and support so bad the best thing one reviewer could say was "my contract is almost over." The MSP is trapped: the RMM/PSA holds every client's monitoring, ticketing, and documentation.
- Frequency: Monthly billing errors per reports; contract pain at each renewal date; support failures per incident (which for an MSP means per client outage).
- Economics today: 3-year commitments (Kaseya says 3-year pricing is "as much as 70% cheaper" — i.e., the 1-year price is punitive); reviewers report being "scammed thousands of dollars"; a year of unsent invoices then 12x billing (Trustpilot). Switching cost = re-onboarding every client endpoint.
- Current solutions: Kaseya VSA/BMS/Datto, ConnectWise; defectors move to NinjaOne/Halo/Syncro-class tools; forums recommend negotiating shorter terms — the Oct 2023 EULA change now allows same-length renewal, but existing 3-year deals ran to expiry.
- Forcing function: Contract renewal dates (hard calendar windows with 5–6 figure implications); otherwise discretionary.
- Why now: Post-Datto-acquisition (2022) integration and PE cost discipline produced the 2023–2025 complaint wave; renewal cohorts from the 3-year era are exiting through 2026 — a large, dated, reachable population actively re-deciding their stack.

## S04-7: Salon/spa/barber owners bled by booking-platform fee creep and marketplace client-capture (Vagaro/Booksy-class)
- Who hurts: Single-location salons, barbershops, spas, lash/nail studios; owner-operator plus 1–10 booth renters or staff.
- The pain: The advertised $23.99–$30/mo subscription becomes a fee lattice: charged for 9,000+ marketing emails that never sent (unresolved 6+ weeks), a "secret" $10/mo cart fee without an online store, marketplace fees billed while the marketplace was turned off, and a 20% cut of first-time-customer bookings — while the platform's SEO makes "our salon appears to have completely disappeared" outside its own app. Multi-location owners are priced "almost like a separate business" per location. Support is voicemail during payroll emergencies. Booksy-side: subscription refund disputes (£130 lost) and "whatever it is it's a rip off."
- Frequency: Monthly subscription + per-booking marketplace take + per-message marketing fees; payout timing per payroll cycle.
- Economics today: Vagaro from $23.99/mo (1 calendar) before add-ons (pricing page); 20% of first-time customer revenue to the marketplace; erroneous multi-thousand-email marketing charges; Booksy ~£24+/mo with disputed charges. For a $60 average ticket, the 20% new-client take is $12/booking — comparable to a junior stylist's commission.
- Current solutions: Vagaro, Booksy, GlossGenius, Square Appointments; owners hop between them — each hop risks losing the client list and reviews held inside the platform (the "disappeared" complaint).
- Forcing function: None — discretionary; the marketplace's ownership of client discovery is the retention mechanism, not contracts.
- Why now: 2025–2026 fee unbundling (marketing messages, cart, marketplace) at Vagaro/Booksy mirrors the Mindbody playbook downmarket; complaints cluster in mid-2026, and client-ownership anxiety is explicit in operator language.

## Ground notes for Manager 1
- **Cross-cutting pattern (feed to clustering):** Six of seven candidates are the same underlying phenomenon — post-2024 PE/public-market monetization of vertical SMB lock-in (auto-renew contracts + ETF + fee unbundling + processor steering). The distinct segments differ in contract severity (ServiceTitan/Kaseya worst) and in whether the platform also owns client acquisition (Mindbody/Vagaro/Booksy marketplaces, Toast ordering).
- **Access constraints:** Reddit (all target subreddits incl. r/msp, r/sweatystartup, r/restaurantowners) is robots-blocked for Anthropic tooling; ContractorTalk/VerticalScope forums are Tollbit-paywalled; AutoShopOwner 403s. Auto repair and agency segments were therefore under-sampled, not proven dry — a scout with Reddit access (or manual browsing) would likely find 1–2 more candidates there.
- **Recency:** All Trustpilot/BBB evidence is 2024–2026 (most mid-2026); two forum artifacts (Mike Holt DIY-invoice threads, BiggerPockets 2017 Buildium thread) are older and used only to establish persistent patterns, with 2026 corroboration alongside.
