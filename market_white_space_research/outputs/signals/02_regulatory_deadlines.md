## S02-1: EAA web-accessibility compliance for SMB e-commerce and SaaS selling into the EU
- Who hurts: E-commerce merchants, SaaS and app businesses above the microenterprise threshold (≥10 staff or ≥€2M turnover) selling to EU consumers — including US/UK companies with no EU presence and no compliance staff; agencies serving them absorb the overflow.
- The pain: Since 28 June 2025 the European Accessibility Act requires e-commerce sites, apps, and consumer digital services in the EU to meet accessibility requirements (in practice WCAG 2.1/2.2 AA via EN 301 549), plus an accessibility statement and ongoing conformity. The workflow is manual: page-by-page audits, remediation tickets, PDF/document remediation, accessibility statements per service, and re-checks every release. Small teams don't know if they're in scope, what "compliant" means, or who (platform vs seller) is responsible.
- Frequency: Continuous — every site release/theme change can break conformity; statements must be maintained; audits recur (typically annual or per major release); per-document remediation for every customer-facing PDF.
- Economics today: Manual audits run $100–$250 per primary page ($1,250–$2,750 typical small-site project, $5k–$50k+ for larger sites); EN 301 549 conformance report $650 on top; PDF remediation $7.50/page; expert support $195/hr (Accessible.org price list). Consultant-reported penalty ceilings €10k–€100k+ per infringement, Italy up to 5% of turnover (Alokai/Bird & Bird summaries).
- Current solutions: Accessibility overlay widgets (accessiBe/UserWay class — sold as one-line fixes), manual audit consultancies, agency retainers, or nothing/ignorance. HN practitioners describe proving compliance itself as audit-and-training cost; eBay sellers openly unsure whether the platform or the seller owns the obligation.
- Forcing function: Real — EU directive in application since 2025-06-28 with member-state fines and market-surveillance authorities; obligation is per-service and ongoing.
- Why now: The deadline passed in June 2025, enforcement/market surveillance started, and the long tail of SMBs (esp. non-EU sellers) is only now discovering scope — while audits remain priced as a per-page human service.
- Evidence:
  1. European Commission AccessibleEU: "EAA comes into effect June 2025 — are you ready?" (2025-01-31) — act applies from 28 June 2025 to consumer electronics, ticketing machines, websites and mobile apps. https://accessible-eu-centre.ec.europa.eu/content-corner/news/eaa-comes-effect-june-2025-are-you-ready-2025-01-31_en
  2. Bird & Bird guide for online retailers (2025): EAA "imposes immediate and ongoing compliance obligations on all retailers and service providers entering into e-commerce transactions with consumers in the EU from 28 June 2025," wherever headquartered. https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/a-guide-to-navigating-the-european-accessibility-act-for-online-retailers-service-providers-and-plat
  3. Accessible.org services price list (accessed 2026-08-27): audits $100–$250/primary page, typical project $1,250–$2,750, EN 301 549 ACR $650, PDF remediation $7.50/page, support $195/hr. https://accessible.org/pricing/
  4. HN commenter bArray (2025-07-02): "Even proving you are compliant is a lot of cost, which includes audits and training staff… some of the changes required are massive, to the point where it may not even be worth existing in the EU market if it's enforced." https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44442473
  5. eBay Community thread "EAA law in the EU and e-commerce platforms" (2025): sellers asking whether eBay "will adapt their site(s), or whether sellers and small SMEs will be responsible for compliance" — unresolved in-thread. https://community.ebay.com/t5/Selling/EAA-law-in-the-EU-and-e-commerce-platforms/td-p/34540058
  6. Alokai EAA 2025 explainer: most member states set penalties €10k–€100k per infringement; Italy turnover-based up to 5%. https://alokai.com/blog/european-accessibility-act-2025
- Scout's confidence: HIGH — official deadline + third-party price list + independent practitioner and seller pain artifacts across three communities.

## S02-2: PCI DSS 4.0 payment-page script security (6.4.3 / 11.6.1) landing on small e-commerce merchants
- Who hurts: Small/mid e-commerce merchants using embedded/iframe payment forms (Stripe, Adyen, etc.) who previously ticked the lightweight SAQ A box, and the developers/MSPs who handle PCI for them.
- The pain: From 31 March 2025, PCI DSS v4.0.1 requires an authorized-and-justified inventory of every script on payment pages with integrity checking (6.4.3) and tamper/change detection with alerting on payment pages and headers (11.6.1). The Council removed these from SAQ A in Jan 2025 but replaced them with a vague eligibility gate — merchants must "confirm that their site is not susceptible to attacks from scripts" — clarified by FAQ only 31 days before the deadline. Merchants must now inventory scripts, justify each, deploy CSP/SRI or monitoring tooling, or extract written attestations from their PSP — none of which small merchants know how to do.
- Frequency: Continuous monitoring plus alert triage; script inventory re-justified at every site change; annual SAQ/attestation cycle with acquirer.
- Economics today: Dedicated script-monitoring tools run ~$99/month at SMB tier (c/side business plan; free tier capped at 2,000 pageviews); QSA consulting on top; the alternative is engineering time to build CSP/SRI + change detection. Non-compliance risks acquirer penalties and losing SAQ A status (fall into far costlier SAQ A-EP/ROC scope).
- Current solutions: Vendor script-monitoring SaaS (c/side, Source Defense, Feroot, Jscrambler), QSA advisory, WAF/pentest workarounds (TrustedSec's listed fallback), processor attestation letters, or quietly ignoring it. QSAs themselves say interpretation is unsettled.
- Forcing function: Real — PCI DSS v4.0.1 requirements effective 2025-03-31; enforced through acquirer/brand compliance programs on every card-accepting merchant.
- Why now: The future-dated v4.0 requirements went live March 2025 and the SAQ A rewrite created mass confusion documented by the Council's own emergency FAQ; small merchants are hitting it at their 2025–2026 attestation renewals.
- Evidence:
  1. PCI SSC blog, "Important Updates Announced for Merchants Validating to SAQ A" (2025-01-30): 6.4.3/11.6.1 removed from SAQ A, new eligibility criterion added, effective 31 March 2025. https://blog.pcisecuritystandards.org/important-updates-announced-for-merchants-validating-to-self-assessment-questionnaire-a
  2. PCI SSC blog, "FAQ Clarifies New SAQ A Eligibility Criteria for E-Commerce Merchants" (2025-02/03): Council forced to clarify what "not susceptible" means weeks before the deadline. https://blog.pcisecuritystandards.org/faq-clarifies-new-saq-a-eligibility-criteria-for-e-commerce-merchants
  3. Scott Helme, "PCI DSS FAQ SAQ WTF BBQ" (2025-03-13): "we went from several pages of nicely defined criteria with guidance, to a single, vague, sentence instead"; clarification came "just 31 days before the deadline"; customers flooded him with is-this-still-required questions. https://scotthelme.co.uk/pci-dss-faq-saq-wtf-bbq/
  4. TrustedSec (QSA), "The Hidden Trap in the PCI DSS SAQ A Changes" (2025-02-06, upd. 2025-03-10): "There will likely be as many opinions on what qualifies as 'not susceptible' as there are people implementing and assessing PCI DSS requirements"; failure of the gate pushes merchants into heavier frameworks. https://trustedsec.com/blog/the-hidden-trap-in-the-pci-dss-saq-a-changes
  5. SecurityMetrics (QSA firm), "Big Changes for SAQ A: 2025 Updates for 6.4.3 and 11.6.1": consultant how-to documenting the new manual work for SAQ A merchants. https://www.securitymetrics.com/blog/big-changes-for-saq-a
  6. c/side pricing page (accessed 2026-08-27): PCI 6.4.3/11.6.1 compliance tooling — free tier to 2,000 pageviews, $99/mo business tier. https://cside.com/pricing
- Scout's confidence: HIGH — official standard change + QSA and independent-practitioner confusion artifacts + tool pricing; the only gap is a verbatim merchant (non-expert) complaint, as Reddit was unfetchable.

## S02-3: EU B2B e-invoicing wave — every invoice becomes a structured, networked filing (DE/FR/BE/PL 2025–2028)
- Who hurts: Freelancers, SMEs and their accountants in Belgium (all VAT businesses from 2026-01-01, via Peppol), Poland (KSeF from 2026-02/2026-04, micro 2027-01), France (receive-all from 2026-09-01; SME issuance 2027-09), Germany (receive since 2025-01; issuance 2027 >€800k, 2028 all) — businesses that today invoice by PDF/email for free.
- The pain: Invoicing stops being a document and becomes a validated data transaction: pick and pay an access point/PDP, register, map invoice fields to EN 16931/Peppol BIS or KSeF schemas, handle validation rejections, and in clearance models route invoices through a government platform before the customer sees them. Freelancers describe being forced from free email invoicing into paid subscriptions and certification regimes; Belgian adoption data shows SMEs lagging badly a year out.
- Frequency: Per invoice, every business day — the highest-frequency forcing function in this ground; plus onboarding (one-time) and per-rejection rework.
- Economics today: HN practitioners cite ~€2,000/yr Peppol membership plus certification for direct access, per-invoice access-point fees, and new paid subscriptions replacing free email; France scrapped its free public platform (Oct 2024) so businesses must buy a certified private platform; Poland imposes penalties from Jan 2027 and invalid invoices can block customer VAT reclamation (Forbes). Non-compliance = unpaid/invalid invoices, i.e., direct cashflow damage.
- Current solutions: Accounting suites bolting on e-invoicing (often country-gapped), standalone access points (Storecove, Peppr-class tools), spreadsheets + accountant, or procrastination — Belgium reported only ~⅓ of SMEs on Peppol vs 70% of large firms. Devs are hand-rolling libraries (multiple Show HN validator/generator tools 2026), signaling tooling gaps.
- Forcing function: Real and layered — statutory dates per country (BE 2026-01-01; PL 2026-02-01/04-01; FR 2026-09-01; DE issuance 2027/2028), fines, and the practical inability to get paid outside the network.
- Why now: 2026 is the crunch year (Belgium live, Poland and France going live); ViDA locks the EU trajectory; SMB adoption is demonstrably behind, and incumbent accounting software is country-fragmented.
- Evidence:
  1. Ask HN: "How are you preparing for PEPPOL?" (2025-01-21, 67 points/76 comments) — OP elric: direct network access "is expensive and requires technical audits"; B1zz3y_: "basic yearly fee of 2000 euros just for the peppol membership and on top of that you need to get certified"; BoredPositron: "I'll suddenly need some third party tool with some kind of paid subscription, to continue to do what I had been doing for free before"; vesinisa: "money is being pulled away from freelancers and small SME's for every invoice they send." https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42777669
  2. Marosa VAT, "E-invoicing B2B and B2G in Europe — overview and dates" (accessed 2026-08-27): DE receive-obligation since Jan 2025, issuance 2027 (>€800k)/2028 (all); FR gradual from Sep 2026; BE "effective since January 2026… exchanged through Peppol"; PL KSeF Feb 2026 (>PLN 200M) then Apr 2026 (all). https://marosavat.com/resources/e-invoicing-in-europe-overview-and-dates
  3. Forbes (Aleksandra Bal), "2026: The Year Mandatory E-Invoicing Sweeps Across Europe" (2025-11-02): "Small traders fear they'll be hit the hardest… the shift feels overwhelming and expensive"; only ~one-third of Belgian SMEs on Peppol vs 70% of large companies; France's Oct 2024 removal of the free public platform forces paid private providers; Polish penalties from Jan 2027. https://www.forbes.com/sites/aleksandrabal/2025/11/02/2026-the-year-mandatory-e-invoicing-sweeps-across-europe/

- Scout's confidence: HIGH — statutory per-invoice forcing function, verbatim SMB/freelancer pain, adoption-gap data, and named costs; the market risk is crowding (ERP/accounting incumbents), not absence of pain.

## S02-4: Companies House identity verification (ECCTA) — 6–7M UK directors/PSCs through a failing funnel
- Who hurts: Every UK company director and person with significant control (6–7M individuals, mostly micro-companies), plus the accountants/company-secretarial firms who must register as ACSPs and shepherd non-digital clients through it.
- The pain: From 18 Nov 2025, new directors must verify identity at incorporation/appointment and existing directors within 14 days of their company's next confirmation statement; PSCs on their own clock. The GOV.UK One Login funnel fails frequently (crashes at final step, photo/lighting rejections, multiple-account linking hell, no passport = manual routes), and every failure lands on an accountant or an ACSP. Firms must run a new client-by-client campaign: identify who's due when, chase, verify or re-verify, record personal codes, and link them to filings — or filings get blocked.
- Frequency: One-time per person in a 12-month wave (Nov 2025–Nov 2026) but recurring forever for every new appointment/incorporation, and deadline-tied to each company's annual confirmation statement.
- Economics today: ACSP verification services charge ~£20–£99 per person (published example: £24.99 standard / £49.99 express / £74.99 international), up to £250 at some providers; non-compliance is a criminal offence with financial penalties at company or officer level and eventual filing blocks; accountancy firms report workload spikes with no matching fee line.
- Current solutions: GOV.UK One Login (free but failure-prone per user reports), ACSP services (per-head fees), IDV vendors sold to firms (Thirdfort-class), spreadsheets to track client due-dates. No dominant tool for the firm-side campaign management (who's due, chased, verified, coded, filed).
- Forcing function: Real — statutory (ECCTA), phased 18 Nov 2025 → Nov 2026, offence + penalties + blocked filings; Companies House says enforcement ramps after the transition year.
- Why now: The 12-month compliance wave is live right now: 1.5M verified by day one, meaning ~4.5–5.5M people must pass through the funnel before Nov 2026, and professional bodies were still reporting faltering compliance in Feb 2026.
- Evidence:
  1.   3. ## S02-5: Making Tax Digital for Income Tax — 864k sole traders/landlords forced into quarterly digital filing (Apr 2026), millions more 2027–28
- Who hurts: UK sole traders and landlords with >£50k income (864k in wave 1 from 2026-04-06; >£30k joins 2027, ≥£20k in 2028 → ~2.9M total), and the small accounting practices that must run 5 filing events per client per year instead of 1.
- The pain: Self Assessment becomes: keep digital records in HMRC-recognised software, file 4 quarterly updates per trade/property business plus a final declaration, with penalty points for misses. Half of mandated taxpayers were still unregistered as the first quarterly deadline passed (Aug 2026); accountants report large non-digital client bases that must be onboarded one by one (software choice, bank feeds, training, chasing records every quarter instead of once in January).
- Frequency: Quarterly per trade/property business + annual final declaration — a landlord with a trade files 8+ updates/yr; practices multiply this by their client book.
- Economics today: HMRC's own estimates: ~£320 transitional + ~£35/yr ongoing per business (gov impact assessment for £50k+ landlords: £285 transitional/£115 annual); software £0–£288/yr (avg £70.73, median £36 across 34 tracked tools, with "£20/month" marketing masking £288/yr real cost); accountants pricing MTD service at typically £250–£400/yr on top of existing SA fees. Multiply by 864k now, ~2.9M by 2028.
- Current solutions: MTD-recognised software list (QuickBooks/Xero/FreeAgent/free tools like Pandle/Clear Books), bridging software over spreadsheets (£20–£60/yr), accountants absorbing quarterly chase work, or non-compliance (half unregistered at deadline). Practitioner surveys: 42% of accountants say more than half their clients still submit nothing digitally.
- Forcing function: Real — statutory from 2026-04-06 with points-based penalties; first quarterly deadline 2026-08-07 already passed with mass non-registration, so a compliance scramble is underway.
- Why now: Wave 1 just went live and visibly failed to onboard half its population; waves 2–3 (2027, 2028) triple the market; HMRC auto-sign-up began Aug 2026.

## S02-6: DORA Register of Information — small EU financial entities failing an annual 15-template xBRL-CSV filing
- Who hurts: Small regulated financial entities — payment/e-money institutions, small asset managers, insurance intermediaries, investment firms — in scope of DORA since 2025-01-17 without compliance engineering teams; their compliance officers and outsourced consultants.
- The pain: Every entity must maintain a Register of Information covering all ICT third-party arrangements — 15 interlinked templates with coded headers, LEI-validated vendor identities, contract dates/values, service-chain and subcontractor mapping — and submit it annually to its NCA in strict xBRL-CSV packaging. The 2025 first cycle was a debacle: only 6.5% of ~1,000 firms passed all 116 data-quality checks in the ESAs' dry run; regulators (e.g. Luxembourg's CSSF) had to publish error-message decoding guides; Excel exports fail encoding/quoting validation; lapsed LEIs cause hard rejections; wrong ZIP folder names kill ingestion before rules even run.
- Frequency: Annual submission (2026 cycle: national deadlines 1 Jan–31 Mar 2026; ESAs cut-off 31 Mar) + continuous register maintenance per new/changed ICT contract + resubmission cycles after rejections.
- Economics today: Effort-based: mapping every ICT contract with procurement/legal, 15-template completion, xBRL-CSV conversion (regulators' free one-time Excel conversion service in 2025 was explicitly not repeated), then error-fix loops; firms buy consultant support (fscom-class advisory) or RegTech automation (Copla, Vendorica) because ESAs "explicitly advise against" manual spreadsheets. No public flat prices found — labeled as a gap.
- Current solutions: The free EBA Excel template + manual completion (advised against by ESAs themselves), consultancy engagements, early RegTech point tools for RoI conversion/validation. Sources say firms "underestimated the effort" and failed on mechanics, not intent.
- Forcing function: Real — DORA applies since 2025-01-17; annual RoI submission with NCA deadlines and supervisory follow-up on failed/poor-quality submissions.
- Why now: First full annual cycle (2026) just ran with documented mass failure; format is stabilizing (xBRL-CSV), making a repeatable software workflow feasible where 2025 was consultancy chaos.
- Evidence:
  1. EBA, "Preparation for DORA application — reporting of registers of information" (official): annual RoI reporting obligation and technical package. https://eba.europa.eu/activities/direct-supervision-and-oversight/digital-operational-resilience-act/preparation-dora-application
  2. CSSF (Luxembourg regulator), "Guidance for interpretation and resolution of CSSF error messages related to the submission of the DORA register" (PDF, 2025): a regulator publishing an error-decoder for its own filing pipeline — direct evidence of widespread submission failure. https://www.cssf.lu/wp-content/uploads/Guidance-for-interpretation-and-resolution-of-CSSF-error-messages-related-to-the-submission-of-the-DORA-register.pdf
  3. fscom, "Preparing for the 2026 DORA reporting deadline: lessons from 2025" (2026-02-19): only 6.5% of nearly 1,000 firms passed all 116 data-quality checks in the ESAs dry run; catalog of real errors (non-xBRL-CSV formats, blank subcontractor chains, duplicate IDs, lapsed LEIs); 2026 national deadlines Mar 1–21 (NL/IE/DE/LU/MT), ESAs 31 Mar. https://fscom.co/blog/preparing-for-the-2026-dora-reporting-deadline-lessons-from-2025-every-firm-should-know/
  4. ## S02-7: NIST 800-171/CMMC compliance for small defense subcontractors — six-figure mandate, ~100 assessors, whiplash rules
- Who hurts: 100k–120k+ small US defense-industrial-base contractors (machine shops, engineering firms, parts suppliers) holding or bidding DoD work that touches CUI/FCI, without IT or compliance staff.
- The pain: CMMC entered contracts 2025-11-10 (Phase 1: mandatory NIST SP 800-171 self-assessment, SPRS score submission, annual affirmations; DFARS 7019/7020 flow-downs), with third-party C3PAO certification (Phase 2) originally set for Nov 2026. The workflow is brutal for a 20-person shop: 110-control gap assessment, SSP and POA&M authoring, evidence collection, enclave/MSP decisions, SPRS scoring, then (until suspended) queueing for ~100 available assessors. In July 2026 DoD abruptly suspended Phase II pending review — baseline self-assessment obligations remain — leaving small subs mid-spend with sunk costs and uncertainty.
- Frequency: Annual self-assessment/affirmation + per-contract flow-down checks + continuous evidence upkeep; (Phase 2, if revived: triennial certification).
- Economics today: DoD's own estimate for a small contractor Level 2 C3PAO certification: ~$104,670 total (assessment $76,743 + prep + reporting + affirmations); Level 2 self-assessment $37k–$49k; consultants $250–$400/hr; compliance MSPs $3k–$25k/month; SBA cites ~$593,800 per third-party certification and ~$388,600 per self-assessment as the burden justifying suspension; survey: 70% of 2,000+ contractors had budgeted under $100k.
- Current solutions: Compliance consultants/RPOs, CMMC-in-a-box MSPs, enclave products (PreVeil-class), GRC tools, DIY spreadsheets against NIST 800-171A — all service-heavy; assessor scarcity (~92–100 C3PAOs) made the certified path arithmetically impossible for the population.
- Forcing function: DOWNGRADED-BUT-REAL — Phase 1 self-assessment/SPRS obligations remain in contracts now (DFARS), and False-Claims-Act exposure attaches to submitted scores; but the Phase II certification deadline was suspended 2026-07, so the hard certification date is currently uncertain. Honest read: mandatory baseline workflow persists; the six-figure certification cliff is paused.
- Why now: Enforcement of the baseline began Nov 2025; the July 2026 suspension creates a window where SMB-priced self-assessment/SSP tooling matters more than certification prep — and any Phase II revival re-arms the cliff.
- Evidence:

## S02-8: GPSR product-safety compliance for micro-sellers shipping into the EU/NI — per-listing documentation and a paid EU "responsible person"
- Who hurts: Non-EU (esp. UK/US) craft sellers, Etsy/eBay/Shopify micro-brands, and small manufacturers selling consumer products into the EU/Northern Ireland — sellers with zero compliance capacity.
- The pain: Since 2024-12-13, GPSR requires every consumer product placed on the EU market to have an EU-established responsible person (for non-EU sellers: a paid authorized representative), risk assessment and technical documentation per product, traceability details (manufacturer name/address/contact) on the product or listing, and marketplace listings updated accordingly — Etsy and other platforms enforce fields and can suppress non-compliant listings. Micro-sellers face a per-product paperwork regime designed for manufacturers; forum threads title it a "Total Nightmare," and many simply stopped selling to the EU.
- Frequency: Per product/listing at creation + per new product + ongoing incident/recall duties; platform enforcement is continuous.
- Economics today: Authorized-representative services ~€50–€500/yr (vendor-published ranges; Etsy partnered with Cert-Rep and International Associates), plus hours of per-listing labeling/documentation work; non-compliance = fines, recalls, delisting or exiting the EU market entirely (lost revenue, which many sellers chose).
- Current solutions: AR service vendors (Cert-Rep, International Associates, 24hour-AR, EUVerify-class), platform help pages, DIY label templates, or geo-blocking EU/NI buyers (Etsy added shop-level EEA/NI restriction settings — itself evidence of mass seller withdrawal).
- Forcing function: Real — EU regulation applicable since 2024-12-13, enforced by market-surveillance authorities and, more immediately for micro-sellers, by marketplace listing requirements.
- Why now: Enforcement and platform tightening ramped through 2025–2026; the seller population is enormous and non-professional; compliance is still handled with PDFs, template packs, and per-head services.
- Evidence:
  1. Regulation (EU) 2023/988 (GPSR), applicable from 13 December 2024 — official text. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/988/oj
  2. Folksy (UK craft marketplace), "GPSR – What you need to know as an artist or maker on Folksy" (2024-11-26): platform how-to translating GPSR into maker-level obligations. https://blog.folksy.com/2024/11/26/gpsr-guide-for-artists-and-makers-on-folksy
