# Scout 03 — Human Middleware Jobs (jobs that are secretly integration software)

**Hunt summary (method + richest vein):**
1. Hunted live US job postings via WebSearch, then pivoted hard to WebFetch when big boards (Indeed/ZipRecruiter/Glassdoor job pages, Lever, CareerBuilder) returned 403s: the bebee.com aggregator and recruiting.paylocity.com serve full posting text and were fetchable, and bebee search pages return total posting counts per query.
2. For every candidate I captured: verbatim duty lines (the "glue work" in the employer's own words), posted pay (the budget proof), the two-plus systems the human bridges, and posting-count scale; access date for all records is 2026-08-27, and nearly all postings were 0–6 days old at capture.
3. Anchored economics with non-vendor primary data where available: AMA 2025 Prior Authorization Physician Survey (press release 2026-05-13) and the CAQH Index ($21B admin-savings opportunity); vendor pages (Conexiom, GCPay, a logistics BPO) are cited only as current-solution inventory, labeled as vendor marketing.
4. Richest vein by far: healthcare revenue-cycle middleware — prior authorization (6,971 open postings on one aggregator), credentialing/payer enrollment (3,990), insurance follow-up (1,177), plus eligibility verification — every posting describes portal→EHR re-keying, faxing, and status-chasing, at $16–$31/hr with revenue directly gated on the work.
5. Non-healthcare veins that cleared the bar: freight track-and-trace (carrier↔TMS↔customer-portal updating, ~$30/hr), PO order entry into ERP at distributors/manufacturers (fax/email/portal→ERP typing, 649 postings), and construction progress billing/lien-waiver coordination (monthly pay-app cycle, $25–$31/hr); reddit was unfetchable in this environment (old.reddit blocked), so worker-side forum threads are absent — postings, salary aggregates, and surveys carry the evidence instead.

**Environment notes for the director:** WebSearch budget for the session was exhausted program-wide mid-hunt (200/200); all subsequent evidence came via WebFetch. cms.gov, federalregister.gov, ncred.net, levelset.com were blocked/dead, so CMS-0057-F (prior-auth API rule, finalized 2024, phased 2026–2027) is referenced as context, not as a ledger row.

## S03-1: Eligibility & benefits verification keying (payer portal → EHR/PM + spreadsheets)
- Who hurts: Front-office / RCM staff at independent and small-group medical practices (spine, cardiology, oncology, pediatric ophthalmology clinics in the postings) — typically 1–3 FTEs per practice dedicated to verification.
- The pain: Before every scheduled visit, a human looks up each patient's coverage on payer websites (Availity et al.) or by phoning the carrier, then re-keys eligibility, copay, and deductible data into the EHR/billing system — and, at one multi-site practice, additionally into spreadsheets distributed to front desks. The same person often also chases PCP referrals. It is pure two-system bridging: payer portal out, EHR/PM + Excel in.
- Frequency: Daily, per-appointment — postings describe "daily verification of active insurance coverage" for all upcoming visits.
- Economics today: ZipRecruiter aggregate: $16–$23/hr, national average $18.87/hr as of 2026-08-07 (≈$39k/yr per head). CAQH Index (2025) frames the macro: a "$21 billion industry savings opportunity" from automating healthcare admin transactions, of which eligibility is the highest-volume type.
- Current solutions: Payer portals + phone + EHR (eClinicalWorks named; Epic/Cerner/Athena in employer guidance) + spreadsheets; clearinghouse eligibility (Availity, Change Healthcare) exists but practices still staff humans to run and re-key checks — the hiring itself is the evidence the API layer doesn't reach them.
- Forcing function: Money-movement gate — unverified coverage becomes denied claims and uncollected patient responsibility; verification is effectively mandatory per visit.
- Why now: Payer portal sprawl and high-deductible plans made per-visit verification standard; eligibility APIs (Availity, Stedi's 2024–2025 clearinghouse push) exist but stop at the portal boundary — the last mile into SMB EHR/PM systems is still human.

## S03-2: Claims status chasing & denial rework (clearinghouse/payer portals → billing system)
- Who hurts: Billing/AR follow-up staff at hospitals, medical groups, and outsourced RCM firms; also solo billers serving small practices.
- The pain: Humans work queues of unpaid/rejected claims: check status by phone and payer portals, read clearinghouse rejection reports, correct codes/demographics in the billing system (Epic named), resubmit, appeal denials, and send secondary claims. Every step is copy-investigate-rekey across payer portal ↔ clearinghouse ↔ billing system.
- Frequency: Daily, per-claim; work is assigned as standing queues ("all outstanding accounts assigned").
- Economics today: Catholic Health posts $24.00–$31.00/hr; ZipRecruiter Texas postings show $17–$20/hr; 1,177 open "insurance follow-up representative" postings on one aggregator (2026-08-27). Outsourced AR-follow-up vendors (e.g., Annuity Health) sell the same human labor as a service — proof of a paid substitute.
- Current solutions: Epic/PM worklists + clearinghouse portals (Waystar/Availity-class) + payer portals + phone; enterprise denial-management modules exist, yet providers and even the outsourcers themselves are hiring humans for the portal/phone last mile.
- Forcing function: Direct money movement plus hard timely-filing and appeal deadlines — miss them and revenue is legally forfeited.
- Why now: Denial rates and payer-portal fragmentation rose post-2023 (AMA/industry reporting), pushing more claims into manual follow-up while RCM staffing stays hard to hire.

## S03-3: Prior authorization chasing (EHR chart → payer portal/fax → track → key back)
- Who hurts: PA specialists/coordinators at physician practices (cardiology, plastic surgery, oncology, community health centers, specialty pharmacy in the postings); 40% of physicians employ staff who do nothing else.
- The pain: A human assembles clinical documentation from the EHR, submits authorization requests "through phone, mail, fax or 3rd party portals/sites," chases status, manages denials and appeals, faxes orders onward, and manually tracks expiration dates of granted auths. Multi-payer portal sprawl plus fax makes this the canonical swivel-chair job.
- Frequency: Continuous: physicians average 40 PAs per week per physician, consuming 13 hours/week of physician+staff time (AMA 2025 survey).
- Economics today: Posted wages $17.90–$25.00/hr (Buffalo NY $17.90–$20.00; Westbrook ME $23.00–$25.00; Gillette WY $18.27–$20.00+). AMA: 40% of physicians employ dedicated PA staff. Scale: 6,971 open PA-specialist postings on one aggregator (2026-08-27).
- Current solutions: Payer portals + fax + phone + EHR task lists; ePA tools exist for pharmacy (CoverMyMeds-class) and enterprise platforms for hospitals, but practice postings still specify manual portal/fax submission; AMA survey documents the burden persisting despite insurer pledges.
- Forcing function: Mandatory payer gate — no auth, no care/no payment; auth expirations create recurring deadlines. Regulatory context: CMS-0057-F (finalized 2024) forces payer PA APIs and decision timeframes in 2026–2027, which will change the integration surface (context; primary text unfetchable — cms.gov/federalregister.gov blocked bots).
- Why now: The June 2025 industry "reform pledge" and CMS-0057-F deadlines are reshaping payer interfaces while AMA's 2026-05 release shows the manual burden unchanged — a moving compliance surface with entrenched manual workflows on the provider side.

## S03-4: Provider credentialing & payer enrollment ops (CAQH/PECOS/payer apps ↔ Excel trackers)
- Who hurts: Credentialing specialists at medical groups, dental/derm/ortho MSOs, telehealth companies, and staffing firms; also billing companies doing credentialing as a sideline.
- The pain: Humans complete initial credentialing and re-credentialing applications payer by payer, keep CAQH profiles attested, enroll providers in PECOS/NPPES/state portals (PAVE), chase providers for documents, respond to payer requests, and hand-track license/contract expirations in logs and Excel. Same provider data re-keyed into N portals, then babysat forever.
- Frequency: Per provider hire and per payer (weeks-long applications); re-credentialing every 2–3 years; CAQH re-attestation quarterly; expirables monitored continuously.
- Economics today: QualDerm posts $48,152–$64,916/yr for a fully remote specialist; LLI Medical Management (El Paso) $10–$25/hr; 3,990 open credentialing-specialist postings on one aggregator (2026-08-27). Until enrollment completes, the provider's claims can't be billed — stalled revenue is the hidden cost.
- Current solutions: CAQH ProView + PECOS + NPPES + payer portals + "credentialing software" + Microsoft Excel/Outlook (named in postings); credentialing SaaS (Modio/Medallion/Verifiable-class) and outsourced services exist, yet SMB provider orgs keep hiring portal operators.
- Forcing function: Revenue gate (cannot bill un-enrolled providers) plus hard expiration/re-attestation deadlines.
- Why now: Provider M&A/MSO rollups and telehealth multi-state licensure multiplied payer-portal permutations since 2023; postings explicitly demand multi-portal fluency (CAQH, PECOS, NPPES, PAVE, Availity).

## S03-8: Referral intake & document chasing in home-based care (fax/portal referrals → EMR + auth)
- Who hurts: Intake coordinators at home health, hospice, and home-infusion agencies (Assisted Home Health & Hospice, AdvisaCare, Aspen Infusion in the postings) — agencies where intake speed decides whether the referral (revenue) is captured or lost to a faster competitor.
- The pain: Referrals arrive by fax, phone, and hospital/discharge portals; the coordinator re-keys patient demographics and clinicals into the agency EMR (WellSky/Kinnser, Allscripts, Epic named), verifies insurance eligibility, obtains and tracks service authorizations, and chases physician orders and supporting documents to complete admission. It is triple bridging: referral source → EMR → payer, under time pressure.
