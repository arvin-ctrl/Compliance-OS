## S12-1: Club-management platform hell — dominant, hated, and holding the clubs' money
- Who hurts: Directors, registrars, and treasurers of competitive youth clubs and leagues (roughly 100–2,000 athletes; $100K–$3M annual revenue), running registration, payments, scheduling, and websites on SportsEngine (NBC Sports Next), TeamSnap, or similar.
- The pain: The two category leaders carry near-bottom satisfaction on every open review channel: TeamSnap sits at 1.1/5 across 692 Trustpilot reviews; SportsEngine sits at 1.11/5 on BBB. Complaints are operational, not cosmetic: registration funds held for weeks ("tying up over $5K... over $20K in accounts receivable... coaches and facilities to pay and are now behind on payments"), support reachable only by email with multi-day waits, bugs open for months, features missing (no desktop schedule creation), and annual charges continuing after cancellation. Clubs are captive because the platform holds their season's registration revenue and their member data.
- Frequency: Continuous — every registration window (2–4 per year), every payment installment (monthly), every schedule change (weekly), every support incident.
- Economics today: SportsEngine's own help doc: management fee = "(net order amount * percentage rate) + (number of transactions * fixed rate)", with example rates of 3.25% + $2.00 per installment — on a $1,500 club fee paid in 7 installments that is ~$62.75 per athlete per season, either absorbed by the club or passed to parents. One BBB complainant reports $5K locked and $20K+ AR stranded. Admin labor to babysit it: youth sport administrator average $20.09/hr (range $14.66–$23.32, ZipRecruiter, May 2026); 871 such jobs listed on Indeed.
- Current solutions: SportsEngine, TeamSnap, LeagueApps, Jersey Watch, Crossbar, PlayMetrics, Bound, GameChanger (DICK'S) for stats/streaming — plus generic form builders, payment links, and spreadsheets to track who paid (sources below describe clubs stitching these together). Reviewers say the incumbents fail on support, bugs, fee stacking, and fund release, not on feature-list breadth.
- Forcing function: Money movement — every season's registration IS a payment event the club cannot skip; platform contract renewal dates. Not a regulatory deadline.
- Why now: Corporate/PE consolidation of the category (SportsEngine under NBC Sports Next; TeamSnap PE-backed; Stack Sports rollup) with visible service decay in 2023–2026 reviews; a 2025 federal bill (see S12-5) explicitly targets private equity's role in youth sports, keeping incumbent behavior in the news; embedded-payments infrastructure (Stripe-class) removes the incumbents' historical processing moat.
- Evidence:
  1. Trustpilot TeamSnap: 1.1/5, 692 reviews. "Coaches can't create schedules from desktop computers...missing so many features" (Todd Harmon, admin, Mar 6, 2026); "Took out an extra $600 + from my credit card in March. Try contacting someone" (Richard Kean, May 25, 2026); "no customer service number to call...took an hour and 15 minutes" (parent, Jul 29, 2024). https://www.trustpilot.com/review/teamsnap.com (2024-2026)
  2. BBB SportsEngine customer reviews: 1.11/5. "We have provided them with all the documents they requested to unlock the processing and release the fund yet they have yet to do so tying up over $5K... over $20K in accounts receivable...coaches and facilities to pay and are now behind on payments" (Derick S., Aug 27, 2024). https://www.bbb.org/us/mn/minneapolis/profile/digital-media/sportsengine-0704-1000003966/customer-reviews (2024-08)
  3. Capterra SportsEngine reviews: "Long time customer- the customer service has major issues... it can take days" (Shawna W., President, Feb 10, 2025, 1/5); "I've had bugs logged for over 4 months that are still not fixed... A scheduling system that doesn't display schedules correctly" (Christopher G., Director of Athletics, Aug 13, 2024, 2/5). https://www.capterra.com/p/134125/SportsEngine/reviews/ (2024-2025)
  4. Software Advice SportsEngine reviews (club administrators): "The registration platform doesn't actually work as their videos claim... ZERO customer service, I have yet to speak to an actual person" (Sara S., Org Administrator, Oct 2021, 1/5). https://www.softwareadvice.com/club-management/sportsengine-profile/reviews/ (2019-2022)
  5. SportsEngine official fee doc: formula and example rates 3.25% + $2.00/installment; org absorbs or passes to parents. https://help.sportsengine.com/en/articles/6310814-what-are-management-fees-and-how-do-they-work (accessed 2026-08-27)
  6. Youth sport administrator pay: average $20.09/hr, most $14.66–$23.32 (ZipRecruiter, May 2026); 871 jobs on Indeed. https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Youth-Sport-Administrator ; https://www.indeed.com/q-youth-sports-administrator-jobs.html (2026-05)
- Scout's confidence: HIGH — two independent review platforms + the vendor's own fee schedule + fresh 2025-2026 complaint dates; the fund-hold complaint is a cash-flow wound, not a preference.

## S12-2: The 10-12% leak — uncollected club dues and installment chasing by volunteer treasurers
- Who hurts: Club directors and volunteer treasurers of travel/club programs (volleyball, soccer, basketball, baseball) charging $1,500–$5,900 per athlete per season on 4–8 installment plans.
- The pain: The Junior Volleyball Association tells its member clubs that 10–12% of revenue simply never arrives — $20K on a $200K club — and its published remedies are manual and medieval: private payment plans, benching kids until paid, work-off arrangements, non-payment registries, and small-claims court that "may not be worth the total cost, time and effort you need to put into collecting." Clubs still take cash, checks, and Zelle to dodge card fees (one club advertises 2.5% off for cash/check), which makes reconciliation and dunning a person's job. Platform-side payment-plan tooling is a known gap reviewers complain about.
- Frequency: Monthly — every installment due date Sept–Mar; escalation events (benching, filings) several times per season.
- Economics today: 10–12% of revenue uncollected (JVA, revised Jul 2026); dues at a sampled club: $3,900–$5,900/athlete with $1,200 deposit + 7 monthly payments and $25 late fees; small-claims filing $90–$100+/case; the human doing the chasing is a $14–$23/hr administrator or an unpaid volunteer (ZipRecruiter, S12-1 E6).
- Current solutions: Platform installment billing (SportsEngine/TeamSnap/LeagueApps) that reviewers describe as inflexible ("SportsEngine should allow partial payments and payment plans as long as all fees are paid by the due date"); spreadsheets + Zelle/checks; benching and contracts as enforcement; JVA's advice sheets. No club-scale AR automation (retry logic, dunning, hardship rescheduling) is named anywhere in the sources.
- Forcing function: Money movement — installments fund coach pay and facility rent in-season; when collection slips, the club misses ITS payables (see S12-1 E2). No regulator.
- Why now: Family cost inflation (+46% since 2019, Aspen) makes delinquency structural, not incidental; embedded-payments rails now support card-account updater, retries, and payment-plan orchestration that the youth-sports incumbents haven't shipped per reviews.
- Evidence:
  1. JVA, "Absorbing the Shock of Uncollected Payments" (revised Jul 6, 2026): "In youth sports, an estimated 10–12% of total revenue goes uncollected"; "$200,000 in gross revenue... approximately $20,000 may never be collected"; recommends a 3–5% uncollected-funds reserve. https://jvavolleyball.org/absorbing-shock-uncollected-payments/ (2026-07)
  2. JVA, "5 Methods for Collecting Unpaid Club Dues" (Sharon Galonski, former club director, Oct 3, 2017): benching ("the player will not be allowed to practice or participate"), non-payment registries, small claims ($90–$100+ filing; judgments public 20 years); "may not be worth the total cost, time and effort you need to put into collecting." https://jvavolleyball.org/5-methods-collecting-unpaid-club-dues/ (2017-10; still the association's published guidance)
  3. Power Volleyball Club dues page (2026-27 season): dues $3,900–$5,900; $1,200 deposit + monthly payments Sept–Mar; "$25.00 late fee for any and all monies collected after 15th of each month"; "Save 2.5% when paying in full by Cash or Check"; Zelle accepted. https://powervolleyballclub.us/club-dues/ (2026)
  4. SportsEngine reviewer (Capterra/GetApp aggregate): "SportsEngine should allow partial payments and payment plans as long as all fees are paid by the due date." https://www.capterra.com/p/134125/SportsEngine/reviews/ (date unknown)
  5. Aspen Institute Project Play survey (Feb 24, 2025): "The average U.S. sports family spent $1,016 on their child's primary sport in 2024, a 46% increase since 2019"; parents spend "more than $40 billion annually." https://projectplay.org/news/2025/2/24/project-play-survey-family-spending-on-youth-sports-rises-46-over-five-years (2025-02)
- Scout's confidence: HIGH — an industry association quantifying its own members' leakage (10-12%) is about as honest as demand evidence gets; club price lists corroborate the manual stack.

## S12-3: Recruiting-services extraction — families pay $1,500-$4,600 for what is largely automatable outreach
- Who hurts: Families of HS athletes (and the club/academy directors they ask first). Secondary: club directors who want to offer credible recruiting support without reselling NCSA-class services.
- The pain: The market leader (NCSA, owned by IMG Academy) sells packages reported at $1,500–$4,200+ as binding contracts "covering the remainder of your athlete's high school career (or up to seven years)" with a ~3-day cancellation window; 1–2 star reviewers in 2026 report automated/canned outreach ("Everything is canned and automated from NCSA"), messages from irrelevant programs, refusal to refund, and $500–$1,500 owed after trying to quit. The underlying workflow families actually need — realistic target list, coach-email outreach, video, eligibility registration — is done manually or bought piecemeal.
- Frequency: Per athlete per recruiting cycle (roughly grades 9–12, intensifying junior/senior year); outreach is weekly during season; deadlines cluster around contact periods and signing days.
- Economics today: NCSA Champion ~$1,386 (18 payments of $77) to MVP+ ~$4,559 (GetRecruited price research from parent accounts); highlight video $150–$400/video from freelance editors or $500–$5,000 full-service; NCSA has 4,473 Trustpilot reviews (scale proxy) and IMG issued a "sets record" press release Dec 4, 2024.
- Current solutions: NCSA, SportsRecruits, FieldLevel (club-resold platforms), Hudl profiles, DIY email + Google Sheets, freelance video editors. Reviewers' stated failure mode: high price, high pressure, canned automation, low personalization — "expensive for absolutely no reason."
- Forcing function: Recruiting-calendar deadlines (NCAA contact periods, signing days) and post-House roster scarcity (S12-4) — real deadlines, but purchase itself is discretionary.
- Why now: House settlement roster limits shrink college roster spots and raise family anxiety (2025-26); NIL noise makes families feel they need professional navigation; the incumbent's own product is described by users as automation sold at concierge prices — a cost-structure gap an AI-heavy team attacks directly.
- Evidence:
  1. Trustpilot NCSA (4,473 reviews, 4.4 overall — 1-star cohort): "NCSA refuses to give a refund" after "4 messages from coaches from middle of nowhere states" (D Long, Jul 6, 2026); "You are locked in for the full year...still leave you on the hook for about $1500.00" (Richard Gonzalez, Jul 6, 2026); "they said we could cancel at any time and then when I went to cancel they said over $500" (Kimberly Kuss, May 1, 2026); "Everything is canned and automated from NCSA" (Chris Gantz, Jul 3, 2026). https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.ncsasports.org (2026)
  2. GetRecruited NCSA cost research (parent accounts + market research): tiers Champion/Elite/MVP/MVP+; "$1,500–$4,200+"; MVP+ ~$4,559; "binding fixed-term contract" up to seven years; "generally 3 days after signing" to cancel. https://getrecruited.cc/how-much-does-ncsa-cost (accessed 2026-08-27)
  3. ComplaintsBoard NCSA page (parent/athlete complaints re: sales pressure and refunds). https://www.complaintsboard.com/national-collegiate-scouting-association-b133921 (ongoing)
  4. Freelance highlight-video rate card: "My professional sports video editing services start at $150 per video"; $150 (10 highlights) to $400 (35 highlights), 48h turnaround, sold direct to families. https://chrisschieman.com/affordable-sports-highlight-video/ (accessed 2026-08-27)

- Scout's confidence: HIGH on pain-and-WTP existence (fresh 2026 reviews with dollar amounts + published price research); MEDIUM on wedge clarity — B2C sale, though club-channel distribution is exactly the founder's asset.

## S12-4: NIL Go / House-settlement compliance chaos — a brand-new mandatory per-deal workflow that is visibly failing
- Who hurts: Athletes and their families/agents submitting NIL deals; collectives; school compliance staff (D-I); downstream, HS/club athletes and academies trying to understand eligibility of money already being offered to 16-year-olds.
- The pain: Since June 11, 2025, every D-I athlete NIL deal over $600 must clear NIL Go (Deloitte-built, College Sports Commission). The system's first year: $90M in deals declined (ESPN, Oct 2025); a $45M reporting error blamed on Deloitte ("We take full responsibility for this reporting error"); The Collective Association reporting that of 384 deals submitted by 25 D-I collectives only 25 were approved, 120 rejected, 192 unaddressed; 2,003 deals worth $35.99M pending at Aug 31, 2025; CSC CEO conceding "I don't think the system was designed with this amount of associated deals in mind" while manual review overwhelms a 15-person staff. A sitting congresswoman demanded answers on denied deals (Oct 10, 2025). Meanwhile roster limits displace athletes with August timing ("extremely difficult for a displaced athlete to find another school").
- Frequency: Per deal (21,000+ deals submitted in year one), per season roster decisions, per transfer window.
- Economics today: $166.5M in deals flowed through in ~8 months; $89.85M+ declined; deal reviews stretch days-to-weeks (50% within 24h, 70% within 7 days — meaning 30% take longer); schools staff compliance manually (CSC itself went 9→15 staff); families hire agents/attorneys at 10-20% of deal value (industry standard, inference — labeled).
- Current solutions: NIL Go itself (mandatory, strained), Teamworks/INFLCR and Opendorse (school/collective side), spreadsheets and outside counsel; for HS/club families: nothing but news articles and paid consultants. Sources describe the system, its backlog, and its data errors — not a served market.
- Forcing function: STRONG — mandatory clearinghouse for payment eligibility; House settlement terms; arbitration process; congressional scrutiny. This is a mandatory computation point on every dollar.
- Why now: The obligation did not exist before June 2025. Rules, thresholds, and enforcement are still moving (2025-2027), which is exactly when tooling and guidance gaps are widest.
- Evidence:
  1. CBS Sports (Mar 10, 2026): Jan–Feb 2026 alone: 3,704 deals/$39.29M cleared, 187 deals/$14.36M rejected; 21,000+ deals/$166.5M since launch; CSC CEO Bryan Seeley: "I don't think the system was designed with this amount of associated deals in mind"; staff grew 9→15; manual review overwhelming automation assumptions. https://www.cbssports.com/college-football/news/college-sports-commissions-nil-clearinghouse-strained/ (2026-03)
  2. Front Office Sports (Sep 5, 2025): cleared-deal data corrected from $79.8M to $35.42M — "clerical error"; Deloitte: "We take full responsibility for this reporting error"; TCA: 384 collective deals → 25 approved / 120 rejected / 192 unaddressed; 2,003 deals worth $35.99M pending. https://frontofficesports.com/nil-go-corrects-its-data-from-80m-in-deals-cleared-to-35m-blames-deloitte/ (2025-09)
  3. ESPN (Oct 2025): NIL clearinghouse "has rejected $90M in deals" — 1,812 deals worth $89.85M declined since June 11, 2025 launch. https://www.espn.com/college-sports/story/_/id/49308194/nil-clearinghouse-rejected-90m-deals (2025-10)
  4. Rep. Lori Trahan letter to the College Sports Commission on denied NIL deals (official congressional letter, Oct 10, 2025). https://trahan.house.gov/uploadedfiles/10.10.2025_trahan_letter_to_csc_on_denied_nil_deals_final.pdf (2025-10)
  5. Sportico (Jan 2026): NCAA eligibility litigation and displaced athletes — "displacement would be especially problematic in August, with the fall semester about to start." https://www.sportico.com/law/analysis/2026/ncaa-demands-stay-displaced-athletes-could-sue-1234940860/ (2026-01)
- Scout's confidence: MEDIUM — the pain and forcing function are the strongest in this ground, but the natural buyers (athletic departments, collectives) are institution-sized; the solo-founder-sellable slice (family/club-level navigation, HS NIL education via academies) is real but less evidenced. Flagging honestly for Manager 1.

## S12-5: Tournament operations — the stay-to-play housing model is under legal fire while scheduling stays manual
- Who hurts: Tournament/showcase directors (thousands of events/year across volleyball, soccer, softball, hockey, cheer) whose P&L depends on hotel-rebate revenue; secondarily the club directors and families forced through the housing funnel.
- The pain: Directors monetize events through mandatory "stay-to-play" hotel blocks with per-room kickbacks ($20/room-night or 30% of agent commission; Varsity made ~$4M/yr this way) — and that model is now the target of a federal class action against Team Travel Source (coercion, junk fees, fake "Lowest Rate Guarantee"), an $82.5M Varsity antitrust settlement, investigative journalism, and a 2025 federal bill that would ban stay-to-play and force PE divestment from youth sports. Meanwhile the operational core — multi-venue schedule builds and mid-event reschedules — is still done in spreadsheets; a scheduling vendor claims directors spend 15–20 hours per event on schedule maintenance alone (vendor claim, labeled), and the incumbent platform sells a human "hire a scheduler" service, which is direct evidence the software doesn't finish the job.
- Frequency: Per event (weekly-to-monthly per director, seasonal peaks); housing compliance per participating team per event.
- Economics today: Buyout fees $500–$2,400 per team just to escape hotel blocks; team entry fees typically hundreds to $1,000+ per event; rebates $20/room-night; 73.5M room-nights / $10.9B lodging spend by U.S. sports travelers (2023); ~40% of tournament destinations required hotel stays in 2023 (down from 60% in 2021 — the model is already retreating under pressure).
- Current solutions: Exposure Events, SportsEngine AES (volleyball), Tourney Machine (Stack Sports), TeamSnap Tournaments for brackets/registration; Team Travel Source, EventConnect, THS for housing; spreadsheets + a paid human scheduler for the hard part. Sources describe housing vendors as coercive and scheduling tools as incomplete.
- Forcing function: Legal — active class action + antitrust exposure + proposed legislation forcing the revenue model to change; per-event dates are hard deadlines. Money movement on every entry fee and room-night.
- Why now: 2025-26 is the exact window in which stay-to-play revenue is being litigated away; directors will need replacement economics (transparent housing, better sponsorship/gate tooling) and lower ops cost. Distribution note: the founder runs events in this world.
- Evidence:
  1. News on 6 / Oklahoma Watch (Apr 30, 2025): "the tournament requires them to book a hotel from a specific list... Families told me they feel stuck... they do it anyway to avoid disqualification" (reporter Jennifer Palmer); buyouts "$500 to $2,000+"; antitrust concerns from bundling. https://www.newson6.com/story/68123675bb145ea102c6b726/why-stay-to-play-hotel-rules-are-costing-youth-sports-families-hundreds (2025-04); underlying investigation: https://oklahomawatch.org/2025/03/13/forced-housing-hidden-kickbacks-how-stay-to-play-squeezes-sports-parents/ (2025-03)
  2. Cheer Daily (Mar 18, 2025; updated Aug 27, 2026): rebates "up to $20 per booked room or 30% of booking agent commissions"; Varsity ~$4M/yr from hotel rebates; Varsity antitrust settlement $82.5M; buyouts up to $2,400/team; ~40% of destinations required stays in 2023; 73.5M room-nights/$10.9B spend. https://cheerdaily.com/industry/how-stay-to-play-policies-squeeze-cheer-families/ (2025-03/2026-08)
  3. Federal class action v. Team Travel Source (Almeida Law Group, co-counsel): "Unlawful coercion to book through TTS, mandatory fees that are not adequately disclosed"; false "Lowest Rate Guarantee". https://ussoccerparent.com/team-travel-source-stay-to-play-lawsuit/ ; https://www.almeidalawgroup.com/investigations/stay-to-play-travel-sports/ (2025)
  4. 2025 federal bill: would require PE firms to divest from youth sports within two years and ban stay-to-play (reported in coverage of the TTS suit). https://www.buyingsandlot.com/p/youth-sports-parents-sue-tournament-housing-firm-over-stay-to-play (2025)
  5. Exposure Events sells "Hire a Tournament or League Scheduler" as a human service on top of its own software. https://exposureevents.com/hire-a-scheduler (accessed 2026-08-27; page intermittently 503 — title and offering confirmed via search index)
  6. Vendor claim (labeled as vendor content, pointer only): directors on manual scheduling spend "15 to 20 hours per event just on schedule maintenance after the initial build." https://www.fastbreak.ai/blog/tournament-directors-switching-spreadsheets-ai-scheduling (2026)
- Scout's confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH — the housing-model disruption is exceptionally well evidenced (lawsuit + settlement + legislation + journalism); director-side workflow pain rests partly on vendor claims and the scheduler-for-hire artifact.

## S12-6: Game film and highlight economics — video is now a four-figure line item per program and a per-family cash cost

On the family side, recruiting highlight reels cost $150–$400 per video from freelancers and $500–$5,000 from full-service shops. The clipping/tagging labor between raw film and a usable reel is bought repeatedly, per team and per athlete.

- Economics today: $1,500–$4,000/program/yr (Hudl list, accessed 2026); $9K–$11.5K AD-wide (coach forum, 2021 — older datum, labeled); $150–$400/video freelance (2026 rate card); $500–$5,000 full-service (2026 market survey by vendor Veo, labeled).

## S12-8: Facility access and rental friction — school gyms priced and gated through a single marketplace
- Who hurts: Club and academy directors who rent school gyms/fields weekly for practices and events; also school-community programs displaced by marketplace-rate pricing.
- The pain: Facilitron now intermediates rentals for 10,000+ U.S. schools (~50% of Florida schools). A PE teacher's public complaint: Facilitron "may have become the monster that has helped to cripple youth sports" — programs that had donated to facilities for years "were treated just like any other 'outside organization'" once the district switched. Before the platform: "double bookings, inaccessible fields, and locked gyms" managed on "literal pen and paper." Now: standardized pricing hierarchies, service fees stacked on rentals, insurance-certificate collection, and district-by-district policies — one district's athletic complex generated $500K+ in rental revenue, which is club money.
- Frequency: Weekly (every practice slot); seasonal permit windows.
- Economics today: District rental revenue of $500K+ at a single complex (Manhattan Beach USD, via Facilities Dive); Facilitron adds "a service fee to cover facility staff wages, utilities and other rental-related expenses" on top of rental rates; clubs' second-largest cost line after coaching is typically facilities (inference from dues structures — labeled).
- Current solutions: Facilitron (dominant school-side), direct district permit offices, private facility leases, church/rec-center gyms, pen-and-paper legacy processes. The marketplace solved the district's problem, not the club's.
- Forcing function: None hard — seasonal permit deadlines; capacity scarcity is the binding constraint, which software alone doesn't create.
- Why now: Facilitron's rapid consolidation (2024-2026) is repricing formerly informal access; complaints are surfacing in trade press.
