# Scout 01 — Orphaned Customers (Incumbent Abandonment)

**How I hunted (5-line summary):** Verified 2024–2026 sunset/repricing events first (official EOL pages, CPA/consultant client alerts), then mined fetchable complaint veins: TaxProTalk, Capterra/GetApp/SoftwareAdvice/Trustpilot reviews, Hacker News (via Algolia API), and The Register. Reddit, G2, TrustRadius, and Ars Technica were hard-blocked, and the program's WebSearch budget hit its 200-call cap mid-hunt, so I pivoted entirely to WebFetch of known URLs.
The richest vein by far: **Intuit's staged execution of QuickBooks Desktop** (hard dates + two 2026 price waves on a stop-sold product) and **PE/acquirer squeezes on professional back-office tools** (Drake Tax post-Cinven, Blackbaud RE7 sunset, Bonterra/Network for Good). Negative findings: rumored Vertafore QQCatalyst, Covetrus ImproMed, and Eyefinity OfficeMate sunsets could NOT be confirmed — excluded. Wild Apricot and Lightspeed/Upserve showed abandonment signals but couldn't clear the 3-artifact bar with Reddit blocked (see ground notes).
All access dates: 2026-08-27. Evidence ledger: `outputs/evidence/s01_orphaned_customers.jsonl`.

## S01-1: QuickBooks Desktop terminal wind-down strands the SMB desktop-accounting base
- **Who hurts:** US SMBs (contractors, wholesalers, light manufacturers, nonprofits — typically 2–50 employees) running QuickBooks Desktop Pro/Premier/Enterprise for job costing, inventory, and payroll; plus the small accounting/bookkeeping firms that support hundreds of these files.
- **The pain:** Intuit stopped selling new Pro/Premier/Mac subscriptions (2024), released no Desktop 2025+, and is killing versions on a schedule: Desktop 2023's payroll, bank feeds, and payments die May 31, 2026; Desktop 2024 — the last version ever — dies September 30, 2027. Meanwhile Intuit raised prices ~10–15% on the stop-sold line effective Feb 1, 2026 (with a second wave reported for Oct 1, 2026) and added per-employee monthly payroll fees to Enterprise. The pushed path, QuickBooks Online, is described by practitioners as buggy and missing Desktop features; forced conversions blow up workflows mid-year.
- **Frequency:** Daily bookkeeping ops; per-payroll-run; annual renewal; hard EOL dates per version-year.
- **Economics today:** Feb 2026 renewals: Pro Plus $999→$1,149/yr; Premier Plus $1,399→$1,609; Enterprise Silver 1-user $1,703→$1,873, 6-user $5,746→$6,608; Basic Payroll $550→$640 + $7/employee/mo; ACH fees doubled ($5→$10 on Advanced) (Brady Martz client alert). Perpetual-license holders already lost support/updates in 2023 (The Register). Migration to QBO/other = consultant projects plus retraining.
- **Current solutions:** Renew Enterprise at ratcheting prices; convert to QBO (practitioners: "incredibly buggy," bank feeds unreliable); hosted-desktop workarounds (gotomyerp/Cloudvara ads target this fear); Sage 50 (same subscription trajectory); TaxProTalk runs live "quickbook alternatives" threads — no consensus refuge for job-costing/inventory shops.
- **Forcing function:** Hard dates: 2026-05-31 (Desktop 2023 services die), 2027-09-30 (Desktop 2024, final version); annual renewal price shocks Feb + Oct 2026.
- **Why now:** The terminal countdown became explicit only in late 2025/2026 (no Desktop 2025 released; Feb 2026 pricing letter). The largest SMB accounting installed base in the US now has a certain end date and no feature-parity destination.
- **Evidence:**
  1. "After May 31, 2026, QuickBooks Desktop 2023 software will be discontinued… won't automatically calculate payroll taxes… You won't be able to download transactions, send online payments" — Intuit service discontinuation policy, https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/help-article/feature-preferences/quickbooks-desktop-service-discontinuation-policy/L17cXxlie_US_en_US (accessed 2026-08-27).
  2. Full Feb 1, 2026 price table (figures above) — Brady Martz CPA client alert, https://www.bradymartz.com/quickbooks-desktop-pricing-update-what-businesses-should-expect-in-2026/ (2026).
  3.   4. - **Scout's confidence:** HIGH — official dates + exact prices + multi-year practitioner revolt across independent sources; enormous installed base with no like-for-like destination.

## S01-2: Blackbaud kills Raiser's Edge "Database View" (summer 2027), stranding nonprofit fundraising power users
- **Who hurts:** Development directors and database managers at small/mid nonprofits (2–200 staff) who run donor operations in Raiser's Edge 7 "Database View" — often decades of gift history, queries, and mail routines.
- **The pain:** Blackbaud announced it will end access to Database View, with support ending summer 2027, forcing everyone onto the RE NXT web view — which even its consultants say "is not a one-to-one replacement for everything teams relied on," so orgs "will need to rethink workflows, reporting, or integrations." Users already describe bouncing between web view and Database View for advanced tasks as "disjointed." Pricing is quote-based and rising; 82% of pricing mentions in Capterra reviews are negative; renewal terms trap orgs ("missed the timeline to unsubscribe… demanded a very expensive fee").
- **Frequency:** Daily gift entry/queries/reporting; year-end giving peak; annual contract renewal; one hard sunset deadline (summer 2027).
- **Economics today:** RE NXT is "a significant investment—especially for smaller nonprofits" (Capterra reviewer, 2025-07); one small org "had to switch companies as soon as our contract ended" purely on price (2026-03); auto-renewal penalty fees reported; migration to any alternative = data conversion + retraining project, typically consultant-assisted (Heller sells exactly this).
- **Current solutions:** Limp on RE7 until 2027; adopt NXT web view + Microsoft Power Platform patches for gaps (per Heller); or defect to Bloomerang/Little Green Light/Virtuous-class CRMs — all requiring full data migration of decades of records.
- **Forcing function:** Hard sunset: Database View access/support ends summer 2027 (announced 2026).
- **Why now:** Announcement landed June 2026; every RE7 org must pick a path in the next ~12 months — a dated, budgeted decision window.
- **Evidence:**
  1. "Blackbaud intends to end access to Database View… [support ends] in summer 2027… It's not a one-to-one replacement for everything teams relied on in Database View—meaning most organizations will need to rethink workflows, reporting, or integrations" — Heller Consulting, https://teamheller.com/resources/blog/raisers-edge-database-view-support-to-end-in-summer-2027 (2026-06-25).
  2. "The price for the product was very expensive so we had to switch companies as soon as our contract ended" — Chase T., President/CEO, 2–10-employee nonprofit, Capterra, https://www.capterra.com/p/247647/Blackbaud-Raiser-s-Edge-NXT/reviews/ (2026-03-24).
  3. "Having to switch back to the old 'Database View' for advanced tasks feels disjointed and makes the learning curve much steeper" — Amy J., Donor Engagement Officer, Capterra (2026-04-06).
  4. Cons ranked from 418 reviews: "High and complex pricing" negative in 82% of 38 mentions; "They insisted we missed the timeline to unsubscribe and demanded a very expensive fee for the following period" — Capterra product page, https://www.capterra.com/p/247647/Blackbaud-Raiser-s-Edge-NXT/ (accessed 2026-08-27).

## S01-3: Drake Tax's PE squeeze breaks the small tax firm's last refuge
- **Who hurts:** Solo and small (1–10 preparer) tax firms — the segment that fled Lacerte/UltraTax pricing to Drake, which was the industry's loved, cheap, well-supported workhorse until Cinven bought it (2021).
- **The pain:** Since "the new owners took over," package prices rose "25% the 1st year, 16% the 2nd, and then 25% again" (TaxProTalk); 2026 top package is $3,145 with another retail hike set for Dec 1, 2026. Support — Drake's historic differentiator — has collapsed in reviews ("worst support team I have ever seen," "efforts to consolidate support have left so many of us on longer phone calls without results"), and users report support being conditioned on adopting the Rightworks hosted cloud. Firms can't switch mid-season, so the ratchet lands every renewal cycle.
- **Frequency:** Annual renewal with early-bird deadlines (e.g., "Renew Drake Tax by May 31, 2025 for our best price of the year"); daily use Jan–Apr; conversion only possible May–Nov.
- **Economics today:** Drake 2026 list: $299.99 (online) to $3,145 (Pro Unlimited multi-user), retail +$100–150 from Dec 1, 2026 (reseller price sheet); a 23%+ single-year increase reported by a 2026 reviewer; alternatives (Lacerte/UltraTax) cost multiples more, per review-site summaries; switching = client-data conversion + retraining in the off-season.
- **Current solutions:** Eat the increases; Intuit Lacerte/ProSeries or Thomson UltraTax (higher-priced, also rising annually per reviews); budget tools (TaxSlayer Pro, MyTAXPrepOffice) viewed as downgrades; conversion friction keeps most captive.
- **Forcing function:** IRS filing season — software must be renewed, installed, and converted before e-file opens each January; renewal-price deadlines each spring/fall.
- **Why now:** The 2025–2026 review cohort shows the loyalty dam breaking ("Drake was sold 3 years ago and it's gotten MUCH WORSE since then," "I will look for a new provider next year") — an installed base actively shopping for the first time in decades.
- **Evidence:**

## S01-4: PCLaw / Time Matters end-of-support strands small law firms on dying on-prem systems
- **Who hurts:** Small law firms (2–50 staff) — an installed base LexisNexis counted at "15,000 paying customers and 130,000 users" (2019) — running PCLaw (billing/trust accounting) or Time Matters (case management) on-prem, with decades of matter and trust data inside.
- **The pain:** The products barely evolve (Time Matters' last major release: 16.4, January 2019). Owner PCLaw|Time Matters LLC (LEAP joint venture) set end-of-version support at December 31, 2025, warning unsupported firms face "vulnerability to data corruption, broken links, and integration issues" — for software running their trust accounting. Reviews record abandonment ("no longer offers IT Support… it has now been replaced with cloud based software" — April 2025) and the overall rating has collapsed to 2.8/5. The exit path is a forced march to cloud suites (LEAP ~$165/user/mo), and data lock-in bites on the way out — one reviewer reports a $4,000 charge to export data.
- **Frequency:** Daily time/billing/trust operations; annual support renewal; EOS deadline passed Dec 31, 2025 — laggards are unsupported *now*.
- **Economics today:** LEAP cited at ~$165/user/mo (solo reviewer, Lawyerist); "$4,000" data-export charge reported; unsupported trust-accounting = malpractice/compliance exposure; CARET Legal (the other consolidator's destination) is quote-only enterprise pricing.
- **Current solutions:** Limp on unsupported on-prem versions; migrate to LEAP or CARET Legal (both owned by the consolidators driving the sunsets); or convert to Clio/MyCase/Smokeball — all requiring paid data conversion of billing + trust ledgers.
- **Forcing function:** Dec 31, 2025 end of version support (passed — stranded base is live); OS/Office compatibility drift compounds annually.
- **Why now:** The support cliff just hit; the base is deciding in 2026 under pressure, and both "official" destinations are priced as full cloud suites with data-export friction.
- **Evidence:**
  1. "Support ends on December 31, 2025" for PCLaw and Time Matters versions; unsupported firms risk "data corruption, broken links, and integration issues" — official End of Version Support FAQ, https://community.pclawtimematters.com/pclawanswers/s/article/End-of-Version-Support-FAQ (accessed 2026-08-27; page is JS-walled — statements captured via search index).
  2. "no longer offers IT Support… it has now been replaced with cloud based software" — Holly P., 2–10-employee firm, Software Advice PCLaw profile (overall 2.8/5, 80 reviews), https://www.softwareadvice.com/legal/pclaw-profile/ (2025-04).
  3. LEAP positioned "for firms looking to make the switch from PC Law or Time Matters"; solo cost "$165/month"; negative review reports "$4,000" charged for a data export — Lawyerist LEAP review, https://lawyerist.com/reviews/law-practice-management-software/leap/ (accessed 2026-08-27).
  4. Last major release "Time Matters 16.4 released on January 30, 2019"; 2019 LEAP JV; "15,000 paying customers and 130,000 users" — Wikipedia: Time Matters, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Matters (accessed 2026-08-27).
- **Scout's confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH — EOL fact, stagnation, rating collapse, and lock-in economics all sourced; volume of verbatim firm-level complaints is thinner than other candidates because legal forums (Reddit, listservs) were unreachable.

## S01-6: The Bench collapse — 35,000 SMBs learned their books can vanish overnight
- **Who hurts:** US small-business owners (e-commerce, agencies, contractors) who outsourced bookkeeping to tech-enabled services; acutely, the 35,000+ Bench customers locked out Dec 27, 2024 — days before tax season.
- **The pain:** Bench shut off platform access with same-day notice: "as of December 27, 2024, the Bench platform will no longer be accessible." Customers were told to file six-month IRS extensions and grab data by Dec 30. Verbatim: "they have not done my books since July and now this… An no refunds?!"; "they had hidden my Gusto payroll line items from view… This led to a $300k shortfall from where I thought I was vs reality"; one buyer financed the annual fee via Capchase and "still owe[s]…the full amount for a year, even if Bench.co shuts down." The rescue acquisition (Employer.com, Dec 30) was undisclosed-price and staffing-shaky before tax season. The durable pain: SMB books held in proprietary, non-portable ledgers by services that can die.
- **Frequency:** Monthly close + annual tax filing; the lock-in risk is continuous.
- **Economics today:** Bench charged ~$3k+/yr prepaid (no refunds per customers); emergency re-bookkeeping and extension filings; a $300k cash-visibility error reported; Bench had raised $113M before failing — the model's unit economics broke, not the demand.
- **Current solutions:** Local bookkeepers/CPAs (capacity-constrained), QuickBooks Live, Pilot, Kick (Bench's own recommended successor), or DIY QBO — per the shutdown notice and HN thread discussions of where to flee.
- **Forcing function:** Tax deadlines made the 2024 event acute; today: none hard — the residual opportunity (portable, continuity-safe bookkeeping) is discretionary. (Honest.)
- **Why now:** Post-2024, buyers explicitly distrust black-box bookkeeping; AI-agent bookkeeping entrants are re-running the Bench playbook, and the demand it proved (35k customers paying for done-for-you books) still exists with no continuity guarantees.
- **Evidence:**
  1. "Bench touted having more than 35,000 U.S. customers just hours before it was shut down… customers should file a six-month extension with the IRS" — TechCrunch, https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/27/bench-shuts-down-leaving-thousands-of-businesses-without-access-to-accounting-and-tax-docs/ (2024-12-27).
  2. "I have an annual account and they have not done my books since July and now this… An no refunds?!" (lindsi); "hidden my Gusto payroll line items… $300k shortfall" (pplante); Capchase still owed post-shutdown (solidgiant) — HN thread, 304 comments, https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42523061 (2024-12-27).
  3. Employer.com acquisition; continuity risk with reduced staff before tax season — TechCrunch, https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/30/bench-to-be-acquired-after-abruptly-shutting-down/ (2024-12-30).
- **Scout's confidence:** MEDIUM — event and pain are impeccably evidenced, but it's a single (huge) event now 20 months old; the live 2026 pain is second-order (distrust + portability gap), which needs Wave-2 validation.

## S01-7: Broadcom's VMware repricing — the decade's biggest "repriced into revolt" (founder-fit caveat)
- **Who hurts:** IT owners at organizations of every size on VMware virtualization; the small end ("forcible eviction for the little") — SMBs and the MSPs/resellers serving them — is the segment relevant to this program.
- **The pain:** Post-acquisition, Broadcom killed perpetual licenses, forced subscription bundles, and repriced brutally: AT&T's filing quotes "+1,050 percent in one year"; Computershare (24,000 VMs) reports pricing "hiked by a factor of ten or more"; a CIO reported an $8M renewal quoted at $100M; distribution was yanked (Ingram dropped, partner margins eliminated, small partners/customers pushed out). Revolt is still escalating in 2026 (Tesco moving 40,000 workloads off VMware "amid Broadcom's abusive conduct," June 2026). Small shops face renewal quotes multiplying while their migration options (Proxmox/Hyper-V/Nutanix) require skills and projects they don't have.
- **Frequency:** Per renewal cycle (1–3 yrs) + audit/true-up events; migration is a one-time project per org.
- **Economics today:** 3–10x renewal multiples (sources above); AT&T pegged migration at "$40–50M" with "very quick payback" — at SMB scale that's consultant/MSP projects (thousands to tens of thousands of dollars) vs. multiplied renewals.
- **Current solutions:** Pay up; or migrate to Proxmox, XCP-ng, Hyper-V, Nutanix via MSPs and consultants — a services market today, with tooling gaps around inventory/assessment/migration for the small end.
- **Forcing function:** Renewal dates and support terminations on perpetual licenses — real, per-customer deadlines.
- **Why now:** The eviction of the small end (partners + sub-1,000-VM customers) concentrated in 2024–2026 and is ongoing.
- **Evidence:**
  1. "This proposed annual increase of +1,050 percent in one year is extreme…"; migration "$40-$50M…very quick payback and strong IRR" — AT&T filings, The Register, https://www.theregister.com/2024/10/01/att_broadcom_filings_update/ (2024-10-01).
  2. Computershare's 24,000-VM migration after pricing "hiked by a factor of ten or more"; Broadcom strategy = "price hikes for the big and forcible eviction for the little" — The Register, https://www.theregister.com/2024/06/03/broadcoms_vmware_strategy_looks_ever/ (2024-06-03).
  3. Ongoing 2026 revolt: "Tesco moving 40k server workloads off VMware amid Broadcom's abusive conduct" (409 points/280 comments, 2026-06-17); "$8M renewal to $100M" (2024-02); "Broadcom VMware dropped by Ingram and Partner margin eliminated" (2024-12) — Hacker News index via Algolia, https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48576838 (accessed 2026-08-27).
- **Scout's confidence:** MEDIUM — pain evidence is HIGH-grade, but founder-fit is questionable: the buyer's need is a migration *project* (services-heavy, infra-touching), not obviously a solo-founder SaaS wedge. Flagging per the anti-pattern rules; Manager 1 should weigh killing or reframing (e.g., MSP-facing tooling).
