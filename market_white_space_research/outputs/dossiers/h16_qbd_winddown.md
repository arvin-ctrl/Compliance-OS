## 1. The pain, restated precisely

**Who hurts.**
1. **US SMBs (2–50 employees) with Desktop-specific workflows** — contractors/trades running job costing, WIP/percent-complete and estimates-vs-actuals; light manufacturers and wholesalers on assemblies, sales orders, units of measure, price levels; nonprofits running class/job/item gymnastics for functional-expense reporting. Base size: "over one million active Desktop users" (Fourlane, 2026) [H16-E14]; ~3.7M combined Pro/Premier/Enterprise users per a 2026 integration-vendor analysis [H16-E15]. Both are secondary estimates; order of magnitude is safe.
2. **The accounting/bookkeeping firms that hold those files** — one firm supports dozens to hundreds of Desktop client books, is itself being repriced (Accountant edition $2,249/yr, ProAdvisor bundles +25%, Enhanced Payroll for Accountants $3,780/yr from Oct 2026 [H16-E6]), and owns the migration labor: a ProAdvisor migration runs **20–40 billable hours per file** [H16-E13].

**The workflow failure.** Intuit executed a staged terminal wind-down: stop-sell of new Pro/Premier/Mac subscriptions Sept 30, 2024 (Enterprise exempt) [H16-E2]; Desktop 2023 services (payroll calculation and forms, bank feeds, online payments, live support) died May 31, 2026, including Enterprise 23.0 [H16-E1]; two price waves in 12 months on the stop-sold line (Feb 2026: Pro $999→$1,149 … [H16-E27]; Oct 2026: Pro $1,149→$1,351, Accountant $1,799→$2,249 [H16-E6, E8]). The pushed exit, QBO, drops exactly the workflows these segments live in — Intuit's own transfer guide lists sales orders, progress-invoicing links, price levels, units of measure, job estimates-vs-actuals, past reconciliation reports, memorized reports, and the audit trail as not moving [H16-E10] — and QBO itself was repriced +41–70% effective Aug 1, 2026 (Plus $99→$140/mo, Advanced $200→$340/mo) [H16-E9]. Firms doing the moves get free-but-lossy Intuit tooling: the Accountant Batch Migration Tool caps at 50 files, excludes Enterprise files, and breaks payroll data [H16-E11, E12]. And leaving is punished: a lapsed Desktop subscription gives ~1 year of view-only access, then locks the business out of its own history — a nonprofit that migrated posted that its old license "won't activate… they just need it for read-only access" [H16-E18, E26].

**Frequency.** Per-firm: continuous through the 2026–2027 window (each client renewal date is a mini-deadline; Oct 1, 2026 and the 2027 services cliff are the big ones). Per-business: an episodic but unavoidable event with a dated countdown, then 7 years of record-retention tail.

**Strongest three artifacts.** (1) Intuit's discontinuation-policy page, fetched and verified, for the executed May-31-2026 kill [H16-E1]; (2) the r/QuickBooks accountant thread (2026-07-14, 31 pts/60 comments) posting Intuit's Oct-2026 renewal tables verbatim — "$4,399!!!! For ONE lousy year!" — with commenters cancelling after 10 years and improvising Excel+Claude escapes [H16-E6, E7]; (3) Intuit's own "what to expect when you switch" loss list [H16-E10] — the incumbent documents the gap this wedge monetizes.

## 4. The wedge

**"QBD Exit Factory" — an agent-powered migration line for accounting firms (direct-to-SMB flat-price as secondary motion).** Per client file, at a flat per-file price:

1. **Extract**: full-fidelity export of the Desktop file via the still-supported qbXML/SDK path on a live install (firm's Accountant copy or our hosted runner) — lists, transactions, payroll YTD, memorized/custom reports inventory, audit trail, attachments [H16-E15].
2. **Triage report**: per-file migration-readiness scorecard — what moves clean, what Intuit's tool would drop (mapped against the official loss list [H16-E10]), destination recommendation (QBO tier / Enterprise / Sage 50 / vertical stack / archive-only) with cost deltas.
3. **Agent migration + cleanup**: automated load to the chosen destination with the drop-list handled by playbook (sales orders → documented workaround objects; price levels → price rules; recurring/memorized transactions rebuilt; suspense-account and payroll-YTD repairs that the free tool causes [H16-E13]).
4. **Verification diff pack**: old-vs-new trial balance, BS, P&L, AR/AP aging, payroll YTD — machine-diffed, human-reviewable, signed off before cutover ("books tie out or you don't pay").
5. **Permanent read-only archive**: searchable web archive of the complete Desktop history (ledger, reports, reconciliation history, audit trail, attachments) — the anti-hostage artifact, priced as a small subscription; kills the $1,351/yr zombie-license and the 1-year view-only trap [H16-E26, E18].
6. **Firm dashboard**: batch pipeline across the client book (no 50-file cap, Enterprise files included), status per client, white-label deliverables.

**Explicitly does NOT do:** build a new accounting engine; process payroll; ongoing bookkeeping; tax filings (no regulated-practice surface — the firm remains the professional of record and approves cleanup decisions).

**≤90 days, founder + agents? Yes, scoped.** The extraction path is documented commodity tech (qbXML/Web Connector; not deprecated [H16-E15]); QBO has import APIs; diff reports are deterministic computation; the archive v1 is rendered reports + searchable transaction ledger, not a Desktop UI clone. Hard parts — payroll-YTD reconstruction and edge-case entities — are bounded by the triage step (v1 refuses what it can't verify and routes it to the firm at a services price). v1 target: Pro/Premier files, QBO + archive-only destinations; Enterprise files and Sage destination in v2.

## 7. AI-structural advantage

The product line collapses a published labor quantity — 20–40 ProAdvisor hours per file [H16-E13] — into agent runtime plus a deterministic verification harness. Incumbent economics resist copying: Fourlane-class consultancies bill those hours (automation cannibalizes revenue); Intuit's free tooling is a QBO-acquisition funnel, so fidelity work that eases exits to Sage/vertical stacks or archive-only is against its interest, and its own docs freeze the drop-list [H16-E10]. The DIY-Claude improvisations in the threads [H16-E7] show the tasks are agent-tractable; what they lack — multi-client scale, playbook memory, verification discipline, an archive artifact — is exactly the software.

## 8. Moat path

Accumulates with usage: (1) a **loss-point playbook library** (per Desktop version/edition/vertical: what breaks, the repair recipe, expected diff signatures) that compounds per file processed; (2) the **verification corpus** (thousands of before/after diffs = QA data no entrant has); (3) **archive lock-in** — 7-year retention turns one-shot migrations into a subscription base and makes us the system of record for the old books; (4) **firm-channel trust** (white-label deliverables embed in their engagement letters). Honest thin-wrapper assessment: the migration step alone could be commoditized by better Intuit tooling or generic agents; the durable assets are the verification harness, the playbook data, and the archive — the wedge must ship all three, not just the file move.

## 9. Risks & unknowns

1. **Intuit closes the gap** (ships fidelity-grade free migration, or extends Desktop 2024 indefinitely, converting the cliff into a plateau). *Test:* monitor R-release notes + migration-tool changelog monthly; kill trigger = sales-orders/price-levels/payroll-YTD preserved by the free tool. Current evidence: 3 years of stable drop-list [H16-E10]; incentives point to QBO price-mix, not exit fidelity [H16-E9].
2. **The 2027 date slips or splinters** (policy change, region differences), deflating urgency. *Test:* the Feb/Oct 2026 waves already force decisions independent of 2027 [H16-E6]; track Intuit's next discontinuation-policy update (due ~Jan 2027) — a slip delays, not kills; archive/hostage pain persists regardless.
3. **WTP undershoots** ($499/file rejected because "Intuit does it free"). *Test:* 10 firm interviews + a paid pilot (3 firms / 25 files in 30 days at $499); kill trigger = <$150/file clearing price. Mitigant already evidenced: firms bill 20–40 hours for the "free" path's aftermath [H16-E13].
4. **Technical fidelity wall** (payroll YTD/attachments/audit trail extraction breaks on real-world files; SDK access requires live licenses and Windows runners). *Test:* 90-day build gate = 20 donated real files end-to-end with TB/BS/P&L diffs at 100% tie-out and payroll YTD within $0.01; measure human-minutes-per-file (must be <60). Platform note: extraction rides the customer's own licensed install and their own data; SDK is alive and not deprecated [H16-E15] — not a single-API hostage, but flag it.
5. **Window economics** (the cohort finishes moving ~2028 and revenue decays). *Test:* archive-subscription attach rate ≥50% in pilot (annuity), and validate the platform expansion — the same factory retargets the sunset conveyor Wave 1 documented (PCLaw done 2025, RE7 2027 [H15], Sage 50's own cloud push). Kill trigger for the *company* (not the wedge): attach <20% and no second sunset vertical validated by month 9.
