# 9. First-20-Customer Acquisition Plan

**Target:** freight brokerages and 3PLs, **$50M–$500M gross revenue**. Buyer: **CFO / Controller / VP Finance**. Product: the Rebill Report (see MVP).

**Universe: 28,351 registered US property brokers.** The $50M+ tier is a few thousand at most — small enough to name, large enough to sustain a solo founder.

---

## The core mechanic: the retro run

Everything below is a way to get one file.

> *"Send me a 12-month CSV export from your TMS. I'll tell you within a week how much you paid carriers in accessorials and never billed your customers. If the number is small, I'll tell you that and go away."*

This works for four reasons the research established:
- **It is one export, not an integration.** No IT ticket, no security review, no procurement.
- **It answers the only objection that matters** — *"prove it before I pay"* — and every vendor in this market fails it.
- **It produces a deadline.** Findings arrive with days-remaining-in-dispute-window attached, which converts a "nice report" into a meeting.
- **It is your measurement instrument.** The single unmeasured variable in this thesis is how much broker leakage actually exists. **The first ten retro runs are the research, and you get paid in access for doing them.**

---

## Phase 1 — Customers 1–3 (weeks 1–8): buy the truth

**Goal: not revenue. A measured leakage rate.**

Target three brokers you can reach through a warm introduction — an ex-colleague, an investor's portfolio, a LinkedIn second-degree at a $50–200M shop.

**The ask:** a free retro run in exchange for (a) the anonymised leakage percentage as a publishable benchmark, and (b) a reference call if the number is good.

**What you are buying:** the number that decides whether this is a company. If measured leakage is **>0.3% of gross revenue**, $50M brokers are viable. **Below 0.05%, only ~200 companies in the country clear — and the honest move is to stop.**

**Success criterion for Phase 1 is not a signed customer. It is knowing whether to continue.**

---

## Phase 2 — Customers 4–10 (months 3–5): the benchmark becomes the pitch

Once you have three measured data points, you own something nobody else in this market has: **a real number for broker accessorial leakage.** Every existing figure in the space traces to vendor marketing.

**Channel A — the benchmark as content.** Publish it. *"We reconciled $340M of brokered freight across three brokerages. 0.42% of gross revenue was paid to carriers and never billed to customers — a median of $71,000 per $50M of revenue, and 61% of it was still inside the customer's dispute window."*

That is a genuinely new number in an industry where FreightWaves reports **only 2% of brokerages have fully automated AR** and RXO's brokerage gross margin fell **13.3% → 11.4%** year over year. It gets picked up because it is data, not opinion.

**Channel B — direct outreach to a named list.** Build it from TIA membership, FreightWaves coverage, load-board presence, and FMCSA's broker registry. Target the CFO/Controller directly — **not the VP of Ops**, who owns loads but not the P&L bridge.

The email is three sentences: the benchmark number, the offer of a free retro run, and one specific reason you picked them.

**Channel C — the margin-compression moment.** Public filings tell you exactly when to call. RXO down 190bps. CHRW holding margin only by cutting headcount 7.1%. **Hub Group's Item 4.02 non-reliance in May 2026 restating two years for *"transactions that were prematurely or incorrectly recognized or not adequately supported."*** When a public comparable reports margin compression, every private broker's CFO is being asked the same question that week.

**Convert 7 of ~40 retro runs. Pure contingency, 25% of collected, no floor.**

---

## Phase 3 — Customers 11–20 (months 6–10): leverage and channel

**Channel D — factoring partnerships (the highest-leverage move, and the most patient).**

Factors hold rate con + invoice + POD on every load and have an existing billing relationship. The pitch: *"recovery engine behind your brand"* — incremental factorable AR at full margin, plus differentiation in a market where rate is the only competitive lever (RTS at 1.5% vs OTR at 2.5% is the entire sales pitch today).

**Target mid-tier and regional factors, not the top five.** They need differentiation and can decide fast.

**Do not pursue Triumph.** TriumphPay Audit is funded by brokers — 41 of the top 100 — and a product that increases what those brokers owe is adversarial to its own customer base. **Triumph is the incumbent you are structurally advantaged against, not a partner.**

Expect the first objection to be **broker-relationship risk**: factors depend on broker goodwill for collections. Answer it directly — this product bills the broker's *customer*, not the broker.

**Channel E — TMS certification.** McLeod runs a 260-partner certified program and is aggressively blessing third-party AI (Augment, Chain, CloneOps) rather than building. **Get certified early. The genuine threat is that the program blesses a different startup as the accessorial partner.**

**Channel F — customer referrals.** By customer 10 you have recovered dollars with names attached. In a market where **nobody publishes a dispute win rate**, a peer saying "they found us $180K" is the strongest asset available.

**Channel G — the practitioner communities.** FreightWaves comment threads, TIA events, LinkedIn freight-finance circles, r/FreightBrokers. **Flagged honestly: this research could not retrieve Reddit (domain-blocked, HTTP 429) or LinkedIn (403), so this channel is unvalidated and needs a human pass before you rely on it.**

## What to say when they push back

The five objections, in the order the research says you will hear them:

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| *"My customer won't pay a 90-day-old rebill."* | **Correct — which is why this runs weekly, not annually.** Findings arrive with days-remaining attached. Arrive's own agreement gives 180 days from the original invoice for an undercharge claim; we work inside it. |
| *"I chose not to bill that."* | **Absorption is a legitimate commercial decision, and Truckstop teaches it.** Mark the account, lane or customer as absorb-by-policy and it never appears again. Without that switch this product is noise. |
| *"My TMS already does this."* | **It audits accounts payable — did the carrier overbill you. This audits accounts receivable.** Opposite directions. Send the export and I'll show you on your own data in a week. |
| *"I'm not giving you my rate data."* | Read-only export. No carrier-rate redistribution. Contractual non-aggregation. Delete on request. **Margin data is the most sensitive file you own and the contract will say so.** |
| *"Prove it before I pay."* | **You pay 25% of what we collect. If we collect nothing, you pay nothing.** |

## Milestones and the honest kill points

| Milestone | Target | Kill trigger |
|---|---|---|
| 3 retro runs measured | Week 8 | **Median leakage < 0.05% of gross revenue → stop** |
| Benchmark published | Month 4 | — |
| 10 paying customers | Month 5 | **Collection rate on rebills < 40% → the uplift assumption is wrong; reprice or stop** |
| 20 paying customers | Month 10 | **CAC > 6 months of contingency revenue → the direct motion doesn't work; go channel-only** |
| 1 factoring or TMS channel signed | Month 12 | — |

**At 20 customers averaging $45K of recovery each at 25%, that is ~$225K of annual contingency revenue** — enough for one person, not yet a company. The Phase 3 pricing conversion (see pricing hypothesis) is what turns it into one.

## What NOT to do

- **Do not sell to owner-operators or sub-25-truck carriers.** ROI Model A caps ACV at ~$9,800 and the floor is anchored by a $12.99/mo app and a $299 Google Sheet.
- **Do not sell to enterprise shippers.** 9–15 months, 6–17 stakeholders, SOC 2 Type II before signature — a solo founder burns their runway inside one cycle.
- **Do not lead with detention.** Detention is the most crowded, most price-anchored word in this market, and the claim is usually already dead on arrival. **Lead with margin.**
- **Do not build an integration for the first 20 customers.** Every one of them can export a CSV, and the moment you promise an integration you have inherited a roadmap instead of a business.
